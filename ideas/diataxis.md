# Diátaxis

## What it is

- **Format:** a documentation framework rather than a tool, separating tutorials, how-to guides, reference and explanation along learning/action and acquisition/application axes ([Diátaxis map, full page read, accessed 2026-11-26](https://diataxis.fr/map)).
- **Licence and scope:** a public set of writing principles covering page purpose and tutorial design; no licence statement was checked. The tutorial page frames a tutorial as a lesson with an absent tutor ([Diátaxis tutorials, full page read, accessed 2026-11-26](https://diataxis.fr/tutorials)).
- **Evidence:** tutorials, map, start-here and tutorials-versus-how-to pages were read directly. Verdicts are research inferences, not approved work.

## Ideas worth taking

- **adopt**: State the destination rather than the topic: Diátaxis asks writers to say what the learner will achieve, not that they will "learn" something ([Diátaxis tutorials](https://diataxis.fr/tutorials)).
  Tam application: path headers should carry an outcome line, with prerequisites and time alongside it.
- **adopt**: Every step should produce a comprehensible result early and often ([Diátaxis tutorials](https://diataxis.fr/tutorials)).
  Tam application: one observable result per checkpoint, rather than several commands between verifications.
- **adopt**: Maintain a narrative of the expected by showing exact output, flagging likely failures and pointing out what to notice ([Diátaxis tutorials](https://diataxis.fr/tutorials)).
  Tam application: store expected output or a matcher per checkpoint instead of only a title.
- **adopt**: Ruthlessly minimise explanation, focus on the concrete and ignore alternatives, linking to explanation instead ([Diátaxis tutorials](https://diataxis.fr/tutorials)).
  Tam application: keep path steps action-only and link theory to glossary or teaching pages.
- **adopt**: Aspire to perfect reliability because flaws cascade through a tutorial's story ([Diátaxis tutorials](https://diataxis.fr/tutorials)).
  Tam application: walk each path end-to-end in corpus tests before accepting it.
- **adopt**: Encourage repetition and use a closing acknowledgement of what the learner built ([Diátaxis tutorials](https://diataxis.fr/tutorials)).
  Tam application: resume and repeat checkpoints, and end with a concrete completion statement.
- **consider**: The four-way map separates learning, goals, information and understanding, while acknowledging that users may enter at any point ([Diátaxis map](https://diataxis.fr/map); [Start here, full page read, accessed 2026-11-26](https://diataxis.fr/start-here)).
  Tam application: keep paths, how-tos, glossary/reference and explanatory pages distinct but cross-linked.
- **consider**: Tutorials and how-to guides collapse into each other when their distinctions blur ([Tutorials and how-to guides, full page read, accessed 2026-11-26](https://diataxis.fr/tutorials-how-to); [How-to guides, full page read, accessed 2026-11-26](https://diataxis.fr/how-to-guides)).
  Tam application: classify a page by learner intent before choosing its section of the corpus.
- **reject**: Level tiers, quizzes and progress state as Diátaxis features: its pages define none of them ([gap, accessed 2026-11-26](https://diataxis.fr/tutorials)).
  Tam application: take those from other sources or design them locally.
- **reject**: Turning every teaching page into a mini-tutorial with step-by-step directions ([Diátaxis map](https://diataxis.fr/map)).
  Tam application: preserve separate page types and link between them.

## Things to avoid

- Do not make a tutorial explain the subject: Diátaxis says to link outward instead ([Diátaxis tutorials](https://diataxis.fr/tutorials)).
- Do not show every flag or alternative in a path step ([Diátaxis tutorials](https://diataxis.fr/tutorials)).
- Do not claim measured learning outcomes: the source offers pedagogical prescriptions, not experimental results ([gap](https://diataxis.fr/tutorials)).
- Do not force cliffhangers or entertainment into a terse CLI reference tool ([Ubuntu tutorials guidelines](https://ubuntu.com/tutorials/guidelines)).
