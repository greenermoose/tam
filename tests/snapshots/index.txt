Welcome to the Tamlinux guide

This is the offline guide for your Tamlinux workstation. It lives on disk, needs
no network, and works before the graphical desktop is running. Read it here in
the terminal, or pipe it into other tools.

Start here

- Welcome and orientation — what Tamlinux is, and how to find your way around.
- Installing and maintaining — how the system is put together and kept
  healthy.

Reading paths

- The beginner's path — new to Linux or the terminal.
- The intermediate path — comfortable at the shell, ready to see how the
  system is built.
- The expert's path — the design decisions and internals.

Using the system

- The shell and streams — Unix philosophy, standard streams, and redirection.
- Shell pipelines — connecting small commands into bigger ideas.
- Processes, jobs, and signals — job control, background execution, and
  signals.
- Filesystem hierarchy — standard directories and storage layout.
- File permissions and ownership — access modes, ownership, and links.

Desktop and workflow

- The Tamlinux desktop — tiling Wayland environment and essential shortcuts.
- Workspaces and multi-monitor — virtual workspaces and synchronized display
  sets.

System health and performance

- Resources and performance — memory (free vs available), ZRAM swap, and CPU
  load.
- Reading man pages — the traditional manual, without the fear.

Why Tamlinux is built this way

- The Tamlinux ecosystem — the software Tamlinux is built from, the
  alternatives considered, and why.
- Carried patches — how fixes to third-party packages are carried and retired.

Using this guide

In the pager, use j and k (or the up and down arrow keys) to scroll the screen a
line at a time, Space or PgDn and b or PgUp to scroll a whole screen, g or Home
and G or End to jump to the top or bottom, Tab or Shift-Tab to select the next
or previous link, Enter or the right arrow to follow it, u or the left arrow to
go back to the previous page, and q or Esc to quit. When the whole page already
fits on the screen, the scrolling keys do nothing.

Every page below this index begins with a breadcrumb trail showing where it sits
in the guide, from this index down to the page itself. The trail's earlier parts
are links, so you can jump back up to a section or to this index.

When you redirect the output, for example tam show | head, the guide is printed
as plain text with a numbered list of links.

Most pages also carry a beginner, intermediate, or expert depth. Read a page at
one depth with tam show shell/pipelines?level=expert, or list the topics
offering a depth with tam explain --level expert.
