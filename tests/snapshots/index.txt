The Tamlinux User Guide

This is the authoritative offline user guide for your Tamlinux workstation. It
lives on disk, needs no network, and works before the graphical desktop is
running. Read it here in the terminal, or pipe it into other tools.

- Welcome and orientation — what Tamlinux is, and how to find your way around.
- The three paths: using, maintaining, and improving your workstation.
- Installing — how the system is inspected, installed, and kept healthy.

Part II: Guided Reading Paths

- The beginner's path — new to Linux or the terminal.
- The intermediate path — comfortable at the shell, ready to see how the
  system is built.
- The expert's path — the design decisions and internals.

Part IV: Maintaining Your Workstation

- Maintaining your workstation — overview of maintenance pillars, tam inspect
  host audits, and tam check diagnostics.
- Non-disruptive updates — inode unlinking (why Linux updates in place without
  rebooting) and 3-tier package model.
- Snapshots and rollbacks — Btrfs Copy-on-Write snapshots, storage checks (
  tam check storage_btrfs), and generation rollbacks.
- System diagnostics and journals — filtering journalctl streams, dmesg ring
  buffer, and log integrity.
- The progressive power ladder — DPMS monitor blanking, RAM suspend, zero-watt
  hibernation, and Shift wake.
- Emergency disaster recovery — text virtual consoles, Magic SysRq REISUB
  sequence, and live USB repair.
- Workstation troubleshooting runbook — solutions for audio, bluetooth, and
  display glitches (planned).
- Frequently asked questions — common workstation questions and answers
  (planned).

Part V: Improving Your Workstation: Coding, Hacking, and AI Agents

- Extending and improving Tamlinux — overview of efficiency, toolchains,
  scripting, and local AI.
- Zero-polling architecture — eliminating periodic polling loops with inotify,
  epoll, and Wayland event sockets.
- Memory compression with ZRAM — doubling effective RAM capacity with
  near-zero latency LZ4/zstd compressed swap.
- Terminal and editor ergonomics — fast shell startup, micro editor
  ergonomics, and live disk reload (Virtue V11).
- Writing tools and extending features — scripting conventions ( tam-* vs
  fred-*) and safe Bash headers.
- Native toolchains and compilation — C toolchains, libtam linking, and
  isolated Git worktrees.
- The tam command — C architecture, compilation, Makefile targets, library
  linking, and release lifecycle.
- Physics of local AI generation — memory bandwidth bounds on autoregressive
  token rate, quantization, and local models.
- Coding with AI agents on Tamlinux — pair programming, isolated worktrees,
  scope claims ( tam-work), and test automation (planned).
- Authoring desktop plugins — Quickshell plugin architecture, QML panels, and
  tam-plugin distribution (planned).
- Adding and packaging software — Nix packaging, Home Manager modules, and
  native distro packages (planned).

Using this guide

In the pager, use j and k (or the up and down arrow keys) to scroll the screen a
line at a time, Space or PgDn and b or PgUp to scroll a whole screen, g or Home
and G or End to jump to the top or bottom, Tab or Shift-Tab to select the next
or previous link, Enter or the right arrow to follow it, u or the left arrow to
go back to the previous page, ` (backtick) to return to this index, and q or Esc
to quit. When the whole page already fits on the screen, the scrolling keys do
nothing.

Every page below this index begins with a breadcrumb trail showing where it sits
in the guide, from this index down to the page itself. The trail's earlier parts
are links, so you can jump back up to a section or to this index.

When you redirect the output, for example tam show | head, the guide is printed
as plain text with a numbered list of links.

Most pages also carry a beginner, intermediate, or expert depth. Read a page at
one depth with tam show shell/pipelines?level=expert, or list the topics
offering a depth with tam explain --level expert.
