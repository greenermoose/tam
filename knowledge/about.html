About Tamlinux and the Tam Project

Tamlinux is workstation software for Unix-like operating systems. The Tam project focuses on extending the useful life of computers, eliminating computational waste, and providing an enjoyable, dependable, distraction-free user experience for people who use computers to teach and make things.

Who develops Tam

"Fred" in Tamlinux documentation is Fred Horch. Fred started the Tam project because he was not satisfied with Windows, MacOS and several versions of Linux, especially since AI agents started getting good in the fall of 2026. After trying Omarchy and listening to an interview with its developer, Fred decided to give it a go himself to see whether he could supervise AI agents to develop better workstation software. (I have AI agents write these guide pages; it's slightly less weird to tell an AI agent to write about you in the third person than it is to do it yourself.)

Feel free to copy and modify Tamlinux for your own use, or just take a look and get ideas for your own projects, as Fred was inspired by Omarchy.

Why "Tam"?

The tamarack (Larix laricina) is a hardy conifer that drops its needles each fall to grow fresh ones each spring. Tamlinux works the same way. It sheds old packages to make room for new ones. "Tam" can also mean "the absolute max" (referring to the goal of eliminating all computational waste), or "teaching and making" (referring to the intended audience of people who teach and make things with computers). Pick your favorite.

Core principles

  • Sustainability and hardware longevity: Manufacturing accounts for the majority of a computer's lifetime environmental footprint. Keeping working hardware in active service can reduce electronic waste. See Sustainability and hardware longevity.
  • Resource efficiency: Avoiding unnecessary polling and background services saves power and compute cycles for more important work.
  • Capability-based profiles: Software can inspect system capabilities and configure itself to run well on available computing resources, however limited.
  • Free software and open standards: Licensing intellectual property under the GPL-3.0-or-later preserves user freedom through copyleft.
  • Offline-first utility: The primary system command, tam, provides offline guide access and terminal tools so you don't need the Internet to learn how your system works.
  • Human direction and transparent provenance: Fred supervises machine intelligence tools (including agy, claude, codex, cursor, and opencode) to write code. The project documents how AI was used.
  • Make computing fun for humans: Computing should be fun, not a chore. AI agents and scripts handle boring drudgery and testing so humans can enjoy using their workstations to get things done quickly and easily.

Read more in Philosophy and principles.

Current status

Tamlinux is evolving. Today it depends on Arch Linux with Hyprland and Quickshell, but in the future Fred plans to migrate to Void Linux with Sway on wlroots. Fred uses Tamlinux as his daily driver on his primary workstation.

There is no simple installation script or procedure for getting the Tamlinux sofware yet. If you'd like to install Tamlinux on one of your computers, your best bet is to point an AI agent at the GitHub repo and ask it to help you.
