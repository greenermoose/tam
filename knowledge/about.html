Tamlinux is a workstation environment for Linux designed to provide a better computing experience for people who care for our planet. The Tam project focuses on extending the useful life of computers, eliminating computational waste, and providing a dependable, distraction-free environment for everyday work.

The developer

"Fred" in the Tamlinux Guide and project documentation is Fred Horch, the developer of Tamlinux. Fred develops Tamlinux for his personal and business computing needs. He started the project so his computers can do more and last longer, and freely shares his code so anyone who values resource efficiency and hardware longevity can use it, too, or just take a look and get ideas for their own projects.

Why "Tam"?

The tamarack (Larix laricina) is a hardy conifer that drops its needles every fall to grow fresh ones in spring. Tamlinux works the same way. It sheds old packages to make room for new ones. "Tam" can also mean "the absolute max," or "teaching and making." Pick your favorite.

Core principles

  • Sustainability and hardware longevity: Manufacturing accounts for the majority of a computer's lifetime environmental footprint. Keeping working hardware in active service is one of the most effective ways to reduce electronic waste. See Sustainability and hardware longevity.
  • Resource efficiency: Avoiding unnecessary polling and background services aims to reduce wasted work. Actual responsiveness and power use need measurement on the target machine.
  • Capability-based profiles: Tamlinux plans profiles by hardware capability, including graphical and terminal-only use. Older machines are validation examples, not a guaranteed age cutoff.
  • Free software and open standards: Tamlinux distributes its own software under GPL-3.0-or-later, preserving user freedom through copyleft. It follows freedesktop.org XDG directory standards and standard Wayland protocols. Read more in Philosophy and principles.
  • Offline-first utility: The primary system command, tam, is written in standard C (C11) linking the reusable libtam library. It provides offline guide access and terminal tools; available manuals and recovery capabilities depend on the installation.
  • Human direction and transparent provenance: Fred supervises machine intelligence tools (including agy, claude, codex, cursor, and opencode) to write code. Fred directs review and testing, and public records describe AI-assisted work.

Current status

Tamlinux is an evolving project developed in public. Today Fred uses it daily on his primary workstation running on Arch Linux with Hyprland and Quickshell. A transition to Sway on wlroots over a smaller, minimalist base system (such as Void Linux, with antiX Core as a fallback) is planned.

There is no installable ISO image yet. The installation framework is planned, while workstation inspection (tam inspect) and health assessment (tam check) operate in read-only mode today. Throughout this guide, anything not yet built is marked planned.
