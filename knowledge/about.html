About Tamlinux and the Tam Project

Tamlinux is a resource-efficient workstation environment for Linux designed for people who care for our planet. The Tam project focuses on extending the useful life of computers, eliminating computational waste, and providing a dependable, distraction-free environment for everyday work.

The developer

References to "Fred" throughout the Tamlinux Guide and project documentation refer to Fred Horch, the developer of Tamlinux. Fred develops Tamlinux for his personal and business computing needs. He started the project to extend the useful life of his own computers, eliminating code bloat and inefficiency, and develops in public so that others who value resource efficiency and hardware longevity can benefit as well.

Why "Tam"?

The tamarack (Larix laricina) is a hardy conifer that drops its needles every fall to grow fresh ones in spring. Tamlinux works the same way. It sheds old packages to make room for new ones. "Tam" can also mean "the absolute max," or "teaching and making." Pick your favorite.

Core principles and mission

Tamlinux approaches workstation design as an environmental and engineering discipline:

  • Sustainability and hardware longevity: Manufacturing accounts for the majority of a computer's lifetime environmental footprint. Keeping working hardware in active service is one of the most effective ways to reduce electronic waste. See Sustainability and hardware longevity.
  • Resource efficiency: Eliminating polling loops and unnecessary resident background daemons lets older computers run smoothly while allowing newer machines to run cooler, quieter, and on less electrical power.
  • Capability-based profiles: Rather than retiring computers by manufacture date alone, Tamlinux defines profiles by actual hardware capability: a full graphical Wayland desktop for capable systems, and a lightweight terminal-only profile for resource-constrained or older hardware (using circa-2006 machines as testing benchmarks).
  • Free software and open standards: Tamlinux distributes its own software under GPL-3.0-or-later, preserving user freedom through copyleft. It follows freedesktop.org XDG directory standards and standard Wayland protocols. Read more in Philosophy and principles.
  • Offline-first utility: The primary system command, tam, is written in standard C (C11) linking the reusable libtam library. Designed to launch in under 5 milliseconds and consume less than 4 megabytes of memory, it ensures that documentation, manual pages, diagnostics, and recovery navigation remain fully accessible without a network connection.
  • Human direction and transparent provenance: Tamlinux is developed under direct human supervision. AI-assisted changes are verified by the developer and explicitly recorded in public provenance logs.

Where things stand now

Tamlinux is an evolving project developed in public. Today Fred uses it daily on his primary workstation running on Arch Linux with Hyprland and Quickshell. A transition to Sway on wlroots over a smaller, minimalist base system (such as Void Linux, with antiX Core as a fallback) is planned.

There is no installable ISO image yet. The installation framework is planned, while workstation inspection (tam inspect) and health assessment (tam check) operate in read-only mode today. Throughout this guide, anything not yet built is marked planned.
