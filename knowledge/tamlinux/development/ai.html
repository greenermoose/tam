Artificial intelligence and human choice

This page explains how people can use artificial intelligence while keeping control of their work.

How does Tamlinux treat AI assistance?

Tamlinux uses AI tools as assistants during development while keeping human judgment in charge of every design decision, release stage, and public record.

Why it matters

AI tools can speed up drafting, research, and testing, but recording clear public provenance ensures readers and contributors always know what was assisted and who verified the result.

How to think about it

Think of an AI assistant like a research clerk in an engineering office: the clerk can draft notes and check references quickly, while the engineer remains responsible for reviewing, signing off, and deciding what ships.

New words

Provenance
The recorded history of who authored, assisted, and verified a change.

Trace AI-assisted work

Goal: explain the boundary between human direction, an AI tool and public provenance.

Mental model

A person directs work; the tool assists; records describe what happened. These are separate roles.

Worked example

AI coding assistants help build Tamlinux. They work under human direction: a person sets the goals, reviews the results, and decides what is tested, deployed, and released. Developers can configure or launch their selected assistant using tam-agent.

Public provenance

  • Every public Tamlinux repository keeps an AI-BLAME.md file and a work/ folder. Each session writes a W<n>-<name> record there and revises WHATNOW; older records are read-only history under work/history/.
  • Each record names the tool and its version, the model, the guiding prompts, and the commits it produced, so anyone can see how a piece of code came to be.
  • Commits made with AI assistance carry trailers naming the tool and model.

Respecting upstream projects

Many open source projects now do not accept AI-assisted contributions. Fred does not submit AI-written work and instead publishes fixes on public forks; see Carried patches.

Check yourself

Does a model name establish that its change passed review?

No. A model identifies the tool used; review and verification are separate evidence.

What evidence separates a proposed change from a delivered one?

Inspect the relevant plan, selected package and acceptance evidence; a proposal alone establishes none of those.
