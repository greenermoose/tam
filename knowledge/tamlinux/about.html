Guide / Tamlinux / About the Tamlinux Project

Tamlinux is workstation software for Unix-like operating systems. The project aims to extend the useful life of computers, boost computational efficiency, and provide an enjoyable, dependable, distraction-free user experience for people who use computers to teach and make things.

Who develops Tam

Fred started Tamlinux because he was unsatisfied with Windows, MacOS and several versions of Linux, especially since AI agents started getting good in the fall of 2026. After trying Omarchy and listening to an interview with its developer, Fred decided to give it a go himself: could he supervise AI agents to develop better workstation software? (Note from Fred: I have AI agents write these guide pages; it's slightly less weird to tell an AI agent to write about you in the third person than it is to do it yourself.)

Feel free to copy and modify Tamlinux for your own purposes, or just take a look and get ideas, as Fred was inspired by Omarchy.

Why "Tam"?

The tamarack (Larix laricina) is a hardy conifer that drops its needles each fall to grow fresh ones each spring. Tamlinux works the same way. It sheds old packages to make room for new ones. "Tam" can also mean "the absolute max" (referring to the goal of eliminating all computational waste), or "teaching and making" (referring to the intended audience of people who teach and make things with computers). Pick your favorite.

Core principles

Hardware sustainability:Manufacturing accounts for the majority of a computer's lifetime environmental footprint. Keeping working hardware in active service can reduce electronic waste. See Sustainability and hardware longevity.
Resource efficiency:Avoiding unnecessary polling and background services saves power and compute cycles for more important work.
Capability-based profiles:Software can inspect system capabilities and configure itself to run well on available computing resources, however limited.
Free software:Licensing intellectual property under the GPL-3.0-or-later preserves user freedom through copyleft.
Offline utility:The user guide, tam, provides offline access to knowledge and tools so you don't need the Internet to learn how your system works.
Process transparency: Fred supervises machine intelligence (including agy, claude, codex, cursor, and opencode) to write code. The project carefully documents how Fred uses AI.
Fun for humans:Computing should be fun, not a chore. AI agents and scripts handle boring drudgery and testing so humans can enjoy using their workstations to get things done quickly and easily.

Read more in Philosophy and principles.

Current status

Tamlinux is evolving. Today it depends on Arch Linux with Hyprland and Quickshell, but in the future Fred plans to migrate to Void Linux and to replace Hyprland with Tamlinux's own compositor, tam-pane. Sway and wlroots are not part of that plan. Fred uses Tamlinux as his daily driver on his primary workstation.

There is no simple installation script or procedure for getting the Tamlinux sofware yet. If you'd like to install Tamlinux on one of your computers, your best bet is to point an AI agent at the GitHub repo and ask it to help you.
