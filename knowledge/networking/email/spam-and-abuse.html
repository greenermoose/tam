Guide / Networking / Email / Spam and abuse

Spam is unwanted bulk mail. Phishing tries to trick a reader into revealing information. Receivers combine many imperfect signals to reduce both threats.

Why filtering is a balancing act

Most people want unwanted mail kept out of the inbox, but no filter is perfect. A filter that is too strict can hide a wanted message; one that is too loose lets abuse through. Systems therefore weigh several clues instead of relying on a single word or list.

Why it matters

A false positive can cost a person an invoice or a message from a new contact. A false negative can expose them to fraud. Good filtering needs review and a way to recover mistakes.

How to think about it

Think of a door keeper who checks a visitor list, asks where the visitor came from, reads the message and watches how often they arrive. One clue can be wrong; several together are more useful.

New words

Spam
Unsolicited bulk mail sent to many recipients.
Allowlist
A set of senders accepted in advance; also called a whitelist.
Denylist
A set of senders rejected or treated as suspicious; also called a blacklist.
Quarantine
A holding area for mail that was not accepted into the inbox.

What just happened

  1. Authentication answers whether a domain is allowed to claim the message.
  2. Reputation lists, greylisting and rate limits look at sender behaviour over time.
  3. Content filters examine words, links and structure after the message arrives.
  4. User reports and quarantine give people a way to correct the system.

If it goes wrong

You expected one check to catch all spam.
Each signal has false positives and false negatives. Systems combine them and tune weightings.
You treated greylisting as permanent rejection.
It asks for a retry later. Many legitimate servers retry automatically, while some bulk senders do not.

Tamlinux does not impose a single anti-spam policy. These pages describe portable mechanisms; the reader's provider or chosen mail software decides which signals are used.

Check yourself

Decide your answer before you reveal it.

Why can DMARC pass for a phishing message?

DMARC checks domain alignment, not intent. A compromised or abusive account on an authorised domain can send a deceptive message that still passes.

What is the trade-off in a strict allowlist?

It blocks almost all unexpected mail, including wanted mail from new contacts. It needs a review path so legitimate senders can be approved.

How it works

RFC 2505 records early anti-spam recommendations and the distinction between unwanted bulk mail and security policy. A DNSBL is consulted by reversing the sending address and looking it up under a list's DNS zone; RFC 5782 describes DNS blacklists and their operational concerns. Reputation systems may combine authentication history, complaint rates and sending volume. Filters assign scores to signals, and an operator chooses thresholds for rejection, quarantine or delivery with a warning.

Advanced usage

Feedback loops let mailbox providers tell senders about complaints so they can remove recipients. DMARC aggregate reports give domain owners visibility into who is sending on their behalf. Bayesian filters learn from labelled examples, so attackers try to add innocent words to legitimate messages; robust systems weight tokens carefully and retrain from trusted feedback. Challenge-response systems ask an unknown sender to prove they are human, but they can create backscatter if automated mail is challenged.

Edge cases

  • An allowlist entry can be spoofed if it trusts a display name rather than an authenticated identity.
  • Shared cloud and mailing infrastructure can cause one sender's behaviour to affect many others.
  • Quarantine without a clear release path turns a false positive into lost mail.

Check yourself

Why is a DNSBL lookup a weak signal on its own?

Lists differ in criteria and freshness, and addresses can be shared or reassigned. Responsible systems combine listing data with authentication, reputation and content signals.
