BS Computer Science Reading List

This page is a curated reading list for a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science. It emphasizes books and key articles that build the foundations expected for graduate study. It is not exhaustive; it selects widely used, durable works that teach core ideas, vocabulary, and methods.

How to use this list

Read for understanding, not just completion. For each area, start with one main textbook, do exercises, and write small programs or proofs. Use the key articles to see where ideas came from and how researchers state problems precisely. If a book has multiple editions, use a recent edition but do not worry if examples differ slightly.

A strong BS foundation usually includes programming, data structures and algorithms, discrete mathematics, computer architecture, operating systems, networks, databases, programming languages, theory of computation, software engineering, security, and at least one area of depth such as artificial intelligence, graphics, distributed systems, or human-computer interaction.

Core books by area

Programming and software craft

  • Brian W. Kernighan and Dennis M. Ritchie, The C Programming Language, 2nd ed., Prentice Hall, 1988. A concise model of systems programming, C semantics, and clear technical writing.
  • Harold Abelson, Gerald Jay Sussman, and Julie Sussman, Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs (SICP), 2nd ed., MIT Press, 1996. Foundational ideas: abstraction, interpreters, state, and programming models. Also available online.
  • Brian W. Kernighan and Rob Pike, The Practice of Programming, Addison-Wesley, 1999. Practical judgment about style, debugging, testing, portability, and performance.

Discrete mathematics and mathematical foundations

  • Kenneth H. Rosen, Discrete Mathematics and Its Applications, McGraw-Hill (use a recent edition). Broad undergraduate coverage: logic, sets, proofs, combinatorics, graphs, and number theory.
  • Ronald L. Graham, Donald E. Knuth, and Oren Patashnik, Concrete Mathematics, 2nd ed., Addison-Wesley, 1994. Mathematical tools used heavily in algorithms and analysis.
  • Gilbert Strang, Introduction to Linear Algebra, Wellesley-Cambridge Press (recent edition). Linear algebra for graphics, machine learning, scientific computing, and systems.
  • Sheldon Ross, A First Course in Probability, Pearson (recent edition). Probability foundations for algorithms, systems, AI, and data analysis.

Data structures and algorithms

  • Thomas H. Cormen, Charles E. Leiserson, Ronald L. Rivest, and Clifford Stein, Introduction to Algorithms (CLRS), MIT Press (recent edition). The standard broad algorithms text: design, analysis, and core data structures.
  • Robert Sedgewick and Kevin Wayne, Algorithms, Addison-Wesley (recent edition). Implementation-oriented algorithms and data structures, with strong practical examples.
  • Steven S. Skiena, The Algorithm Design Manual, Springer (recent edition). Algorithm design patterns and a practical catalog of algorithmic problems.

Computer architecture and organization

  • David A. Patterson and John L. Hennessy, Computer Organization and Design (RISC-V or MIPS edition), Morgan Kaufmann (recent edition). How programs map to hardware: instructions, datapaths, memory, and I/O.
  • David A. Patterson and John L. Hennessy, Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach, Morgan Kaufmann (recent edition). Performance-oriented architecture: caches, pipelines, parallelism, and evaluation.
  • Randal E. Bryant and David R. O'Hallaron, Computer Systems: A Programmer's Perspective (CS:APP), Pearson (recent edition). Bridges architecture, operating systems, compilers, and systems programming from a programmer's viewpoint.

Operating systems

  • Remzi H. Arpaci-Dusseau and Andrea C. Arpaci-Dusseau, Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces (OSTEP), Arpaci-Dusseau Books (recent edition). Clear coverage of virtualization, concurrency, and persistence. Also available online.
  • Abraham Silberschatz, Peter B. Galvin, and Greg Gagne, Operating System Concepts, Wiley (recent edition). Broad traditional OS survey.
  • Andrew S. Tanenbaum and Herbert Bos, Modern Operating Systems, Pearson (recent edition). OS concepts with implementation and case-study perspective.

Computer networks

  • James F. Kurose and Keith W. Ross, Computer Networking: A Top-Down Approach, Pearson (recent edition). Accessible network foundations from applications down to links.
  • Andrew S. Tanenbaum, Nick Feamster, and David J. Wetherall, Computer Networks, Pearson (recent edition). Comprehensive networking reference from physical layer to applications.
  • Larry L. Peterson and Bruce S. Davie, Computer Networks: A Systems Approach, Morgan Kaufmann (recent edition). Systems-oriented networking and protocol design. Also available online.

Databases and data management

  • Abraham Silberschatz, Henry F. Korth, and S. Sudarshan, Database System Concepts, McGraw-Hill (recent edition). Core database concepts: relational model, SQL, design, transactions, and storage.
  • Hector Garcia-Molina, Jeffrey D. Ullman, and Jennifer Widom, Database Systems: The Complete Book, Pearson, 2nd ed., 2008. Thorough foundations, especially relational theory and query processing.
  • Martin Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications, O'Reilly, 2017. Modern data systems trade-offs: replication, partitioning, transactions, batch, and stream processing. Useful bridge to graduate systems work.

Programming languages and compilers

  • Alfred V. Aho, Monica S. Lam, Ravi Sethi, and Jeffrey D. Ullman, Compilers: Principles, Techniques, and Tools ("Dragon Book"), Pearson, 2nd ed., 2006. Compiler structure, parsing, analysis, optimization, and code generation.
  • Robert W. Sebesta, Concepts of Programming Languages, Pearson (recent edition). Survey of language concepts and paradigms.
  • Michael L. Scott, Programming Language Pragmatics, Morgan Kaufmann (recent edition). Language design and implementation with broad coverage.

Theory of computation

  • Michael Sipser, Introduction to the Theory of Computation, Cengage (recent edition). Automata, computability, and complexity with clear proofs.
  • John E. Hopcroft, Rajeev Motwani, and Jeffrey D. Ullman, Introduction to Automata Theory, Languages, and Computation, Pearson (recent edition). Classic formal-language and automata foundation.
  • Sanjeev Arora and Boaz Barak, Computational Complexity: A Modern Approach, Cambridge University Press, 2009. More advanced; useful for students preparing for theory-oriented graduate study.

Software engineering

  • Frederick P. Brooks Jr., The Mythical Man-Month, Addison-Wesley (anniversary edition). Enduring lessons about teams, complexity, and project communication.
  • Steve McConnell, Code Complete, 2nd ed., Microsoft Press, 2004. Practical software construction, design, and quality.
  • Erich Gamma, Richard Helm, Ralph Johnson, and John Vlissides, Design Patterns: Elements of Reusable Object-Oriented Software, Addison-Wesley, 1994. A shared vocabulary for common design structures.

Security and cryptography

  • Ross J. Anderson, Security Engineering, Wiley (recent edition). Broad view of security as systems engineering, human factors, and economics. Also available online.
  • William Stallings, Cryptography and Network Security: Principles and Practice, Pearson (recent edition). Broad undergraduate cryptography and network security survey.
  • Dan Boneh and Victor Shoup, A Graduate Course in Applied Cryptography, online draft/book. Rigorous modern cryptography; good preparation for graduate security. See also bibliography entry.

Artificial intelligence and machine learning

  • Stuart Russell and Peter Norvig, Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach (AIMA), Pearson (recent edition). Broad AI foundation: search, knowledge, uncertainty, learning, and applications.
  • Christopher M. Bishop, Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning, Springer, 2006. Statistical machine learning foundations.
  • Ian Goodfellow, Yoshua Bengio, and Aaron Courville, Deep Learning, MIT Press, 2016. Deep learning foundations. Also available online.

Key articles and papers

These papers are worth reading for historical context, core ideas, and research style. Read them with a guide or course notes when needed; some use notation and assumptions from their era.

Foundations and theory

  • Alan M. Turing, "On Computable Numbers, with an Application to the Entscheidungsproblem," Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society, 1936. Formal model of computation and undecidability.
  • Alonzo Church, "An Unsolvable Problem of Elementary Number Theory," American Journal of Mathematics, 1936. Lambda calculus and undecidability, developed alongside Turing's work.
  • Claude E. Shannon, "A Mathematical Theory of Communication," Bell System Technical Journal, 1948. Information, entropy, channels, and coding limits.
  • Stephen A. Cook, "The Complexity of Theorem-Proving Procedures," STOC, 1971. NP-completeness and the P vs NP question's formal framing.
  • Richard M. Karp, "Reducibility Among Combinatorial Problems," in Complexity of Computer Computations, 1972. Classic NP-complete problem reductions.

Systems and programming

  • Edsger W. Dijkstra, "Go To Statement Considered Harmful," Communications of the ACM, 1968. Structured programming and the case for clear control flow.
  • Edsger W. Dijkstra, "The Structure of the 'THE'-Multiprogramming System," Communications of the ACM, 1968. Layered system design and synchronization.
  • C. A. R. Hoare, "An Axiomatic Basis for Computer Programming," Communications of the ACM, 1969. Hoare logic and reasoning about program correctness.
  • Edgar F. Codd, "A Relational Model of Data for Large Shared Data Banks," Communications of the ACM, 1970. Relational data model.
  • James H. Saltzer and Michael D. Schroeder, "The Protection of Information in Computer Systems," Proceedings of the IEEE, 1975. Foundational security design principles.
  • Butler W. Lampson, "Protection," ACM Operating Systems Review, 1974. Access control and protection models.

Networking and distributed systems

  • Vinton G. Cerf and Robert E. Kahn, "A Protocol for Packet Network Intercommunication," IEEE Transactions on Communications, 1974. Foundations of internetworking and TCP/IP architecture.
  • David D. Clark, "The Design Philosophy of the DARPA Internet Protocols," SIGCOMM, 1988. Design goals and trade-offs behind the Internet architecture.
  • Leslie Lamport, "Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System," Communications of the ACM, 1978. Causality and logical clocks in distributed systems.
  • Eric A. Brewer, "Towards Robust Distributed Systems," PODC keynote, 2000; later formalized as CAP by Gilbert and Lynch. Consistency, availability, and partition tolerance trade-offs.

Languages, compilers, and software

  • John Backus et al., "The FORTRAN Automatic Coding System," Western Joint Computer Conference, 1957. Early compiler and high-level language milestone.
  • Peter J. Landin, "The Next 700 Programming Languages," Communications of the ACM, 1966. Influential view of language design and ISWIM.
  • Barbara Liskov, "A Design Methodology for Reliable Software Systems," AFIPS Fall Joint Computer Conference, 1972; and later work on data abstraction. Foundations of abstract data types and modular design.

Preparation for graduate school

Graduate readiness is not only about reading titles. It means you can read primary sources, implement ideas, analyze trade-offs, and write clearly. Before graduate school, aim to be comfortable with:

  • Proofs and mathematical maturity: induction, invariants, asymptotics, probability, and basic linear algebra.
  • Systems implementation: write and debug non-trivial programs, use version control, read documentation, and measure performance.
  • Reading papers: identify the problem, assumptions, model, method, evaluation, and limitations.
  • Communication: explain technical decisions, write concise reports, and cite sources accurately.

For depth, pick one or two areas and read beyond the first textbook: recent survey papers, graduate course syllabi, and foundational papers in that subfield. Pair reading with a project, reproduction, or small implementation.

This reference is for readers at all levels.

This reading list is a curated selection of widely used computer science textbooks and foundational papers. Edition years are included where a specific edition is commonly cited; for active textbooks, use a recent edition unless a course requires another.

Many listed works are commercially published; check library access or official author/institution pages. Some works listed here are freely available online from their authors or publishers, including SICP, OSTEP, Computer Networks: A Systems Approach, Security Engineering, Deep Learning, and Boneh-Shoup.
