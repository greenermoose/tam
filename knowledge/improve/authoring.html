Authoring Knowledge Pages

This page explains how knowledge pages are written, structured into learning levels, annotated with interactive quizzes, and verified by build tools.

How the Guide teaches

The tam knowledge corpus is the built-in reference and learning guide for Tamlinux. Instead of splitting documentation into separate manuals for beginners and advanced readers, each topic presents progressive depths on a single page.

If you are new to the Guide, use the spacebar or down arrow to scroll, press 0 through 3 to change your reading lens, and press q to exit.

Why it matters

Detailed technical manuals often overwhelm beginners with options and internal architecture, while introductory guides frustrate experienced users by omitting edge cases. A unified page allows readers to choose their lens and learn at their own pace.

Under the hood, reading levels are filtered dynamically in C by src/depth.c based on your active lens.

How to think about it

Think of each page as a ladder. The first rung offers an everyday mental model and basic terminology. Later rungs add hands-on practice, error recovery, precise tables, and system internals.

New words

Lens
The reader's chosen level (All, Novice, Learner, or Expert) that filters page sections across a browsing session.
Retrieval practice
A learning method where questions hide their answers until the reader decides their response.
Fold
An interactive element that expands or collapses text in the console pager.

Write and test a laddered topic page

Goal: you will be able to author a valid multi-level knowledge page with interactive quizzes and verify it using corpus tools.

Worked example

  1. Start from the ladder template in docs/templates/ladder.html and declare standard metadata: 
    <meta name="kind" content="tutorial">
<meta name="skills" content="contributing.beginner, contributing.intermediate, contributing.expert">
<meta name="level" content="novice, learner, expert">
<meta name="track" content="improve">
<meta name="parent" content="improve/index.html">
  2. Format command walkthroughs with pre.session and an explicit risk level: 
    $ mkdir -p /tmp/scratch && cd /tmp/scratch
$ echo "practice" > test.txt
  3. Add a self-quiz fold at the end of the learner section: 
    <section class="quiz">
<h3>Check yourself</h3>
<details class="q">
<summary>Which tag wraps an interactive quiz question?</summary>
<p><code>&lt;details class="q"&gt;</code> inside <code>&lt;section class="quiz"&gt;</code>.</p>
</details>
</section>
  4. Verify page anatomy, links, and formatting before submitting: 
    $ make corpus-and-test

Try it

Preview any corpus page in the pager at a specific level to observe section filtering and interactive folds:

$ tam show --level learner improve/contributing

Press r to reveal hidden quiz answers, * to toggle all folds, or 0 through 3 to switch the reading level.

Need a hint on fold controls?

Pressing Enter on any highlighted fold link toggles that fold open or closed. Pressing r jumps directly to and expands the next closed quiz answer.

If it goes wrong

Linter reports QUIZ: details.q requires a summary
Ensure every <details class="q"> contains a non-empty <summary> element stating the question.
Linter reports RISK: privileged, package or boot commands require data-risk="system" or "boot"
Add data-risk="system" or data-risk="boot" to any <pre> block mentioning sudo, pkexec, pacman, or boot utilities.
Builder reports missing parent or cyclic hierarchy
Check <meta name="parent">; parent chains must terminate at index.html.

Tamlinux pages follow the sourcing rule: every claim about defaults, bindings, or behavior must be verified against actual code or command output, never remembered.

Check yourself

Decide your answer before you reveal it.

Where do command walkthroughs and practice exercises belong?

Learner (Level 2) only. Novice sections stay conceptual without CLI examples, while Expert sections show advanced reference examples rather than student tasks.

How does the pager behave when you press 'r'?

It opens the next closed fold at or below the cursor line and scrolls it into view.

Markup patterns: folds and asides

The corpus uses two mechanisms for supplementary guidance: interactive folds (which expand and collapse) and static asides (which render inline callouts with bold labels). Below are the full HTML snippets for each pattern and how the renderer displays them.

1. Question and answer (details.q / details.q.open)

Wrap self-quizzes in a <section class="quiz"> with an <h3>Check yourself</h3> heading. Each question is a <details class="q"> containing a mandatory <summary> stating the question and a non-empty answer body. Use class="q open" in expert sections for model answers.

<section class="quiz">
<h3>Check yourself</h3>
<p>Decide your answer before you reveal it.</p>
<details class="q">
<summary>Which key reveals the next closed quiz answer?</summary>
<p>The <code>r</code> key jumps to the next closed fold and opens it.</p>
</details>
</section>

How it renders: In the pager, the question displays with a collapsible fold control labeled [+] Answer (-> to show). Pressing Enter, Right Arrow, r, or * opens the fold to show [-] Answer (<- to hide) and reveals the answer text. In piped output (--pipe), the question appears inline and all answers are collected under an <h3>Answers</h3> heading at the end of the section.

2. Hint (details.hint)

Provide progressive clues or non-essential hints in exercises with <details class="hint">. A <summary> is optional; if omitted, the console label is used.

<details class="hint">
<summary>Need a hint on navigating links?</summary>
<p>Use Tab and Shift+Tab to jump between links, or press the number badge shown next to each link.</p>
</details>

How it renders: In the pager, renders a fold link labeled [+] Hint (-> to show) below the summary line. When expanded, it reveals the clue text. In piped output, the hint text is collected under Answers at the section end.

3. First time (aside.first-time)

Novice-level orientation for readers who have never used a Linux terminal belongs in an <aside class="first-time"> within the novice section.

<aside class="first-time">
<p>Type commands without the leading dollar sign (<code>$</code>). The prompt indicates the shell is ready for input.</p>
</aside>

How it renders: Implemented as a fold by src/fold.c. In the pager, it displays as an interactive fold link labeled [+] New to this? (-> to show), keeping novice guidance out of the way of experienced readers until expanded. In piped output, it prints New to this? inline and defers the body to Answers.

4. On Tamlinux (aside.tamlinux)

Highlight where Tamlinux defaults, keybindings, or tools differ from generic Arch Linux or upstream conventions using <aside class="tamlinux">.

<aside class="tamlinux">
<p>Tamlinux binds <code>Meta</code> as its primary desktop modifier instead of generic Super.</p>
</aside>

How it renders: In both the pager and piped mode, src/render.c automatically injects a bold On Tamlinux: heading block above the aside content. It is rendered inline as a static callout block rather than an interactive fold.

5. Peek under the hood (aside.peek)

Connect beginner simplifications to underlying system internals, source files, or architecture with <aside class="peek">.

<aside class="peek">
<p>Under the hood, fold links use custom URIs of the form <code>tam:fold:&lt;id&gt;</code> handled internally by <code>src/pager.c</code>.</p>
</aside>

How it renders: In both the pager and piped mode, src/render.c automatically injects a bold Peek under the hood: heading block above the paragraph. It is rendered inline as a static callout block linking conceptual explanations to implementation details.

How it works

The display pipeline runs in two distinct stages before text formatting:

  1. Fold numbering (src/fold.c): Folds are assigned sequential IDs before level filtering. This guarantees fold identifiers remain stable across lens changes.
  2. Level filtering (src/depth.c): Based on the active lens, sections matching other levels are replaced with Hiding <N> text placeholders. Piped mode (--pipe) prints questions inline and appends answers under an <h3>Answers</h3> heading at the end of each section.

Advanced usage

Inspect generated index tables and coverage matrices during build:

$ python3 tools/report-coverage.py --warnings
$ python3 tools/report-component-coverage.py --check

Edge cases

  • No hand-typed prefixes: Do not type Beginner: or Expert: in headings; src/depth.c injects [1 Novice] and [3 Expert] automatically in the All view.
  • The public boundary: Never include real home directories (use alex and /home/alex), private repository names, or internal decision codes.

Check yourself

Why are folds numbered before level filtering rather than after?

Numbering folds before level filtering ensures each fold's address (such as tam:fold:3) remains stable regardless of which reading lens is active, preventing link invalidation and ensuring consistent history.
