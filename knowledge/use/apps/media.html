Viewing photos, playing media, and converting files

This page explains how to look at pictures, play audio or video clips, and shrink large recordings for sharing without editing your original files in place.

Pictures, music, and video clips

Tamlinux packages lightweight native tools for everyday media: imv views photos and screenshots, mpv plays audio and video, and tam-transcode shrinks or converts a clip or image into a smaller copy for sharing.

Why it matters

Screen recordings and camera photos can be large. Converting a copy makes them easy to share while leaving your original capture untouched.

How to think about it

tam-open launches the right viewer automatically, and tam-transcode writes a new converted file beside the original instead of overwriting it.

New words

PNG
A lossless picture format used for crisp screenshots and diagrams.
File
Your original media item on disk, preserved separately when making a transcoded copy.

Inspect media properties and convert a copy safely

Goal: inspect media duration or dimensions read-only with ffprobe and understand how tam-transcode picks format and resolution.

Before you start: running ffprobe on an existing image or video is read-only. When testing tam-transcode, practice on a copy inside a scratch folder first.

Worked example

Check whether a media file exists and inspect its streams read-only with ffprobe:

$ ffprobe -hide_banner -v error -show_entries stream=codec_name,width,height ~/Pictures/example.png

To play a clip or view a photo cleanly from the terminal:

$ tam-open ~/Pictures/example.png

When converting a video or picture to share via Menu → Transcode (or tam-transcode), interactive pickers guide you through choosing the file, format, and target resolution without memorizing long ffmpeg flags.

Try it

Run command -v mpv ffmpeg ffprobe imv in your terminal to see which packaged media tools are present, or take a small scratch screenshot and view it with tam-open.

If it goes wrong

Screen recording plays smoothly locally but fails to upload due to size
Open the menu (Meta + Space) and choose Transcode (or run tam-transcode) to create a smaller copy while keeping the original recording.
No audio plays in mpv
Check the active sound output sink with tam-audio-output-sink or follow Changing volume and sound devices.

Tamlinux pairs tam-transcode with native ffmpeg and ffprobe, plus tam-menu-images and tam-transcode-ascii for specialized terminal or menu asset workflows.
