Changing the volume and choosing speakers or headphones

This page shows how to change the sound level, mute it, and choose which device plays or records.

Control sound

Goal: you will be able to change the volume, mute it, switch to headphones or speakers, and mute the microphone.

Why it matters: sound often comes out of the wrong device, or a muted microphone makes a call silent. Both are quick to fix when you know where the controls are.

Before you start: you need a graphical session and a sound device. The volume keys work even when another window has focus, unless that program has captured the keys for itself.

Use the volume keys at the top of the keyboard. A small overlay shows the new level. Press the mute key to silence the output; press it again to restore the level. If you hear nothing, press Shift with the mute key to try the next output, such as headphones instead of speakers.

Worked example

  1. Press the volume-up key a few times. An overlay shows the level rising.
  2. Press the mute key once. The overlay shows that sound is muted.
  3. Press Shift with the mute key. The overlay names the next output device, and that device becomes the default.
  4. Press Meta + Ctrl + A to open the audio panel. It lists the outputs and inputs you can choose.
  5. Press the microphone-mute key. The overlay says the microphone is muted; press it again to unmute.

Captured from the packaged audio commands and keybindings on 2026-11-02. Device names in the overlay depend on what is connected.

What just happened

  1. The volume keys call tam-audio-output-volume with a small step, and holding a key repeats it.
  2. The mute key toggles mute on the current output.
  3. Shift with the mute key ran tam-audio-output-switch, which moved to the next available output and kept its own level.
  4. The audio panel offers a direct choice when cycling is too slow.
  5. The microphone key toggled the default recording device.

Try it

Ask the system which output it is using:

$ tam-audio-output-sink
alsa_output.usb-C-Media_Electronics_Inc._Lenovo_USB-A_Wired_Stereo_On-Ear_Headset-00.analog-stereo

Captured on 2026-11-02. A long name is normal: it identifies the device by its connection and model. Your name will differ.

If it goes wrong

No sound at all
Press the mute key once and watch the overlay. Then press Shift with the mute key to check the output. Finally open the audio panel and confirm the intended device is selected.
Sound comes from the wrong device
Use Shift with the mute key to cycle outputs, or choose the device in the audio panel. Plugging in headphones normally switches to them.
Other people cannot hear you
Press the microphone-mute key and check the overlay. Then open the audio panel and confirm the intended microphone is the input.
Sound stops working after a device change
Run tam-restart-audio in a terminal. It restarts the sound service without rebooting.

Check yourself

Decide your answer before you reveal it.

Which keys switch to the next output device?

Shift with the mute key. The plain mute key only mutes or unmutes the current output.

You can hear music but callers cannot hear you. Which control do you check first?

The microphone mute key, then the input chosen in the audio panel. Output volume does not affect recording.

New words

Output
The device that plays sound, such as speakers or headphones.
Input
The device that records sound, such as a microphone.
Default device
The output or input used when a program does not choose one.
