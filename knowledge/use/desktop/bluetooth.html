Connecting Bluetooth headphones or a mouse

What this helps with

This page shows how to turn Bluetooth on, pair a wireless device, and remove one you no longer use.

Why it matters

Wireless headphones, mice, and keyboards need a one-time pairing. After that, connecting is usually a single click.

How to think about it

Press Meta + Ctrl + B to open the Bluetooth panel. If Bluetooth is off, turn it on there.

New words

Pairing
The one-time exchange that lets two Bluetooth devices trust each other.
Adapter
The Bluetooth radio inside the computer.
Forget
Remove a paired device and its stored key.

Pair and connect a device

Goal: you will be able to turn Bluetooth on, put a device into pairing mode, pair it, and connect it again later.

Learning path: Everyday desktop tasks — Start | ← Back | Next →

Before you start: you need a graphical session and a Bluetooth adapter. The device must be charged and close to the computer. Have its manual ready if you do not know how to start pairing mode.

Press Meta + Ctrl + B to open the Bluetooth panel. If Bluetooth is off, turn it on there. Put your device into pairing mode: usually hold its power or pairing button until a light flashes. The device appears in the panel; click it and wait for the connection.

Worked example

  1. Press Meta + Ctrl + B. The Bluetooth panel opens.
  2. Turn Bluetooth on if the panel says it is off.
  3. Hold the pairing button on the headphones or mouse until its light flashes.
  4. Wait for the device to appear in the panel, then click its name.
  5. When it connects, the panel marks it as connected. Play some sound or move the mouse to confirm.

What just happened

  1. The panel asked the Bluetooth service for nearby devices.
  2. Clicking a device told the service to pair with it and trust it.
  3. The device is remembered, so later connections do not need pairing mode again.

Try it

Check whether the adapter is on:

$ tam-bluetooth-power is-on
$ echo $?
0

Captured on 2026-11-02. The command prints nothing; the exit status is the answer. 0 means Bluetooth is on, and 1 means it is off.

If it goes wrong

The device does not appear
Put it back into pairing mode and keep it within a metre of the computer. Make sure Bluetooth is on. A device already connected to a phone may refuse a second connection until you disconnect it there.
Pairing starts but fails
Remove the device from the computer and try again: open the panel, choose the device, and use its forget or remove action. Then pair once more.
Headphones connect but there is no sound
Use Shift with the mute key to switch the audio output, or choose the headphones in the audio panel. See Changing the volume.
Bluetooth is off and will not turn on
Turn it on in the panel once. If that fails, run tam-restart-bluetooth in a terminal, then try again.

Check yourself

Decide your answer before you reveal it.

Which keys open the Bluetooth panel?

Meta + Ctrl + B. The audio panel is Meta + Ctrl + A.

Why does a paired device not need pairing mode every time?

Pairing exchanges and stores a key. The computer and device reuse that key when they connect later.

Bluetooth commands

CommandWhat it does
tam-bluetooth-power onTurn the adapter on
tam-bluetooth-power offTurn the adapter off and keep it off after a reboot
tam-bluetooth-power is-onPrint nothing; exit status 0 means on
tam-bluetooth-device pair <address>Pair and connect a device by address
tam-bluetooth-device connect <address>Connect an already paired device
tam-bluetooth-device forget <address>Disconnect and remove a device
tam-restart-bluetoothRestart the Bluetooth service

To find an address, run bluetoothctl devices. Each line shows an address such as AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF followed by the device name.
