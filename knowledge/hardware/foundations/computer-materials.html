Guide / Hardware / Physical foundations / Computer materials

A computer is built from metals, plastics, glass and carefully chosen chemicals. This page explains what each kind of material does and how to read a part by the job its materials perform.

What a computer is made of

Every part of a computer is made of matter. Copper carries electricity in cables and on circuit boards. Plastic shapes the case and keeps electricity where it belongs. Glass and special chemical layers make the screen show pictures. A small battery, strong magnets and tiny capacitors each use their own materials.

Why it matters

Different materials have different strengths. Metal conducts electricity and carries heat. Plastic insulates, is light and can be moulded into a shape. A material is chosen because it does one job well, not because it is rare or expensive. Knowing the job helps you understand why a laptop contains so many different substances.

How to think about it

Picture a layered sandwich. The outer plastic case protects; inside it a stiff board carries thin copper paths; small parts sit on the board; a metal sheet spreads heat; glass and chemical films form the screen. Each layer has a role, and the layers stack together into one working machine.

New words

Printed circuit board
The stiff sheet that holds components and connects them with thin copper paths.
Brominated flame retardant
A chemical added to plastics and board laminates to slow burning.
Indium tin oxide
A transparent conductor used as an electrode on screens.
LCD
A liquid crystal display that controls light from a backlight.
OLED
A display whose pixels make their own light from organic materials.

Read a material by its job

Goal: you will be able to look at a computer part and say which materials it uses and what job each one does.

Worked example

Imagine a laptop that feels warm along its base and shows a dim, even picture. You are not opening the case; you are reading the materials by their jobs.

  1. The bottom panel is often aluminium or steel. Metal spreads heat from the processor over a wider area and gives the laptop stiffness.
  2. Inside, copper traces on the printed circuit board carry signals and power. Copper conducts well and can be etched into fine paths, while the epoxy glass laminate called FR-4 insulates and supports them.
  3. The screen is a stack, not one substance: transparent indium tin oxide electrodes, polarisers, liquid crystals, colour filters and an LED backlight each change the light in a different way.
  4. Small plated contacts use gold, silver or palladium because these metals resist corrosion. A gold-coloured contact is therefore a choice about reliability, not decoration.

The method is to ask what the part must do first, then name the material that does that job.

None of these lists is a complete parts list. A designer picks one material from a family to meet a cost, safety or performance target.

Try it

On paper, list three parts you can see without opening the machine: the case, the screen and a cable. For each, write the job, one material that does it, and why that material suits the job. If you are unsure, name the property you would need to check, such as conduction, stiffness or transparency.

If it goes wrong

Two metal parts look the same
Ask about the job. A heatsink spreads heat; a bracket holds shape; a copper trace carries current. Similar looks do not mean the same alloy or the same reason for being there.
A screen is advertised as LED
The LED may only be the backlight of an LCD. Read the panel description before deciding which display materials are inside.
All plastics seem interchangeable
They are not. A case plastic, a cable insulation and an epoxy board laminate are chosen for different properties, even though all are polymers.

Check yourself

Decide your answer before you reveal it.

Why is copper used for traces on a board while the case is plastic?

Copper conducts electricity well and can be made into thin paths, so it connects components. The case is an insulator that can be moulded into a light, protective shape. Each material is chosen for its job; neither could simply replace the other.

A connector contact is plated with gold. What is the most likely reason?

Gold resists corrosion and keeps a low-resistance contact reliable over many insertions. It is used in a very thin layer, so the important property is surface behaviour rather than the amount of metal.

They are not grams per computer, and they must not be read as a recipe for a particular machine. Flame retardants and regulation

EPA assessments report brominated epoxy laminates with roughly 20 weight percent bromine for TBBPA-type systems and about 50 weight percent for a high-brominated resin. These are examples of documented formulations, not universal values for every circuit board. Brominated compounds also appear in housings, and antimony compounds act as synergists in some systems; see EPA PCB manufacturing and recycling and EPA DecaBDE alternatives assessment.

The EU RoHS Directive restricts polybrominated biphenyls (PBB) and polybrominated diphenyl ethers (PBDE) above its limits for homogeneous materials. It does not ban every brominated flame retardant; a board using a different bromine chemistry is not automatically outside the rule. Separate controls apply to particular persistent substances on the Stockholm Convention POPs list. Current thresholds and exemptions belong in EU RoHS Directive itself, not in a general materials page.

Display chemistry

DisplayMaterials and layersWhat they do
LCDLiquid crystal layer between glass sheets with indium tin oxide electrodes, polarisers often made from iodine-doped polyvinyl alcohol, colour filters and an LED backlight.The electrodes change the crystal orientation so the layers modulate backlight into a coloured image.
LED backlightCommonly blue InGaN/GaN LEDs with a yellow Ce:YAG phosphor.The blue light and converted yellow light combine into white light for the LCD to modulate.
Quantum-dot LCDCadmium selenide (CdSe) or indium phosphide (InP) dots.The dots convert backlight colours narrow-band, improving colour output; they are not the pixels themselves.
OLEDOrganic emitters between an indium tin oxide anode and a metal cathode, with encapsulation; no separate backlight.The organic layers emit light when current passes, and the encapsulation keeps out moisture and oxygen.
PlasmaNeon/xenon gas that produces ultraviolet light, exciting red, green and blue phosphors.The gas discharge supplies the energy and the phosphors convert it into visible light.

E-paperElectrophoretic microcapsules with charged white and black pigments such as TiO2 and carbon black.An electric field moves the pigments; the image is bistable and reflective rather than self-lit.

Avoid claims about exact layer thicknesses, gram amounts or element percentages for a named product unless a supplier's own documentation supports them.

Check yourself

A summary says a phone contains 7% gold. Why is that not a usable statement?

7% is a share of global gold use in electrical and electronics applications, not a fraction of a phone's mass. Without a defined denominator, product and year, the sentence mixes two different kinds of quantity. A defensible version names the source, the region and what the share measures.
