How display technologies make images

How display devices produce or control light, from picture tubes to electronic paper.

Screens do not all make light the same way

Some screens emit light; others control light from a lamp or reflect light from the room. Older picture tubes and modern flat panels use different physical processes. These technologies overlap in time rather than replacing each other in one simple chain.

From tubes to electronic paper

Goal: distinguish a picture tube, an LCD, an OLED and an electronic-paper display.

Worked example

An LED-backlit LCD uses LEDs as a light source and liquid crystals to modulate that light. An OLED pixel emits its own light. Both can be flat, but their pixels work differently.

Try it

Look up the display specifications for a device you use. Identify whether its panel is LCD, OLED or electronic paper, and whether it names a backlight or frontlight. Do not assume that an advertised LED screen has self-emitting LED pixels.

If it goes wrong

If a product description says only “LED”, consult its full panel specifications. The word may describe an LCD backlight rather than the image-forming layer.

A CRT directs electron beams onto phosphors that emit light and then decay. An LCD uses liquid crystals and polarizers to control light, often from a fluorescent or LED backlight. OLED uses light-emitting organic materials. Micro-LED uses tiny self-emitting inorganic LEDs; mini-LED often names small LEDs in an LCD backlight.

Other devices include plasma panels, which excite phosphors with tiny gas discharges; digital light processing projectors, which steer light with microscopic mirrors; and electroluminescent and LED indicators. LCD variants include twisted-nematic, in-plane-switching and vertical-alignment panels. Touchscreens add an input-sensing layer and can be paired with different display types.

Electrophoretic electronic paper moves charged pigment particles using an electric field. It normally reflects ambient light and can retain an image without continuously powering the panel. Some devices add a frontlight; a reader's processor and other electronics may still use power. Some Supernote tablets use E Ink electrophoretic displays, while model features vary. Ghosting, refresh speed and color support vary too.

Check yourself

Is an LED-backlit LCD an OLED?

No. An LCD modulates a separate backlight. An OLED's pixels emit light from organic materials.

Mechanisms and tradeoffs

CRTs scan a beam and rely on phosphor persistence. LCDs modulate polarization and typically use color filters and a backlight. OLED and micro-LED are emissive. LCD response time, backlight modulation, viewing angle and contrast depend on design; refresh rate alone does not describe the whole temporal response.

Electrophoretic panels move pigment in microstructures and can be bistable. They may show ghosting and use different update waveforms to trade speed against image cleanup. E Ink is a supplier and technology family, not the name for every electronic-paper mechanism. No display type guarantees comfort for every reader.
