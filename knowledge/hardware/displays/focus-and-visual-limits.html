Focusing and the limits of sight

How the eye focuses and why the detail you can see depends on conditions.

Focus is not fixed

The cornea bends much of the incoming light. The flexible lens changes shape to help focus objects at different distances. The image falls on the retina, where the brain receives information about light and shape. As the lens becomes less flexible with age, nearby text can become harder to focus.

Seeing detail depends on contrast, lighting and distance, as well as the eye. Two people may see the same screen differently.

Detail, size and contrast

Goal: relate apparent size and viewing distance to the detail you can see.

Worked example

A letter on a screen keeps the same physical size when you step back, but it occupies a smaller angle in your field of view. Fine strokes can become harder to distinguish even though the screen has not changed.

Try it

Read comfortable-sized text from two ordinary viewing distances. Notice how its apparent size changes. Stop if uncomfortable; this is not a vision test.

If it goes wrong

If text is uncomfortable, enlarge it or adjust viewing distance and lighting. Persistent changes in vision or discomfort merit advice from an eye-care professional.

Visual acuity is often reported with an eye chart. A standard 20/20 letter subtends five arcminutes, with critical features about one arcminute wide. This is a chart convention, not a universal threshold for every shape, motion, color or size difference. Contrast, exposure time, retinal position, age and individual vision matter.

Check yourself

Does 20/20 define the smallest change anyone can notice?

No. It describes performance on a particular chart task; detection and recognition vary with the task and viewing conditions.

Angular and perceptual limits

Visual angle depends on physical size and distance. Resolving two features, detecting a single high-contrast line, identifying an object and judging a small displacement are different tasks and can have different thresholds. Color discrimination also depends on the colors, surrounding field, luminance, adaptation and observer. There is no single fixed number of colors or universal minimum detectable size change.

Accommodation changes optical power to focus at different distances; presbyopia is the age-related loss of near focusing ability. Display pixel density is only one factor: viewing distance, optical blur, contrast and rendering also matter.
