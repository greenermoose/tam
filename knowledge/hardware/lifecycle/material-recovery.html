Guide / Hardware / Lifecycle and environment / Material recovery

After depollution, machines separate materials so they can be used again. Recovery ranges from taking out whole parts for reuse to dissolving metals from a fine mixture.

Taking the mixture apart

A depolluted device may be shredded into small pieces. Magnets pull out iron and steel, other machines push away non-ferrous metals, and light or density checks sort the rest. Some metals are then refined with heat or with solutions.

Why it matters

Recovery keeps materials in use and reduces the need to take new ones from the ground. Not every stream becomes the same product again: some plastic is reused in a lower-value item, a process called downcycling.

How to think about it

Think of sorting recycling at home, but with more steps. A magnet finds iron, a spinning field pushes aluminium, and a light sensor recognises a type of plastic. The exact machines in a plant vary.

New words

Shredding
Cutting material into small pieces so it can be separated.
Magnetic separation
Using a magnet to remove ferrous metal.
Eddy-current separation
Using changing magnetic fields to push non-ferrous metals away from other material.
Cullet
Recovered glass that can be melted to make new glass.
Chain of custody
A record of who held a batch of material and where it went next.

Worked example

Trace one batch after hazardous parts have been removed. Plant configuration varies, so read this as a common pattern rather than a universal line.

  1. Shred the material into manageable pieces.
  2. Use magnetic separation for ferrous metal.
  3. Use eddy-current separation for non-ferrous metal such as aluminium.
  4. Use optical or near-infrared and density separation for plastics and glass.
  5. Send metal concentrates to pyrometallurgy (heat) or hydrometallurgy (solutions).
  6. Track each output to its next holder.

EPA guidance documents stripping and dissolving gold, silver and platinum, then recovering them by reduction. Separated display glass can become cullet; CRT glass may need controlled landfill.

Try it

Match three outputs to a separation step: a steel bracket, an aluminium heat spreader and a mixed plastic housing. Then write one sentence explaining why brominated plastic is harder to return to a new housing than clean glass is to return to cullet.

If it goes wrong

You expect all recovered plastic to become new housings.
Brominated flame retardants impede closed-loop recycling, so much e-waste plastic is downcycled or discarded.
You assume one plant uses every method listed.
Configurations vary by feedstock and local markets. Ask what the plant actually does.
You treat metal recovery as the whole result.
Glass, plastics and residues also need an outcome. Ask where each fraction ends up.

Check yourself

Decide your answer before you reveal it.

Why comes magnetic separation before fine optical sorting?

Ferrous metal is easy to pull out with a magnet and would interfere with later steps. Removing it early reduces the load on finer, slower separation and protects downstream equipment.

What is the difference between recovery and closed-loop recycling?

Recovery means a material is obtained from waste; it may be used in a lower-value product. Closed-loop recycling returns it to the same kind of product. A recovered plastic can still be downcycled, so the word alone does not say where the material went.

How it works

R2v3 was released by SERI on 1 July 2020 and became the current version when the R2:2013 transition ended on 30 June 2023. It has Core and Process requirements covering reuse, downstream recycling, data security and environmental controls. Downstream material is tracked to final disposition or to registration with SERI. e-Stewards v4.1 from the Basel Action Network applies the Basel Annex VII export ban as if it were ratified everywhere and defines the start and end of chain of custody.

R2v3 and e-Stewards v4.1 are facility certifications, not product labels. They answer whether a facility follows documented practices; they do not certify that every output becomes a new product.

Edge cases and failure modes

  • Brominated flame retardants in plastic hinder closed-loop recycling; the fraction is often downcycled or discarded.
  • CRT glass may require controlled landfill rather than ordinary cullet use.
  • A chain-of-custody record ends at final disposition; a gap means the claim cannot be followed further.

Check yourself

Why does a certified facility still need downstream tracking?

Certification sets the facility's own requirements, but material usually leaves that facility. Tracking to final disposition or registration is what connects the first holder to the last. Without it, a claim about recycling stops at the gate.
