Guide / Hardware / Lifecycle and environment / Diagnosing and repairing

A device that looks dead is not always beyond repair. A few checks of power, signs of life, the firmware setup screen and the backlight narrow the fault before anyone opens the case.

Check the easy things first

Repair means finding the part that has failed and replacing or reworking it. Many failures are in parts that can be changed: a battery, a display, a keyboard, a fan, a drive or a memory module. The main board is often blamed before those parts are checked.

Why it matters

A careful check can save a working machine. It also keeps you safer, because a battery or a board can be damaged by careless handling. Ideas about a right to repair concern access to the parts and information needed for this work, but the rules differ by place.

How to think about it

Think like a doctor: ask what the patient can still do before assuming the worst. A light that comes on, a fan that turns or a setup screen that appears is evidence that narrows the search.

New words

Signs of life
Any response to power, such as a light, fan movement or a keyboard reaction.
Firmware setup
The built-in configuration screen available before the operating system loads.
Module
A part that can be replaced as a unit, such as a battery or memory card.
Electrostatic discharge
A sudden flow of static electricity that can damage sensitive electronics.

Narrow the fault before you open the case

Goal: you will be able to run a first-line check before deciding that a device needs board-level work.

Worked example

A laptop powers on but shows no picture. Work from outside inwards.

  1. Check the charger and the battery. Try the known-good charger and confirm the outlet works.
  2. Look for signs of life: a power light, fan movement, keyboard response or a boot sound.
  3. Try to reach the firmware setup screen. If it appears on an external display, the main board is probably working.
  4. Shine a flashlight at an angle through the screen. A faint image means the backlight may have failed rather than the panel.

Only after those checks does a display or board fault become the leading explanation. Each step removes a simpler cause.

Try it

With a powered-off device, list the parts you could replace as modules: typically the battery, display, keyboard, fan and heatsink, storage drive, memory, hinges and ports. Mark which ones are slotted, such as RAM or M.2 storage, and which are soldered or glued. Note where static control would be needed before touching a board.

If it goes wrong

You pry at a glued battery.
Use a suitable adhesive remover instead. A battery that is punctured or heated can be dangerous.
You touch a board without static control.
Use a grounded wrist strap, a common-point ground and a static-safe surface; store parts in static-safe packaging.
You conclude the motherboard is dead after one dark screen.
Repeat the flashlight test and try an external display. Display and backlight faults are common.

Check yourself

Decide your answer before you reveal it.

Why reach for a flashlight before replacing the screen?

A faint image under strong side light shows that the panel is still forming a picture. That points to the backlight or its drive rather than the whole display. Replacing the panel first would be guesswork and would leave a working part unused.

Which parts are usually the first replaceable modules to check?

Battery, display, keyboard, fan and heatsink, storage drive, memory, hinges and ports. Slotted RAM and M.2 storage are straightforward; soldered memory or processors need board-level work. A glued battery needs adhesive remover, not force.

How it works

Under electrostatic discharge control, a grounded wrist strap and a common-point ground keep the worker and the board at the same potential. Use a static-safe surface and static-safe packaging for removals; ESD standards give the controlling practice. Isolate batteries and tape their terminals. Never puncture or heat a lithium-ion cell. A glued battery is softened with adhesive remover rather than levered out.

Solder rework uses a temperature-controlled iron, solder wick and flux. Professional acceptability criteria for repair are in IPC-7711/7721. Soldered memory or processors need board-level work; slotted RAM and M.2 storage do not. Service manuals, board views, part numbers and firmware versions reduce misdiagnosis because they tell you what should be present and where it sits.

Policy is separate from technique. EU Repair Directive 2024/1799 applies from 31 July 2026 and requires repair at a reasonable price that does not deter repair for covered goods. It is not a single global right-to-repair law; scope and duties depend on the jurisdiction and product category.

Edge cases and failure modes

  • A failed backlight can look like a failed panel until the flashlight test separates them.
  • A loose cable can mimic a dead module; reseat once before buying parts.
  • Board-level rework can damage nearby pads if heat or force is applied for too long.

Check yourself

Why is a common-point ground part of ESD control?

If the worker, board and tools sit at different potentials, a touch can discharge through a sensitive input. Bonding them to one point removes the voltage difference. A wrist strap alone does not help if the work surface or the board floats at another potential.
