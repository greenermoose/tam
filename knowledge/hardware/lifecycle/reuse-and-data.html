Guide / Hardware / Lifecycle and environment / Reuse and data

A repaired or upgraded computer can serve another owner. Before it leaves your hands, the data on its storage must be handled deliberately, because deleting a file is not the same as making it unreadable.

A second owner, a clean start

Reuse means a product keeps being used instead of being discarded. A backup is a separate copy of files you want to keep. Sanitising storage means making old data unrecoverable by the next user.

Why it matters

Repair and upgrades usually save more of the existing product's embodied resources than buying new. The useful comparison is product-specific: the model, the failed part and the remaining life all matter. Without a backup, a repair can lose the very files it was meant to protect.

How to think about it

Think of lending a notebook to a friend. You copy the pages you still need, then remove your own notes so the new reader starts with a clean book. The device is the notebook; its storage holds the notes.

New words

Backup
A separate copy of data kept so it survives a change to the original device.
Sanitisation
Removing data from storage so it cannot be recovered by the next user.
Clear
A sanitisation category for ordinary reuse of the same device.
Purge
A stronger category for cases that need more assurance.
Verification
A check that the chosen method actually produced the intended result.

Prepare a device for its next owner

Goal: you will be able to write a short handover plan with a backup and a verified sanitisation step.

Worked example

A repaired laptop will go to a colleague. The plan has five short steps.

  1. Back up the files you must keep to another device or service, and confirm the copy is readable.
  2. List every storage location in the machine, including any second drive.
  3. Choose a sanitisation category from NIST SP 800-88r1: Clear for ordinary reuse, Purge when stronger assurance is needed.
  4. Apply the chosen method to each storage location.
  5. Verify the result and keep a short record of what was done.

Destroy is a separate category for media being retired, not the normal route when the whole device is passed on.

Try it

Write the same five-step plan for a device you know. Replace the laptop with your device, name its storage locations and state what would count as verification. Keep the plan to a few lines; the goal is a repeatable decision, not a long document.

If it goes wrong

You relied on deleting files or a quick format.
Those steps may leave data recoverable. Use a defined Clear or Purge method and verify it.
You forgot a second storage device.
Inventory the machine first. A card or spare drive can hold data even when the main drive is clean.
You sanitised before making a backup.
Stop and recover what you can from another copy. Make the backup the first step next time.

Check yourself

Decide your answer before you reveal it.

Why does a handover need verification?

A method only works if it was applied to every storage location and completed correctly. Verification is the evidence that the intended state was reached. A note of the method and the check also gives the next owner a clear record.

Why back up before a repair rather than after?

A repair can replace a drive, disturb a connection or fail in a way that makes the old storage unreadable. A verified copy made first means the files survive whatever happens to the device during the work.

NIST SP 800-88r1 defines Clear, Purge and Destroy and treats verification as part of the process. Choose the category from the risk of the data and the future use of the media, not from the speed of the method.

How it works

The choice between repairing and buying new is a life-cycle question, not a slogan. Repair and upgrades usually preserve more of the existing product's embodied resources, but the useful comparison is product-specific: a device with a replaceable battery and a long remaining life is unlike one with a failed soldered processor. Compare like with like before repeating a general claim.

Edge cases and failure modes

  • A device may contain several storage devices, including removable cards and small soldered memories.
  • A backup that has never been read back is a guess, not evidence.
  • Sanitising storage does not address data already synced to another account; check those separately.

Check yourself

Why is Purge not automatically better than Clear for every handover?

Purge gives stronger assurance but can cost time and may affect media differently. The right category follows the sensitivity of the data and the next use of the device. For ordinary reuse, a verified Clear is often sufficient; for sensitive data, Purge or separate media handling may be warranted.
