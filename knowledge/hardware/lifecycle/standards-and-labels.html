Guide / Hardware / Lifecycle and environment / Standards and labels

Labels and standards answer different questions. Some restrict what a product contains, some describe how an organisation is run, and some certify a facility. Reading the category first stops a badge from being taken as more than it says.

Four kinds of claim

A product label applies to a named product category. An organisational system describes how a company manages its work. A method tells you how to measure or assess something. A facility certification applies to a place that handles material.

Why it matters

Two badges can look similar and still mean very different things. A label on a box says something about that product. A certificate held by a company says something about the company's management, not about every item it sells.

How to think about it

Think of four stamps. One is on the product, one is on the company's paperwork, one is on the instruction book for measuring, and one is on the door of a recycling plant. Each stamp is valid in its own place.

New words

RoHS

Rules about collecting and recovering electrical waste.

A framework for managing chemicals.
ENERGY STAR
A voluntary certification for named product categories.
EPEAT
A product rating with Bronze, Silver and Gold tiers.
ISO 14001
A standard for an organisation's environmental management system.

Classify the claim

Goal: you will be able to say whether a claim is a product label, an organisational system, a method or a facility certification.

Worked example

Four claims, read by category rather than by how impressive they sound.

  1. "This laptop meets RoHS substance limits." A product claim about what is in it.
  2. "This company runs a certified environmental management system." An organisational claim about how it works.
  3. "The product was assessed with ISO 14040 and 14044." A method claim about the study.
  4. "This facility is certified for downstream recycling." A facility claim about a place, not a product.

None of these statements alone proves that a device is better in every environmental respect. Each answers one bounded question.

Try it

Classify three claims you find in product or company material. For each, write the category and one question the claim does not answer. If the source does not say which product or facility is covered, note that as a gap.

If it goes wrong

You read RoHS as a ban on all flame retardants.
It restricts ten named substances in homogeneous materials, with exemptions that change often. It does not ban all brominated flame retardants.
You treat ISO 14001 as a product label.
It certifies an organisation's environmental management system, not the goods that organisation sells.
You assume any software can carry ENERGY STAR.
The certification is voluntary and applies to named product categories. Software alone cannot be certified.

Check yourself

Decide your answer before you reveal it.

Why is a method not the same as a passing label?

A method describes how to measure or disclose a result. ISO/IEC 21031, for example, defines a scoring and disclosure method with no pass threshold. A report using it can be compared, but using the method does not mean the product earned a grade.

What does WEEE add that RoHS does not cover?

RoHS restricts substances placed in products. WEEE sets producer duties for collecting and recovering electrical and electronic waste. One is about content at sale; the other is about what happens after use.

Precise scope of common schemes

Reference

RoHS restricts ten substances in homogeneous materials: lead, mercury, hexavalent chromium, PBB, PBDE, DEHP, BBP, DBP and DIBP at 0.1%, and cadmium at 0.01%. Exemptions change often, and RoHS does not ban all brominated flame retardants. The RoHS directive is the authority for the scope. WEEE sets producer collection and recovery duties; see WEEE Directive 2012/19/EU. REACH manages chemicals through ECHA; a Candidate List listing of a substance of very high concern is an information duty, not a ban, under the REACH regulation.

ENERGY STAR is a voluntary US EPA certification for named product categories. Current specifications include Computers v9.0 of 27 Oct 2025, Displays v8.0, Enterprise Servers v4.0 of 12 Jan 2024 and Data Center Storage v2.1 of 19 Jan 2022. Software alone cannot be certified. EPEAT uses IEEE 1680.1 criteria with Bronze, Silver and Gold tiers; see EPEAT criteria.

EU Ecodesign uses Regulation (EU) 2019/2021 for electronic displays, Regulation (EU) No 617/2013 for computers and 2019/424 for servers and data storage. Framework Regulation (EU) 2024/1781 is the ESPR. Regulation (EU) 2019/2022 covers dishwashers, not computers. See EU ecodesign rules. TCO Certified generation 10 covers IT products (TCO Certified). Blue Angel has DE-UZ 215 for defined desktop software, DE-UZ 78 for computers and DE-UZ 213 for servers and data storage (Blue Angel).

ISO 14001:2026 was published on 15 Apr 2026. It certifies an organisation's environmental management system, not its products (ISO 14001). ISO 14040 and ISO 14044 are life-cycle assessment methodology, not labels (ISO 14040 / 14044). ISO/IEC 21031:2024 SCI is a measurement and disclosure method with no pass threshold (ISO/IEC 21031).

EU Batteries Regulation 2023/1542 includes portable battery removability from 18 Feb 2027 and staged targets (EU Batteries Regulation). Conflict-minerals rules are disclosure and due-diligence duties, not a sourcing ban (OECD minerals guidance). A product carbon footprint is a reported result within a chosen boundary, not a label by itself.

How it works

Ask four questions of any claim: who is certified, what category or material is covered, which version or specification applies, and what is excluded. Facility schemes such as R2v3 and e-Stewards belong with the recovery stage, while the schemes above mostly address product content, energy, management or method. Mixing the categories is the most common reading error.

Edge cases and failure modes

  • RoHS exemptions change often; a static list copied from an old page may be wrong.
  • A product can meet one scheme's criteria and still fail another scheme's scope conditions.
  • A version number matters: an older specification may not contain the requirements a reader assumes.

Check yourself

Why does an SVHC Candidate List entry not make a product illegal?

Under REACH, listing a substance of very high concern triggers information duties and may lead to restriction later. It is not itself a ban on the product. Check whether a restriction, not only a listing, applies to that use.
