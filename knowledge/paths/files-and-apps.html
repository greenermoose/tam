Guide / Learn step by step / Files and applications

This path shows where files live, how to inspect archives before extracting them, and how to open applications without risking real documents.

Outcome: After this path, you can find and inspect files, move data safely, manage permissions and open applications without risking real documents.

Prerequisites: none beyond reading

Time: About 50 minutes, including checkpoints and the capstone.

If you're stuck: read the program manuals with tam man <command>, list built-in help with tam help, or search this guide with tam search -- <term>.

Practice in temporary folders and on copies. A move, extraction or removal is hard to undo once it succeeds, so read the listing before you act on real documents.

Files, folders and safe copies

Everyday documents belong under your home directory; /tmp is scratch space. Before unpacking an archive, its listing tells you which paths it will create, so you can choose a separate destination and notice surprises.

Think of an archive listing as the label on a parcel. You read the label before opening the parcel, especially when the contents could land on top of existing work.

Should everyday documents be saved in /tmp?

No. /tmp is scratch space and may be cleared across reboots. Lasting work belongs under your home directory.

Expected sandbox state: No sandbox in this stage; no simulated files or output are expected.

chmod printed nothing. Did it fail?

Not necessarily. Many successful commands are silent. Inspect the mode with stat -c '%A %a %n' FILE before assuming failure.

What must you do before unplugging a USB stick?

Unmount or eject it, and wait for the desktop to confirm. Pulling it while writes are pending can corrupt the filesystem.
