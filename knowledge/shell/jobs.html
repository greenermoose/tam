Processes, Jobs, and Signals

Every running program on Tamlinux is a process with a unique identifier (PID). The interactive shell groups running commands into jobs that you can suspend, background, and resume.

Beginner: Controlling background jobs in the shell

Summary: Suspend a running program and move it into the background to keep your shell prompt free.

Prerequisites: Basic shell interaction (The Shell and Streams).

Command sequence

sleep 100

Press Ctrl+Z, then run:

bg %1
jobs

Expected output after Ctrl+Z:

[1]+  Stopped                 sleep 100

After bg %1:

[1]+ sleep 100 &

After jobs:

[1]+  Running                 sleep 100 &

What just happened

  • sleep 100 started running in the foreground, monopolizing the terminal.
  • Pressing Ctrl+Z sent a SIGTSTP signal, pausing the process and returning control to your prompt.
  • bg %1 sent a SIGCONT signal to job 1, resuming execution in the background.
  • jobs reported that job 1 is actively running in the background.

Common pitfall

Closing your terminal window sends a SIGHUP (hangup) signal to all child jobs, terminating them unless they have been detached with disown or started with nohup.

Intermediate: Process signaling and graceful termination

Summary: Locate processes by name and send polite termination signals before resorting to unconditional kills.

Worked example

Find and politely terminate lingering background sleep processes:

pgrep -l sleep
pkill -15 sleep

SIGTERM (signal 15) gives applications the opportunity to save state, flush write buffers, and clean up socket files.

Completion task

Start a heavy background compilation task with lowest CPU scheduling priority (nice level 19) and detach it from your shell so it survives terminal closure. Complete the missing command step below (marked with ___):

nice -n 19 make &
___ -h %1

Target output: job is marked so SIGHUP will not terminate it on logout.

Verification hints

  • The shell builtin to detach a job from the active session is disown.
  • Use jobs -l to inspect the job number and PID.
  • Use ps -o pid,ni,cmd -p <PID> to verify the nice level is 19.

Expert: POSIX process semantics and signal specifications

Reference: Process states (R: running, S: interruptible sleep, D: uninterruptible disk sleep, Z: zombie/defunct). Zombies occur when a child terminates and the parent has not yet invoked wait4(2) to read its exit status; orphan processes are adopted by PID 1 (systemd or runit). Signals are synchronous or asynchronous interrupts handled via sigaction(2).
