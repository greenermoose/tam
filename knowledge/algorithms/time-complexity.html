Time complexity describes how an algorithm’s work grows with input size. Big-O notation gives a short name to that growth.

Work, not stopwatch time

Time complexity counts how the work grows when the input grows. Big-O notation writes that growth with the small details left out.

Why it matters

A method that checks every pair of items can be fine for ten items and hopeless for ten thousand. The notation warns you before you try the large case.

How to think about it

Scanning a guest list once grows steadily as guests are added. Comparing every guest with every other guest grows much faster, like adding rows and columns to a square.

What just happened

The scan column doubles with n, matching O(n). The pairs column grows roughly four times when n doubles, matching O(n²). These counts are sample calculations for tiny inputs, not timings from your machine.

Try it

Run grep n=16 growth.txt. Compare 16 scan steps with 120 pair checks to see why the all-pairs method becomes painful quickly.

Check yourself

Decide your answer before you reveal it.

Which grows faster: scan or pairs?

Pairs. It is proportional to n times (n-1) divided by two, while scan is proportional to n.

Why leave constants out of Big-O?

Constants do not change the growth pattern; they matter for small inputs but not for the trend as n becomes large.

Reference: notation and caveats

Reference

NotationBoundMeaning
O(f(n))UpperNever grows faster than a constant times f(n)
Omega(f(n))LowerNever grows slower than a constant times f(n)
Theta(f(n))TightBoth upper and lower bounds match

How it works

Choose a cost model and an input size, count model steps, then keep the fastest-growing term and drop constant factors. Big-O notation is widely used even when a tight Theta bound would be more informative.

Edge cases and failure modes

  • Log bases differ only by constants, so log2 n and ln n share a Big-O class.
  • Multiple parameters need separate symbols, such as O(V + E) for graphs.
  • Best, average and worst cases can belong to different classes.
  • Cache misses and allocations may add hidden cost not visible in operation counts.
