Measurement shows how long code really takes on a particular machine. It complements analysis, because theory predicts growth while a benchmark records one run.

Stopwatch and map

A benchmark times a piece of work. Profiling looks for the parts that take the most time. Both give evidence about a real program, but only for the inputs and machine used.

Why it matters

A change that looks clever on paper may lose to cache effects or a library call. Measuring tells you whether the change actually helped.

How to think about it

Analysis is a map of the roads; a benchmark is one trip with a stopwatch. You need both to choose a route and to know how the trip went.

What just happened

The sample input doubles from 100000 to 400000 and the time roughly quadruples, matching a linear-looking cost over this tiny range. These numbers are sample output, not timings from your machine; one short run cannot establish a general bound.

Try it

Run grep 200000 bench.txt. The time is about twice the first row, which is the pattern you would expect from a single pass over the input.

If it goes wrong

The numbers are not from my computer.
Correct. They are sample data chosen to show a pattern. The sandbox does not benchmark your machine.
grep returned nothing.
Type grep 400000 bench.txt exactly, including the six digits.

Check yourself

Decide your answer before you reveal it.

Why not trust a single timing?

Background work, caching and clock resolution can change one run. Repeat measurements and report variation.

What does this table tell you about 10 million items?

Very little on its own. It covers three small inputs; you need wider data and analysis before extrapolating.

How it works

Warm up the code, repeat runs, and report a median or distribution rather than one number. Keep the input realistic, pin the build, and separate setup from measured work. Compare against a cost model so a surprising result points to a hypothesis, not a guess.

Edge cases and failure modes

  • Turbo boost and thermal throttling change clocks during a run.
  • Compiler optimisations can remove work whose result is unused.
  • Cache warmth can dominate small benchmarks.
  • Profiler sampling can miss short, frequent events.
