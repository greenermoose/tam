FFmpeg is a toolkit for audio and video files. It can inspect a file, change its codecs, filter a picture, or send media onward.

What FFmpeg works with

FFmpeg reads and writes media files. A container such as MP4 holds streams; a codec such as H.264 or MP3 encodes those streams; a filter changes them on the way.

Why it matters

One tool can answer basic questions about a file, extract its audio, shrink its picture, or prepare a stream for playback. Knowing which job you want helps you pick the right option.

How to think about it

Think of a packed lunch. The container is the box, the codecs are the recipes used for each item, and filters are the small changes you make before packing it again.

Check yourself

Decide your answer before you reveal it.

What does -vn do?

It disables video output, leaving the audio stream for the next step.

Why use -c:a copy when scaling video?

The audio stream is already usable, so copying avoids a second lossy encode and saves work.

How it works

FFmpeg builds a pipeline of demuxer, decoders, filters, encoders and muxer. Re-encoding costs CPU time and may lose quality; stream copy preserves compressed bytes but only works when the target container accepts them.

Edge cases and failure modes

  • Filter graphs change frame timing; timestamps may need repair.
  • Some codecs cannot be copied into every container.
  • Hardware encoders vary by machine and driver.
  • Spectrum and statistics filters depend on windowing and colour settings.
