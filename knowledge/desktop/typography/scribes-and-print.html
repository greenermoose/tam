Guide / Desktop and applications / Typography / Scribes, manuscripts and the printing press

Before books were printed, people copied them by hand. This page follows that craft from the first scribes through the printing press, and explains why the press changed more than just the speed of copying.

Books before machines

What this is

For a long time, stories and laws were spoken aloud and remembered. When communities began to keep written records, a trained person called a scribe copied the words onto a surface. A manuscript is a book or document made by hand.

Early writings were pressed onto clay tablets in Mesopotamia. In Egypt, people wrote on papyrus and rolled it into scrolls. Later, readers folded sheets into a codex, the ancestor of the modern book: a stack of pages bound along one edge.

Why it matters

Hand copying is slow, and every copy can introduce a small change. A printing press makes many matching copies, so a reader far away can hold the same text as the printer. That helped new ideas travel faster and helped more people learn to read.

How to think about it

Think of a photocopier that needs a person to redraw every page. A medieval scriptorium was a room where scribes did exactly that work, often adding decorated letters called illumination. Beautiful handwritten books remained valuable even after printing arrived.

Printing did not replace speaking or handwriting. Oral tradition, hand copying and printing overlapped for centuries, and each served a different purpose.

If it goes wrong

You placed block printing with movable type because both are printing.
They differ in the reusable unit. Block printing carves a whole page or image; movable type uses individual reusable characters that can be rearranged.
You thought illumination was a printing technique.
Illumination is hand decoration added to manuscripts, often in colour and with gold.

This guide describes the historical press in general terms. When you view a scanned manuscript or an early printed book on a current desktop, its letters are still drawn by a font. Check yourself

Decide your answer before you reveal it.

Why did movable type matter more than a carved block for setting new text?

Individual characters can be reused and rearranged into a new page. A carved block is tied to one page of content. Reusable type makes setting a different book a job of assembly rather than recarving.

Name one thing the printing press needed besides the machine itself.

Paper, ink, metal for type, skilled typesetters and a distribution network. The press was a system, not a single device; a printed book reached readers through trade and transport.

How it works

Gutenberg's press combined existing European craft with a mechanical press adapted from other uses; the 42-line Bible is the best-known early product, and Britannica's account sets out the sequence of his work Gutenberg and the press. Johann Fust and Peter Schöffer continued the workshop after him and produced books under their own names. Printing spread through European cities in the decades that followed, and books printed before 1501 are called incunabula.

Asia had printing earlier. Carved woodblocks printed whole pages, and Bi Sheng is credited with movable type made of clay in eleventh-century China. The European innovation was a durable metal type system tied to a press, not the idea of printing itself. Bringhurst treats the book as a craft object whose form follows from its making, which is why the change from script to type altered page design as well as production The Elements of Typographic Style.

The press then spread through European cities as craftsmen carried the process with them. Early printed books often copied the look of manuscripts: scribes rubricated headings, and binders finished volumes by hand. An incunabulum therefore sits between two worlds, using a new production method for an old-shaped book. The word refers to the earliest European printed books, not to a particular style of letter.

Industrial typesetting changed the economics again. Hot-metal composition let a newspaper set text far faster than a hand compositor, and phototypesetting later removed the metal while keeping the idea of a master letter. Digital typesetting turned type into data, which is why a modern family can serve a printed book, a web page and a terminal without recasting anything.

Advanced usage

When you examine a digitised catalogue, separate the object's shape from its production method. These read-only queries treat a local sample listing as data:

grep -c printed timeline.txt
cut -d: -f2 timeline.txt | sort | uniq -c
grep -i 'type' timeline.txt | sort

The first line counts printed entries. The second groups by production method. The third gathers every row whose name mentions type, which is a quick way to compare block printing with movable type without assuming they are the same thing.

Edge cases

  • A codex is a binding format, not a production method. Manuscript codices and printed codices both exist.
  • Oral tradition continued alongside writing and print. Printing did not erase performance, memorisation or handwriting.
  • Early type imitated handwriting. Blackletter styles in northern Europe copied the look of manuscript letters, so a printed page can resemble a written one.
  • Incunabula vary widely in quality. An early date does not guarantee careful setting or clear reading.
  • Movable type in Europe and movable type in Asia used different materials and were supported by different crafts; neither is a simple copy of the other.
  • Industrial typesetting changed the unit again: Linotype cast whole lines of metal, while Monotype cast single characters. Digital typesetting removed the metal entirely but kept the idea of reusable character shapes.

Check yourself

Why is the press called a social and technical system rather than a machine?

Because printed books required paper, ink, type metal, compositors, a press and a route to readers. A failure in any part stopped production. The technical device alone cannot explain how texts spread.

What is wrong with saying Gutenberg invented printing?

It ignores earlier block printing and Bi Sheng's movable type in Asia. Gutenberg's achievement was a durable metal-type system combined with a press in fifteenth-century Europe. Historical claims should name which innovation they mean.
