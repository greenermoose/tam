Voice Dictation on the Desktop

This page explains how to speak to enter text directly into any focused window without straining your hands or sending your audio to the cloud.

All voice recognition on Tamlinux runs entirely on your local computer using an on-device neural network. No audio, transcripts, or personal data ever leave your machine.

Starting and stopping dictation

Tamlinux provides two keybindings for dictation:

  • F9 (Tap to toggle): Tap once to start recording, speak your sentence, and tap again to stop and transcribe.
  • SUPER + CTRL + X (Alternative toggle): Also toggles dictation start and stop without reaching for function keys.

Dictate a sentence into a window

Goal: you will be able to speak a sentence and have it typed into a text editor or terminal without touching your keyboard keys.

Why it matters: dictation allows fast note-taking and relieves physical wrist strain during long sessions.

Before you start: ensure your microphone (such as a USB headset) is plugged in and selected as your default recording device. Open an empty text editor or terminal.

F9 is a toggle key. Tap F9 once to start speaking, then tap F9 again when you finish your sentence.

Worked example

  1. Click into your empty practice window so it has focus.
  2. Tap F9. A small on-screen indicator appears showing that the system is recording.
  3. Speak in a clear, natural voice: "Tamlinux runs speech recognition entirely on my local machine."
  4. Tap F9 again. The indicator displays transcribing, and within one to two seconds the transcribed text is typed at your cursor.

This walkthrough is checked against the packaged Voxtype bindings, not a claim that a GUI acceptance test was performed in your session.

What just happened

  1. The compositor detected the F9 press and instructed the Voxtype background daemon to start recording.
  2. The daemon captured your voice stream directly into memory through the PipeWire audio server.
  3. When you tapped F9 a second time, the local Whisper speech model converted the recorded sound into text.
  4. The Wayland virtual keyboard tool (wtype) simulated keyboard strokes to type the words into the focused window.

Try it

Practice dictating another sentence: tap F9, speak a brief phrase, then tap F9 again to stop. You can also press SUPER + CTRL + X to toggle dictation. Observe that the transcribed text is typed into the focused window.

If it goes wrong

No text appears after speaking
Check that your microphone is unmuted and set as default. Check the service status in a terminal with systemctl --user status voxtype.
A word is misheard
Whisper uses context to guess words. Speak at a steady pace. Custom word replacements can be added to your configuration in ~/.config/voxtype/config.toml under [text] replacements.

Check yourself

Decide before revealing the explanation.

Where is your voice recording sent during dictation?

Nowhere outside your machine. Audio is held in local memory and processed on your own CPU by an on-device Whisper model.

How does the transcribed text enter the active application?

A virtual keyboard injector (wtype) sends Wayland keyboard events to the focused window, exactly as if typed on a physical keyboard.

How dictation works under the hood

Tamlinux links several fast, lightweight components to deliver offline dictation:

  1. Audio capture: PipeWire records 16 kHz 16-bit mono audio directly into daemon process memory (RAM). No audio files are written to disk.
  2. Inference engine: The Voxtype daemon passes the audio buffer to whisper.cpp, which executes OpenAI's Whisper model (ggml-small.en.bin stored in ~/.local/share/voxtype/models/) using AVX2 vector instructions.
  3. Context optimization: Clips under ~22 seconds skip unnecessary padding, allowing typical phrases to transcribe in about 1 second.
  4. Window injection: The daemon instructs wtype to inject keystrokes into the active Wayland surface with a 1 ms character delay.
