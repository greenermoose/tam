Voice Dictation on the Desktop

This page explains how to speak to enter text directly into any focused window without straining your hands or sending your audio to the cloud.

All voice recognition on Tamlinux runs entirely on your local computer using an on-device neural network. No audio, transcripts, or personal data ever leave your machine.

What is local voice dictation?

Dictation turns spoken words into typed text in the focused window. Tamlinux does that on your machine: audio stays in memory, a local Whisper model transcribes it, and a virtual keyboard types the result.

Two shortcuts start recording: hold F9 (push-to-talk), or press Meta + Ctrl + X once to start and again to stop.

Why it matters

Dictation speeds note-taking and can relieve wrist strain. Keeping recognition on-device means you do not send speech to a remote service.

How to think about it

Think of dictation as a microphone plus a typer: the compositor starts recording, Whisper turns sound into words, and wtype injects keystrokes into the focused window as if you typed them.

Before you start: ensure your microphone is plugged in and selected as the default recording device. Open an empty text editor or terminal.

F9 is push-to-talk. Press and hold F9 while you speak, then release F9 when you finish. Meta + Ctrl + X toggles start and stop with separate presses.

Worked example

  1. Click into your empty practice window so it has focus.
  2. Press and hold F9. A small on-screen indicator shows that the system is recording.
  3. Speak clearly: "Tamlinux runs speech recognition entirely on my local machine."
  4. Release F9. The indicator shows transcribing, and within one to two seconds the text is typed at your cursor.

This walkthrough is checked against the packaged Voxtype bindings, not a claim that a GUI acceptance test was performed in your session.

What just happened

  1. The compositor detected F9 and told the Voxtype daemon to start recording.
  2. The daemon captured audio in memory through PipeWire.
  3. On release, the local Whisper model converted the sound into text.
  4. wtype injected Wayland keyboard events into the focused window.

Try it

Hold F9, speak a brief phrase, then release. Or press Meta + Ctrl + X once to start and again to stop. Confirm the text appears only in the focused window.

If it goes wrong

No text appears after speaking
Check that the microphone is unmuted and set as default. In a terminal, run systemctl --user status voxtype.
A word is misheard
Speak at a steady pace. Custom replacements can be added under [text] replacements in ~/.config/voxtype/config.toml.

Shell prompts use a separate F10 path that validates commands before inserting them. See Shell voice commands.

Check yourself

Where is your voice recording sent during dictation?

Nowhere outside your machine. Audio is held in local memory and processed on your own CPU by an on-device Whisper model.

How does the transcribed text enter the active application?

A virtual keyboard injector (wtype) sends Wayland keyboard events to the focused window.

How dictation works under the hood

  1. Audio capture: PipeWire records 16 kHz 16-bit mono audio into daemon memory. No audio files are written to disk.
  2. Inference: whisper.cpp runs OpenAI's Whisper model (ggml-small.en.bin under ~/.local/share/voxtype/models/).
  3. Context optimization: Short clips skip unnecessary padding so typical phrases finish in about a second.
  4. Window injection: wtype injects keystrokes into the active Wayland surface.
