Notifications

This page explains how desktop alerts appear, how to dismiss them, and how to quiet them for a while.

What is a notification?

A notification is a short alert from an app or the system: a finished download, a calendar reminder, or a battery warning. It appears briefly without forcing you into that app.

Why it matters

You can stay informed without leaving your current window—or silence alerts when you need quiet.

How to think about it

Alerts come from the notification service; silencing is a toggle; history lets you reopen recent items.

New words

Silencing
Temporarily suppressing notification display without quitting apps.

Dismiss or quiet alerts

Goal: you will be able to clear a toast and find the silence control.

Worked example

  1. Follow the job recipe Reading and dismissing notifications to send a test alert with tam-notification-send.
  2. Dismiss with Meta + N; open history with Meta + Shift + H.
  3. Silence new pop-ups with Meta + Ctrl + N (tam-toggle-notification-silencing), then turn them back on the same way.

Bar plugins may surface unread counts; see The top bar.
