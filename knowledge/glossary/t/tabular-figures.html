Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with T / Tabular figures

Tabular figures are numeral glyphs that all have the same advance width, so numbers form neat columns.

When numbers are listed under each other you want the decimal points to line up. Tabular figures give every digit the same width, which makes that happen. They are the usual choice for invoices and spreadsheets.

The OpenType tnum feature switches a font to tabular digits; pnum selects proportional ones. Tabular figures are important in tables and financial reports but look slightly unnatural in running prose. They can combine with lining (lnum) or oldstyle (onum) shapes.

Tabular digits usually get identical advances while keeping different outline widths, with extra side bearing inserted for narrow digits such as 1; a font may also provide a fixed decimal-point glyph. Feature precedence matters because a font may map both tnum and pnum to the same lookup. Terminal interfaces use monospace faces, where every glyph already shares an advance.
