Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with T / Text shaping

Text shaping is the step that turns a sequence of characters into positioned glyphs before drawing.

The letters you type are not always the shapes you see. Shaping is the hidden step that joins f and i, moves accents onto letters and arranges Arabic into its connected forms. Without it, complex scripts would look wrong.

A shaper such as HarfBuzz reads the font's OpenType tables, applies features for ligatures, kerning and mark placement, and returns glyph IDs with offsets. Different scripts need different rules: Arabic letters join, Indic vowel signs move around the base consonant, and Latin text mostly needs kerning and ligatures.

Shaping works on items with a script, direction, language and font; it runs GSUB substitutions before GPOS positioning, with features such as ccmp, locl, rlig, liga, calt, kern, mark and mkmk. Output clusters map glyphs back to input code points for editing. Fallback for missing glyphs must restart shaping at a cluster boundary so joins and marks are recalculated.
