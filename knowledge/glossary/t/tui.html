TUI (Text User Interface)

A full-screen interactive program that draws menus, panes, and forms inside a terminal using text characters and keyboard navigation.

A TUI is like a full-screen app that lives inside your text terminal. Instead of scrolling one line at a time like ordinary commands, it takes over the whole terminal window with boxes, lists, and status bars you can move around with arrow keys and shortcuts—even over a remote text connection where graphical windows are not available.

Unlike a line-oriented CLI tool that prints output and exits, a TUI (such as tam or htop) switches the terminal out of normal line-buffered mode so it can react immediately to individual keystrokes and window resizes. Because TUIs draw with text cells and ANSI cursor codes, they work seamlessly over SSH or serial consoles, though selecting text with a mouse may require holding Shift when the app captures mouse events.

Why does resizing the window or pressing keys behave differently in a TUI than at a normal shell prompt?

A TUI puts the terminal into raw or cbreak mode and handles keystrokes and SIGWINCH resize events in its own event loop, redrawing the screen rather than letting the kernel line discipline buffer lines.

A TUI configures termios (disabling ICANON and ECHO for raw/cbreak input, often masking ISIG when binding Ctrl+C), switches to the alternate screen buffer (CSI ?1049h / CSI ?1049l) to preserve scrollback, and issues ECMA-48/ANSI escape sequences (via terminfo capabilities or direct VT sequences) for cursor addressing and diffed cell updates. Modern TUIs also negotiate bracketed paste (?2004h), SGR mouse reporting (?1006h), and synchronized output (?2026h) to avoid tearing. Failure modes include leaving the TTY in raw/alternate-screen state after a crash (reset restores termios) and width miscalculations on wide characters or grapheme clusters.
