KDE (K Desktop Environment / Plasma)

KDE is a long-running open-source community that builds the Plasma desktop environment, the KDE Frameworks libraries, and applications such as Dolphin and Okular.

Think of KDE like a complete workshop for your computer screen: it provides the taskbar, menus, settings panels, and everyday apps (like a file manager and PDF viewer) so everything looks and feels cohesive. It matters if you want a Linux desktop that feels familiar right away while letting you customize almost every detail.

In practice, “KDE” often refers to the Plasma desktop, which runs on top of the Qt toolkit and the KWin window manager/compositor. You do not have to run Plasma to use KDE apps—tools like Kdenlive, Krita, or Kate work on other desktops or tiling compositors, though they pull in Qt and KDE Frameworks libraries alongside GTK-based apps.

If you install a KDE app like Okular on a non-KDE desktop, why does it bring in extra Qt and Frameworks packages?

Because KDE apps are built on Qt and KDE Frameworks rather than GTK, so your package manager installs those shared libraries for them to run.

Organized legally under KDE e.V., the ecosystem splits into three release tracks: KDE Frameworks (tiered Qt add-on libraries such as KIO and Kirigami), Plasma Desktop (centered on plasmashell, kwin_wayland, and xdg-desktop-portal-kde), and KDE Gear applications. Under Wayland, kwin_wayland acts as both compositor and session manager for window placement, fractional scaling, and ICC color management, whereas X11 sessions separate KWin from Xorg.
