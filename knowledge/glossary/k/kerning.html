Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with K / Kerning

Kerning is the small spacing adjustment made between certain pairs of letters so words look evenly spaced.

Letters such as A and V have sloping sides that leave a big gap if spaced like straight letters. Kerning tightens that gap so the word looks even. You rarely notice it, but you notice when it is missing.

Kerning comes from the font, not from the document: the font lists pairs and adjustments that a shaping engine applies when those letters meet. It is automatic in most text and differs from tracking, which adds uniform space to every letter. Terminal fonts generally avoid pair kerning to keep character cells aligned.

Modern kerning is expressed as GPOS pair-adjustment lookups keyed by glyph classes and script or language systems; older fonts use the kern table. Values are in font units, scaled by pixels per em, and can be positive or negative. HarfBuzz applies kerning after substitutions so ligatures and contextual alternates are accounted for. Monospace faces usually omit it because changing a pair's total advance would break grid alignment.
