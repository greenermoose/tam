Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with I / Illumination

Illumination is the painted decoration added to manuscripts, from gold initials to full-page illustrations.

Medieval manuscripts were not just text: scribes and artists added bright initials, borders and pictures, sometimes with real gold. That decoration is called illumination, and it made books beautiful as well as readable and valuable.

Illumination was usually done after the main text was written, often by a different craftsperson. A large decorated initial marked a section start, while smaller rubrics and flourishes guided the reader. A single page could combine text, music and image, and wealthy patrons commissioned elaborate books.

Illuminators worked with pigments, gold leaf and sizing on parchment; the order of text, ruling, illustration and binding can be reconstructed from physical evidence. Digital facsimiles capture illumination as image layers alongside transcribed text. The term does not name a writing system: the underlying text of an illuminated manuscript is still Latin, Greek or another language.
