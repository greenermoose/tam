Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with I / Italic

Italic is a font style with leaning, often calligraphic letterforms, designed rather than merely tilted.

Italic letters lean to the right and often look handwritten. They are used for emphasis, book titles and quotations. A true italic is drawn by the type designer, not just slanted automatically by the computer.

Italic faces often change letter shapes: the lowercase a and g may look completely different from their upright forms. CSS distinguishes italic from oblique, which is a slanted version of the upright design with an optional angle. If a font has no italic face, applications may synthesize a slant, with mixed results.

OpenType marks italic design with OS/2.fsSelection and head.macStyle bits and records the angle in post.italicAngle, negative for a rightward lean. Variable fonts use the ital axis for a designed italic and slnt for shear. GSUB features can select italic-specific forms; mixing an italic run with upright fallback glyphs produces jarring style changes.
