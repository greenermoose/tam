Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with I / Ideogram

An ideogram is a sign whose shape conveys an idea rather than the sound of a word.

A drawing of a tree that means 'tree' regardless of your language is an ideogram. It communicates a concept directly instead of spelling out a word sound by sound. The idea is easier to grasp than the exact pronunciation.

Chinese characters are often called ideograms, though most are actually logograms or phonetic-semantic compounds that hint at pronunciation. Egyptian hieroglyphs and cuneiform signs likewise mix idea-based and sound-based uses. Few writing systems are purely ideographic, so the word should be used carefully.

Semiotics distinguishes ideograms, which denote concepts, from logograms, which denote words of a particular language. Chinese characters usually combine a semantic radical with a phonetic component. Unicode encodes characters rather than ideas, giving each standardized sign a code point regardless of its history; fonts may draw regional variants differently.
