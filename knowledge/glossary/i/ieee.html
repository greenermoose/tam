IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers)

The IEEE is a worldwide association of engineers whose published standards shape how computers, networks and electronics work together.

Devices from different makers have to agree on small details before they work together, such as how a cable carries bits or how a program stores a fraction. The IEEE publishes the written agreements, called standards, that settle those details. You meet its work every day: Ethernet and Wi-Fi come from IEEE standards, and so does the way most programs handle decimal numbers. The letters come from two older engineering societies that joined in 1963 to form the organization.

On Linux you rarely see the IEEE name, but its standards are everywhere. The IEEE 802 family defines local networking: 802.3 is Ethernet and 802.11 is Wi-Fi, while IEEE 802.3an is the 10 Gigabit Ethernet amendment over copper cabling. IEEE 754 defines the floating-point format behind C's float and double. The POSIX interface is published as IEEE Std 1003.1 jointly with The Open Group. The association grew out of the AIEE and the Institute of Radio Engineers, which merged in 1963.

Are all IEEE standards free to read?

No. Many standards are sold, though the IEEE GET Program makes a selection available at no cost. By contrast, the IETF publishes its protocol documents as freely available RFCs.

Formed on 1 January 1963 by the consolidation of the American Institute of Electrical Engineers (founded 1884) and the Institute of Radio Engineers (founded 1912), the IEEE now coordinates more than 2,000 active standards through its Standards Association. IEEE 802 covers local and metropolitan area networks: 802.1 for bridging and virtual LANs, 802.3 for Ethernet, and 802.11 for Wi-Fi. IEEE 754 (revised in 2019) specifies binary and decimal floating-point formats, rounding rules and exception handling; IEEE 1164 defines the nine-valued logic set used by VHDL's std_logic. POSIX is IEEE Std 1003.1, maintained with The Open Group, and ISO/IEC often adopts IEEE work as international standards, as with ISO/IEC 9899 for C. A standard may be identified by its revision year, so IEEE Std 1003.1-2024 is the 2024 edition of POSIX.
