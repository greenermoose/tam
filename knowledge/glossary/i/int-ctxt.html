INT-CTXT (Integrity of Ciphertext)

A cryptographic security property guaranteeing that an attacker cannot forge a new valid ciphertext that decrypts without being rejected.

Imagine sealing a letter inside a tamper-evident envelope. Even if a thief cannot read what is inside, INT-CTXT ensures the thief cannot alter the envelope or glue together a fake one that the receiver would mistakenly accept as genuine. Any tampering causes the receiver to reject the message right away.

Encryption alone (such as unauthenticated AES-CTR or AES-CBC) only hides data; it does not stop attackers from flipping bits in transit. Pairing IND-CPA encryption with INT-CTXT authentication—as modern Authenticated Encryption with Associated Data (AEAD) modes like AES-GCM and ChaCha20-Poly1305 do—achieves full IND-CCA2 security against active tampering.

What can go wrong if a protocol uses AES-CTR without an authentication tag (no INT-CTXT)?

An attacker can flip specific bits in the ciphertext and predictably flip the corresponding bits in the decrypted plaintext without triggering any decryption error.

In the INT-CTXT game, a PPT adversary with access to an encryption oracle E_k(·) has negligible probability of producing a ciphertext c* not previously returned by E_k(·) that verifies without returning ⊥. By the Bellare–Namprempre theorem, Encrypt-then-MAC with a strongly unforgeable MAC (SUF-CMA) and an IND-CPA cipher achieves both INT-CTXT and IND-CCA2, whereas MAC-then-Encrypt can fail if padding errors leak before MAC verification. INT-CTXT is strictly stronger than INT-PTXT, as it also forbids forging a fresh ciphertext for an already-seen plaintext.
