Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with I / ISO/IEC 21031 (Software Carbon Intensity)

International standard defining the Software Carbon Intensity (SCI) specification for measuring software’s carbon footprint.

Software does not burn fuel directly, yet running it on laptops, phones, and data-center servers uses electricity and wears out hardware that took energy to build. ISO/IEC 21031 gives developers and organizations a shared way to score how much carbon pollution a piece of software causes per unit of work—such as per API request, per user, or per video stream—so teams can tell whether a code change actually makes their software greener.

Originating from the Green Software Foundation and adopted as ISO/IEC 21031:2024, the standard measures carbon intensity (a rate per functional unit) rather than only raw total emissions. That means a service handling twice as much traffic with slightly more energy can still improve its SCI score, and teams can lower SCI in three ways: using less energy (energy efficiency), running workloads when or where grid electricity is cleaner (carbon awareness), or extending hardware lifetimes (hardware efficiency). ISO/IEC 21031 explicitly excludes carbon offsets from the score so teams cannot buy credits to mask inefficient code.

Why does ISO/IEC 21031 report carbon per functional unit (such as per API call) instead of just total server electricity?

A growing application naturally uses more total energy as users increase. Measuring carbon per functional unit isolates software efficiency from traffic volume, allowing fair comparisons across releases and workloads.

ISO/IEC 21031:2024 specifies SCI = ((E × I) + M) / R, where E is energy consumed within the software boundary (in kWh), I is location-based marginal grid carbon intensity (gCO2eq/kWh, deliberately favoring marginal over market-based/REC accounting), M is amortized embodied emissions of the underlying hardware (TE × (TR / EL) × (RR / TR_total) across time and resource share), and R is the chosen functional unit (such as requests, ML inferences, or active users). Because SCI focuses on actionable engineering decisions rather than corporate GHG Protocol Scope 1–3 inventory totals, boundary selection (including or excluding client devices and networks) must be disclosed alongside R for scores to remain comparable. The standard sets no threshold, pass/fail level or ecolabel: it is a measurement and disclosure method, so a published SCI score is a claim by the publisher rather than certification of a product.
