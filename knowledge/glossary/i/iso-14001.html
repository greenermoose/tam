Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with I / ISO 14001 (Environmental Management Systems)

ISO 14001 is the international standard that sets requirements for an environmental management system, the organised way an organisation manages its environmental responsibilities.

The standard is about how an organisation works, not about a score for one item it sells. A company can hold a certificate for its management system without any individual product carrying an environmental label.

ISO 14001:2026 was published on 15 April 2026, with transition from 2015 certificates expected by about 2029. ISO itself does not certify organisations; independent third parties do. It is not a product label.

A third-party certificate says the audited management system conforms to the standard edition named on the certificate, which is why the transition date matters. For product-level claims, look instead to labels such as EPEAT or ENERGY STAR, or to a method such as life cycle assessment. An LCA is a study method, not an environmental management system.
