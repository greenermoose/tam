Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with I / Incunabulum

An incunabulum is a European book printed in the earliest decades of movable type, up to the end of 1500.

The first books printed in Europe look like a mix of old and new: the letters come from movable type, but the pages still imitate handwritten manuscripts. Books from that early period are called incunabula.

Gutenberg's press work in Mainz in the 1450s and 1460s inaugurated the period, and printing spread across Europe in the following decades. Incunabula are studied for their typefaces, layout, watermarks and provenance, which help identify the printer and date. They are rare and often kept in special collections.

The cutoff of 31 December 1500 is a bibliographic convention, not a technological turning point. Many incunabula are folios with gothic textura type, rubricated initials and no title page; printer's marks and colophons supply identification. Catalogues assign stable identifiers, and digitized copies preserve both the text and physical features such as woodcut decoration.
