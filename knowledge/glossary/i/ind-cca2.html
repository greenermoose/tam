IND-CCA2 (Indistinguishability under Adaptive Chosen-Ciphertext Attack)

The standard security benchmark for encryption and key-encapsulation schemes, ensuring an attacker cannot learn anything about encrypted messages even when allowed to tamper with ciphertexts.

Imagine sending locked boxes through a courier who is secretly working for an eavesdropper. Even if the eavesdropper swaps labels, dents boxes, or sends fake boxes to see how the receiver reacts, IND-CCA2 guarantees the attacker still learns nothing about what is inside your real locked box.

Modern public-key encryption and post-quantum key encapsulation mechanisms (KEMs) such as RSA-OAEP, ECIES, and ML-KEM (via the Fujisaki–Okamoto transform) target IND-CCA2 security. Older schemes without it—like textbook RSA or unauthenticated CBC padding—were broken in practice by padding-oracle attacks, where servers leaked plaintext bytes just by returning “invalid padding” errors.

Why is passive eavesdropping protection (IND-CPA) not enough when a server decrypts untrusted network messages?

Because an active attacker can modify or craft ciphertexts and watch whether decryption succeeds or fails, using that feedback as a decryption oracle to recover secrets—a threat only IND-CCA2 rules out.

In the IND-CCA2 game, a probabilistic polynomial-time (PPT) adversary submits two equal-length plaintexts (m0, m1) and receives a challenge ciphertext c* encrypting mb, with adaptive access to a decryption oracle D_k(·) both before and after seeing c*, restricted only from querying c* itself. IND-CCA2 is equivalent to non-malleability (NM-CCA2); in symmetric cryptography, Encrypt-then-MAC (AEAD) combining IND-CPA and INT-CTXT achieves IND-CCA2, whereas MAC-then-Encrypt can fail if verification leaks timing or padding side channels.
