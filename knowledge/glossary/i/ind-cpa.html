IND-CPA (Indistinguishability under Chosen-Plaintext Attack)

A baseline encryption security property ensuring an eavesdropper who can choose messages to be encrypted still cannot tell which of two plaintexts a ciphertext hides.

Suppose an eavesdropper watches you send scrambled messages and can even trick you into encrypting specific words of their choice to study the output. IND-CPA means that if you encrypt either “yes” or “no”, the eavesdropper still cannot tell which one you sent—not even by noticing that identical messages look the same.

Deterministic encryption modes such as AES-ECB fail IND-CPA because encrypting the same plaintext block twice produces identical ciphertext, leaking patterns. Achieving IND-CPA requires randomized encryption using a fresh initialization vector (IV) or unique nonce (as in AES-CTR or AES-CBC with an unpredictable IV) for every message.

Why does AES in ECB mode fail IND-CPA even though AES itself is a strong block cipher?

Because ECB is deterministic: encrypting the same plaintext twice produces identical ciphertexts, allowing an attacker to distinguish a repeated message from a different one.

Formally, a PPT adversary with an encryption oracle E_k(·) chooses two equal-length plaintexts (m0, m1), receives c* = E_k(mb), and must guess b with advantage negligible over 1/2. Because IND-CPA grants no decryption oracle, it does not prevent ciphertext malleability (for example, flipping bits in AES-CTR ciphertext flips the corresponding plaintext bits predictably); defending against active attackers requires pairing IND-CPA with INT-CTXT or using an IND-CCA2 scheme.
