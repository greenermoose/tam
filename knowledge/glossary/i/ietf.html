IETF (Internet Engineering Task Force)

Open international standards organization that develops and maintains core internet protocols.

The IETF is an open community of engineers, researchers, and network operators who write the shared technical rules—such as IP, TCP, DNS, TLS, and HTTP—that let computers everywhere talk to each other. Nobody owns it, and anyone can read its documents or join the discussion.

Unlike member-only or paywalled standards bodies, anyone can join IETF mailing lists and working groups without fees. Proposals start as Internet-Drafts, undergo open technical review and rough consensus (“running code” matters as much as theory), and are published as RFCs. Not every RFC is a binding standard—some are informational or experimental. See The IETF and how RFCs work.

Does getting published as an RFC automatically make a document an official internet standard?

No. The RFC series includes Standards Track documents alongside Informational, Experimental, Historic, and Best Current Practice (BCP) memos; only Standards Track RFCs (Proposed Standard and Internet Standard) define formal internet protocol standards.

Standards-developing body organized into technical Areas overseen by the IESG under the IETF Administration LLC and IAB, publishing Standards Track, BCP, Informational, and Experimental documents in the RFC series (BCP 9 / RFC 2026 and RFC 9280). Working-group chairs judge rough consensus by whether technical objections are addressed rather than by headcount voting—a trade-off that preserves interoperability and open participation while risking multi-year timelines or contention when large vendors dominate mailing-list volume. See The IETF and how RFCs work.
