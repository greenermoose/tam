VPN - Virtual Private Network

A VPN (Virtual Private Network) creates an encrypted network tunnel over a public or untrusted network so distant devices can communicate as if they were on the same private network.

Imagine sending a letter inside a locked steel box through the regular mail. Anyone handling the mail can see the outside address on the box, but nobody can read the letter inside. A VPN does the same thing for your internet traffic: it wraps your data in encryption so people on a shared Wi-Fi network cannot snoop on it, and it can connect your laptop safely to a home or office network from anywhere.

On Linux, VPNs are commonly managed with NetworkManager or systemd-networkd using protocols such as WireGuard, OpenVPN, or IPsec. Depending on routing configuration, a VPN can act as a split tunnel—sending only traffic for a private subnet (like 10.8.0.0/24) through the tunnel—or a full tunnel that replaces the default route so all internet traffic exits via the VPN gateway, which requires careful MTU and DNS handling to avoid leaks or stalled connections.

Why can browsing fail or stall on certain websites over a VPN even when ping works?

Encapsulating packets in VPN headers shrinks the effective payload size (MTU). If Path MTU Discovery is blocked by an intermediate firewall that drops ICMP “Fragmentation Needed” messages, small ping packets succeed while larger TLS or HTTP packets silently drop until the interface MTU or TCP MSS clamping is lowered.

A Layer-3 VPN (such as WireGuard or IPsec tunnel mode) creates a virtual network interface (for example, wg0 or tun0) that encapsulates inner IP packets inside outer UDP/ESP datagrams after authenticated encryption (ChaCha20-Poly1305 in WireGuard; AES-GCM or ChaCha20-Poly1305 in IPsec/OpenVPN). On Linux, routing loops are prevented via separate routing tables, policy routing rules (ip rule/fwmark), or SO_MARK socket marks. Common failure modes include asymmetric routing, DNS leaks outside the tunnel namespace, and NAT-traversal keepalive timeouts; WireGuard avoids stateful handshake renegotiation storms via Noise_IKpsk2 cryptokey routing and stateless cookie replies under load.
