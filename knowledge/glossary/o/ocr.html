Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with O / OCR (Optical Character Recognition)

Software that recognizes text inside images, scans, or screenshots and converts it into selectable, editable characters.

If someone sends you a screenshot of an error message or a photo of a printed page, you cannot highlight or copy the words because the computer only sees colored dots (pixels). OCR looks at the shapes of those dots, matches them to letters and numbers, and turns the picture into real text you can copy, paste, and search.

Tamlinux screenshot capture integrates OCR (via tesseract) so you can grab text directly from any region of the screen. On the command line, tesseract image.png stdout prints recognized text to standard output, while ocrmypdf adds an invisible, searchable text layer to scanned PDFs. Recognition accuracy drops sharply on low-resolution screenshots, inverted dark-mode themes, or stylized fonts—scaling up small UI text and ensuring high contrast before running OCR noticeably improves results.

Why does OCR sometimes misread text from small or dark-themed UI screenshots?

Most OCR engines are trained on dark text on a light background at ~300 DPI. Low-DPI screen fonts have few pixels per glyph, and light-on-dark themes or subpixel antialiasing can confuse binarization unless the image is upscaled and inverted first.

Modern OCR pipelines (such as Tesseract 4/5) proceed through adaptive binarization (e.g., Otsu or Sauvola thresholding), connected-component and layout segmentation (detecting columns, baselines, and text lines), and sequence recognition using an LSTM neural network trained on line images rather than isolated character templates. Failure modes include mixed-script lines without matching --oem/-l language models, rotated or skewed scans without deskew preprocessing, and tabular layouts where column reading order breaks unless Page Segmentation Mode (--psm) is tuned.
