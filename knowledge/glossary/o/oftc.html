OFTC (Open and Free Technology Community)

OFTC (Open and Free Technology Community) is a non-profit IRC network that hosts public chat channels for Debian, Linux kernel subsystems, QEMU, LLVM, and other free-software projects.

Before modern web chat apps existed, open-source developers talked in real time using IRC—a simple, lightweight text chat system organized into topic rooms called channels. OFTC is one of the main non-profit IRC networks where volunteers and users gather to ask questions and coordinate work on major projects like Debian. Because it is run by a non-profit rather than an ad-supported company, projects do not have to worry about commercial lock-in or sudden paywalls.

On Linux, you typically connect to OFTC over TLS on irc.oftc.net (port 6697) using a terminal client such as weechat, irssi, or senpai, or via a bouncer. Unlike Libera.Chat—another major free-software IRC network—OFTC is an associated project of Software in the Public Interest (SPI) and is the primary IRC home for Debian, QEMU, and several kernel and compiler communities. Registering your nickname with NickServ and authenticating via TLS client certificate (CertFP) or SASL helps protect your identity and lets you join restricted channels.

Why might a Linux contributor use both OFTC and Libera.Chat?

Free-software projects choose their own IRC network: Debian, QEMU, and several kernel channels live on OFTC, while many other upstream projects host their rooms on Libera.Chat. Using an IRC client or bouncer lets you stay connected to both at once.

OFTC operates an oftc-hybrid IRC daemon network under Software in the Public Interest (SPI) governance, offering IPv4/IPv6 dual-stack and Tor hidden-service endpoints. Authentication relies on NickServ with X.509 certificate fingerprints (CertFP) and SASL EXTERNAL, alongside cloak/vhost issuance to mask contributor IP addresses in channel joins. Operational considerations include IRCv3 capability negotiation (such as message-tags and server-time), flood/DDoS rate-limiting on unauthenticated connections, and split-horizon identity or bridge-lag issues when Matrix or webchat bridges relay traffic into irc.oftc.net.
