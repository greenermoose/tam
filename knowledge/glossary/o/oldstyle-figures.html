Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with O / Oldstyle figures

Oldstyle figures are numerals whose heights and shapes follow lowercase letters rather than capitals.

Most numbers today line up like capital letters. Oldstyle digits instead look like small letters: some sit on the baseline, others dip below it, and 1 and 2 may have small tails. They blend into running text instead of standing out.

Oldstyle figures are enabled with the OpenType onum feature, while lining figures use lnum. They suit body text, historical styles and prose; lining figures suit tables, headings and interfaces where digits need to align. Many fonts provide both sets in one file.

Oldstyle digits have mixed x-height and descender placement, often with differing advance widths, so they do not align in columns unless tnum is also applied. The tags onum, lnum, pnum and tnum select shape and width independently, and feature order depends on the application. Fonts may supply small-capital variants through smcp and c2sc.
