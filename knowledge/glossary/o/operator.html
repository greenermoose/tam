Operator (vi)

A vi command that performs an action (such as delete, change, or yank) over the range of text selected by a following motion.

In vi, an operator is an action verb—such as delete (d), copy/yank (y), or change (c)—that waits for you to name where to apply it.

Doubling an operator applies it to the whole current line (for example, dd deletes a line and yy copies a line); following it with a motion applies it across that motion's span.

Why does pressing d once in vi command mode appear to do nothing at first?

Because d is an operator waiting for a motion (like w or $) or a second d to tell it how much text to delete.

In vi's operator-pending mode, the editor awaits a motion or text object, computes the start/end buffer coordinates, saves the affected text to the unnamed or named register, and mutates the buffer atomically.
