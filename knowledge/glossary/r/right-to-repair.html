Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with R / Right to repair (repair access law and practice)

Right to repair is the idea that people who own a product should be able to have it repaired, with reasonable access to repair services and parts.

There is no single global right-to-repair law. Coverage depends on where you live and what kind of product you own, so a claim that a device "has a right to repair" needs a place and a rule behind it.

EU Directive (EU) 2024/1799 was adopted on 13 June 2024 and applies from 31 July 2026. It covers repair of technically repairable goods outside the legal guarantee, requires repair within a reasonable time and at a reasonable price, introduces a repair notice, and requires at least one national repair incentive. EU Ecodesign spare-parts rules and national measures complement it; in the United States there is a patchwork of state laws.

The EU measure works alongside ecodesign requirements rather than replacing them, and its notice and incentive provisions are obligations on the repair system and member states, not a warranty on any one product. EU Batteries Regulation removability rules address a related but distinct design question. In practice, repair access also depends on diagnostics and parts handling described in diagnosing and repairing.
