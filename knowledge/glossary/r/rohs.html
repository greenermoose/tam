Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with R / RoHS (Restriction of Hazardous Substances)

RoHS (Restriction of Hazardous Substances) is the European Union rule that limits hazardous substances in nearly all electrical and electronic equipment sold in the EU.

The letters stand for Restriction of Hazardous Substances. The rule keeps substances such as lead and mercury below set levels in everyday electronics, so it is about what a device is made of rather than how much power it uses or how it is collected at the end of its life; those are covered by EU Ecodesign and WEEE.

The current measure is EU Directive 2011/65/EU, the recast directive. Directive (EU) 2015/863 added four phthalates and is sometimes called RoHS 3 informally. Limits are maximum concentrations by weight in each homogeneous material, not an average across the whole product: lead, mercury, hexavalent chromium, polybrominated biphenyls (PBB), polybrominated diphenyl ethers (PBDE), DEHP, BBP, DBP and DIBP are each limited to 0.1%, and cadmium to 0.01%. Conformity assessment and CE marking declare that a product meets the rules.

A homogeneous material is a part that cannot be mechanically separated into different materials, so a cable sheath, solder joint or plastic housing is assessed on its own. The exemption annexes change often, which makes the exemption status part of a conformity check. RoHS does not ban all brominated flame retardants: only the listed PBB and PBDE families are restricted, while other formulations may still be used. Chemical information duties for articles also exist under REACH, with a different trigger and purpose.
