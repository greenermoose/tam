RTT (Round-Trip Time)

The elapsed time for a network signal or packet to travel from a sender to a destination and for the reply to return.

Round-trip time is how long it takes a message to cross the network to another computer and come back—measured in milliseconds.

Protocols like TLS 1.3 and QUIC are engineered to minimize the number of round trips required during connection setup because each extra RTT adds noticeable delay on long-distance links.

Why did TLS 1.3 reduce its handshake from 2-RTT to 1-RTT?

Cutting a round trip halves the network wait time before encrypted data can start flowing, noticeably speeding up connections on high-latency links.

RTT combines propagation delay, transmission/serialization delay, and queuing delay across hops; TCP congestion control (e.g., BBR, CUBIC) continuously estimates smoothed RTT (SRTT) and RTT variance to size windows and retransmission timers.
