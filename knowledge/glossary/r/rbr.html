RBR (Reduced Bit Rate)

RBR (Reduced Bit Rate) is the baseline DisplayPort link speed of 1.62 Gbps per lane.

Think of a DisplayPort cable as a multi-lane highway carrying video from your graphics card to your monitor. RBR is the slowest speed limit on that highway. You usually do not want it for a high-resolution gaming screen, but it is a reliable fallback speed so an older monitor or a long, noisy cable can still show a picture instead of a black screen.

In DisplayPort link rates, RBR sits below HBR, HBR2, and HBR3. During DisplayPort link training, the GPU and monitor negotiate the highest rate the cable can carry cleanly; if signal integrity is poor, they step down to RBR. Four lanes at RBR carry enough bandwidth for 1080p at 60 Hz, but not 4K at 60 Hz.

Why might a high-resolution monitor drop to a lower resolution or refresh rate when using a cheap or damaged cable?

If link training detects too many errors at higher speeds, DisplayPort falls back to a lower link rate such as RBR. Because RBR carries less data per second, the display driver must reduce resolution, refresh rate, or color depth to fit within the available bandwidth.

Introduced in DisplayPort 1.0, RBR runs at a 1.62 Gbps symbol rate per lane using ANSI 8b/10b line coding (20% overhead), yielding 1.296 Gbps of usable payload per lane—or 5.184 Gbps across 4 lanes before framing overhead. With 24-bit RGB (8 bpc) and CVT-RB blanking, 4-lane RBR supports 1920×1080 at 60 Hz (~3.2 Gbps) or 2560×1440 at 60 Hz (~5.0 Gbps, tight margin), whereas 3840×2160 at 60 Hz (~12.5 Gbps) requires HBR2 or DSC.
