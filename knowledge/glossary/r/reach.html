Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with R / REACH (Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals)

REACH (Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals) is the European Union's chemicals regulation, administered by the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA).

The name describes the three main jobs of the regulation: chemicals are registered, some need authorisation, and some are restricted. For an electronic product the practical effect is often a question passed up the supply chain: does this article contain a substance on the Candidate List?

REACH is Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006. Its Candidate List of substances of very high concern (SVHC) creates supply-chain information duties when such a substance is present above 0.1% weight by weight in an article. That duty is to communicate information; it is not a ban on the article. The Candidate List held 253 SVHCs on 4 February 2026.

The 0.1% w/w trigger is measured per article, so a component can trigger a question even when the finished device as a whole contains only a trace. Listing on the Candidate List does not itself prohibit use, although other REACH processes can lead to authorisation requirements or restrictions. This differs from RoHS, which sets fixed concentration limits for a short list of substances in electrical and electronic equipment. Conflict minerals rules are another separate, disclosure-based regime.
