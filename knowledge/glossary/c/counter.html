Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with C / Counter

A counter is the empty space partly or fully surrounded by a letter's strokes.

Look at the hole inside o, e or 8. Those white spaces are counters. They matter as much as the ink around them: when counters are too tight, letters blur into blobs at small sizes, while open counters make a face feel airy.

Type designers distinguish closed counters, as in o and b, from open ones, as in e and c. Their size and shape strongly affect legibility and the perceived weight of a font. Fonts intended for screens or low vision often open counters wider, and bold weights must keep them from closing up.

Counters are not stored as objects in a font: they emerge from contour winding direction, because the rasterizer's non-zero winding or even-odd fill rule leaves the interior unfilled. Hinting can move bowl contours to preserve counter width at small pixel sizes, and optical-size axes often widen counters at display sizes. An enclosed counter becomes a hole; an open counter is a concave region of the outer contour.
