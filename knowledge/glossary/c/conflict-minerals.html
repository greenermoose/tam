Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with C / Conflict minerals (tin, tantalum, tungsten and gold due diligence)

Conflict minerals are tin, tantalum, tungsten and gold whose mining or trade may fund conflict, especially in and around the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

These four metals are sometimes grouped as 3TG. They are small parts of a device but can have a large effect on the places they come from, so rules ask companies to trace and report rather than simply trust a supplier's word.

Dodd-Frank section 1502 led to the SEC's 2012 Form SD and Conflict Minerals Report for issuers using tin, tantalum, tungsten or gold from the DRC or adjoining countries. The OECD Due Diligence Guidance, third edition (2016), sets out the due-diligence approach, and EU Regulation 2017/821 applies to importers.

These are disclosure and due-diligence duties: they require inquiry and reporting, not a ban on sourcing from a region, and they are not a product label. TCO Certified includes responsible-minerals criteria as a separate product-certification route. The metals themselves are covered in general mineral references, not only in conflict-specific rules.
