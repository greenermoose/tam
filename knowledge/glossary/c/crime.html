CRIME (Compression Ratio Info-leak Made Easy)

CRIME (Compression Ratio Info-leak Made Easy) is a 2012 security attack that exploited TLS-layer data compression to recover secret session cookies from encrypted web traffic.

Imagine someone mailing sealed envelopes, and an eavesdropper cannot read what is inside—but the sender always vacuum-packs the envelope before sealing it. If the eavesdropper slips a guessed word into the envelope and notices the package suddenly shrink, they know that word matched something already inside. CRIME worked the same way on encrypted web connections: when browsers compressed requests before encrypting them, attackers could guess secret login cookies one character at a time just by watching packet sizes.

Encryption hides the contents of a TLS packet, but it does not hide the exact length of the data. When TLS-level compression was enabled, repeated text—such as a guessed cookie prefix matching the real Cookie: header—compressed into fewer bytes, producing a smaller encrypted packet on the wire. Modern browsers, OpenSSL defaults, and TLS 1.3 disable TLS-layer compression to prevent CRIME, while the related BREACH attack targeted HTTP-layer (gzip) compression in responses.

Why didn’t TLS encryption prevent the CRIME attack?

Stream and padded block ciphers in TLS protect data confidentiality, not exact message length. Because DEFLATE compression ran before encryption, matching guesses shrank the plaintext and visibly reduced the ciphertext size.

CRIME (CVE-2012-4929) is a chosen-plaintext side-channel against TLS record-layer DEFLATE compression (RFC 3749) and SPDY header compression. By injecting attacker-controlled substrings into cross-origin requests and observing ciphertext lengths passively, an adversary turns LZ77 back-references and Huffman coding into a byte-by-byte oracle for high-entropy tokens; even CBC block padding is bypassed via two-tries-aligning techniques. Mitigation in TLS 1.2 requires disabling compression (SSL_OP_NO_COMPRESSION), and TLS 1.3 (RFC 8446) removes record-layer compression from the specification entirely.
