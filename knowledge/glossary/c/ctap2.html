CTAP2 (Client to Authenticator Protocol 2)

A FIDO2 protocol that lets a computer or browser communicate with a hardware security key or passkey authenticator.

When you plug in a USB security key or tap one against your phone to log in without a password, your device and the key need a shared language to talk to each other. CTAP2 is that language. It lets your computer ask the key to prove your identity while keeping your private cryptographic secret safely locked inside the hardware key.

CTAP2 works alongside WebAuthn: WebAuthn is the browser-to-website API, while CTAP2 is the local protocol between your computer and the authenticator. On Linux, browsers, libfido2, and OpenSSH (ed25519-sk) use CTAP2 over USB HID, NFC, or Bluetooth to handle PIN checks, touch confirmation, and resident passkeys stored on the key.

How does CTAP2 differ from WebAuthn during a passkey login?

WebAuthn carries the login challenge between the website and your browser, whereas CTAP2 carries that request from your browser or OS to the physical security key. Separating them lets browsers and command-line tools like SSH share the same hardware key without each website needing to speak USB or NFC directly.

A FIDO Alliance CBOR-encoded (RFC 8949) application protocol layered over USB HID (CTAPHID), NFC (ISO 7816-4 APDUs), or BLE GATT. Core commands include authenticatorMakeCredential (0x01), authenticatorGetAssertion (0x02), authenticatorGetInfo (0x04), and authenticatorClientPIN (0x06). PIN/UV Auth Protocol v1 and v2 establish a shared secret via ECDH (P-256) and HKDF-SHA-256 to protect PINs and bind pinUvAuthParam. Trade-offs and edge cases include limited flash slots for discoverable (resident) credentials, strict rpIdHash binding that prevents phishing, and the distinction between user presence (up) and user verification (uv) flags in authData.
