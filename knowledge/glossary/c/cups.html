CUPS (Common Unix Printing System)

The standard modular printing service on Linux and Unix-like systems that manages print queues, protocols, and drivers.

When you click Print in an app, the app does not need to know how to speak every printer’s private language. CUPS works like a mailroom and translator in the background: it takes your document, lines up print jobs in a queue so they do not collide, translates them into a format your printer understands, and sends them over USB or Wi-Fi.

On Linux, CUPS runs as the cups.service background daemon, offers a local web dashboard at http://localhost:631, and provides commands like lp and lpstat. Modern printers typically use driverless IPP Everywhere (discovered via mDNS/Avahi), letting CUPS print without installing vendor-specific driver packages.

How does driverless printing in CUPS work without vendor driver files?

CUPS queries the printer over IPP (Internet Printing Protocol) to learn its supported capabilities—such as paper sizes, duplexing, and standard raster formats like PWG Raster or PDF—and formats the job directly without needing a model-specific PPD or binary driver.

The OpenPrinting cupsd daemon implements IPP/2.0+ (RFC 8010/8011) over HTTP/HTTPS on TCP port 631 and /run/cups/cups.sock. Incoming jobs are spooled in /var/spool/cups and routed through a cost-weighted MIME filter pipeline (cups-filters, e.g., pdftopdf followed by pdftoraster or gstoraster) to emit PWG Raster, Apple URF, or PCLm before a backend (usb, ipp, ipps, socket) delivers the stream. Key nuances include sandboxed filter execution under an unprivileged user, mDNS/DNS-SD discovery failing across segmented VLANs, and the transition from legacy PPD files to IPP Printer Applications (PAPPL).
