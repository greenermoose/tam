CTA-861 (DTV Video Timings)

CTA-861 is the industry standard that defines uncompressed digital video timings, audio formats, InfoFrames, and EDID extension blocks used over HDMI and DisplayPort.

When you plug a monitor or TV into your computer, the two devices need a quick handshake so the computer knows which resolutions, refresh rates, and features like HDR or audio the screen supports. CTA-861 is the shared catalog they use for that conversation. Instead of spelling out every timing detail from scratch, a TV can simply say “I support format #16,” and both devices know that means 1080p at 60 Hz.

CTA-861 (formerly CEA-861) works alongside VESA EDID. Standard PC monitors often list basic modes in the base 128-byte EDID block, while TVs, HDMI monitors, and HDR displays add a CTA-861 extension block listing Video Identification Codes (VICs), audio codecs (PCM, Dolby, DTS), colorimetry, and HDR metadata. On Linux, edid-decode /sys/class/drm/card*-*/edid reveals these blocks—explaining why a TV defaults to overscan or limited RGB range (16–235), or why a malformed extension block can hide HDMI audio or HDR options.

Why might an HDMI display look washed out (gray blacks) on Linux compared to a DisplayPort monitor?

CTA-861 TV timings often default to limited RGB quantization range (16–235) rather than full range (0–255). If the GPU outputs limited range while the monitor expects full range, blacks appear dark gray.

ANSI/CTA-861 (editions G/H/I) standardizes Video Identification Codes (VICs 1–219+, covering 480p through 8K120 and 21:9 modes), Data Blocks inside the 128-byte CTA Extension Version 3 (0x02 EDID tag), and AVI, Audio, and DRM InfoFrames transmitted during blanking over TMDS/FRL (HDMI) or Secondary-Data Packets (DisplayPort). Key interoperability fields include AVI InfoFrame quantization-range bits (Q/YQ for 0–255 vs. 16–235), YCbCr 4:2:0 capability maps for high-bandwidth 4K/8K modes, and the HDR Static Metadata Data Block (SMPTE ST 2084 PQ EOTF, MaxCLL/MaxFALL), which Linux drm_edid.c parses into Colorspace, HDR_OUTPUT_METADATA, and Broadcast RGB KMS properties.
