PRIME (Linux GPU Buffer Sharing)

Linux DRM/DMA-BUF mechanism for sharing rendered frames between an iGPU and dGPU.

Many laptops have two graphics chips: a power-saving chip wired to the built-in screen, and a faster gaming chip that is not directly connected to it. PRIME is the behind-the-scenes bridge in Linux that lets the fast chip draw the picture and hand it to the power-saving chip to display.

Using DMA-BUF file descriptors, PRIME lets you offload an individual app to the discrete GPU (for example with DRI_PRIME=1) while the integrated GPU drives the display. Unlike a hardware display MUX, PRIME keeps the discrete GPU powered down when idle, saving battery at the cost of a small frame-transfer overhead.

Why can PRIME render offload have slightly higher latency or lower FPS than a hardware display MUX?

With PRIME offload, frames rendered on the discrete GPU must be transferred over PCIe into memory the integrated GPU can scan out, adding synchronization and bandwidth overhead that a direct hardware MUX connection avoids.

Implemented in the Linux DRM subsystem via drm_gem_prime_handle_to_fd and drm_gem_prime_fd_to_handle over dma_buf, PRIME enables cross-device buffer sharing without CPU copies. Zero-copy scanout requires mutually supported DRM format modifiers; if the display engine cannot scan out the dGPU’s tiled buffer directly, Mesa or the compositor falls back to an extra blit into a linear or compatible buffer. Cross-GPU synchronization relies on dma_fence / explicit sync, where PCIe runtime power management (RTD3) wakeups can introduce frame-pacing stalls.
