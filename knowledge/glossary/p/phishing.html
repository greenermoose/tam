Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with P / Phishing

Phishing is a fraudulent message that tries to trick a person into giving away information or taking a dangerous action.

A phishing email pretends to come from a bank, a colleague or a delivery service. It pushes you to click a link or reply quickly. The message is a trick aimed at the person reading it.

Checks include looking at the real link destination, treating unexpected requests for secrets as suspicious, and confirming through a separate trusted channel. Domain authentication can help receivers, but a valid DKIM or DMARC result only shows authorised domain use, not honest intent.

Phishing combines spoofing, social engineering and often a lookalike site. Anti-phishing controls include DMARC, browser and mail-client warnings, FIDO2 origin-bound credentials and user reporting. Attackers adapt, so controls must be layered.
