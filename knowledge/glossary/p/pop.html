Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with P / POP (Post Office Protocol)

POP is a mail retrieval protocol that lets a client download messages from a server mailbox.

The letters stand for Post Office Protocol. The version in use is POP3. Think of it as collecting letters from a post office box and taking them home, rather than reading them while they stay on the shelf.

A POP3 client authenticates, lists messages, retrieves them and usually deletes them from the server. The optional UIDL command gives stable message identifiers so a client can avoid downloading the same mail twice. Many clients offer a "leave on server" setting.

RFC 1939 defines POP3. Its normal model is download-and-delete, and it has little support for server-side folders, flags or synchronization across devices. RFC 5598 notes that individual-message POP retrieval is now rare compared with IMAP or webmail. POP still suits a single device that keeps its own archive.
