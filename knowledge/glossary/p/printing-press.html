Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with P / Printing press

A printing press is a machine that transfers ink from a prepared surface onto paper under pressure.

Hand printing means pressing paper onto an inked surface one sheet at a time. A printing press does the same job with rollers and a screw or lever, so it can produce many copies much faster and more evenly.

Gutenberg's mid-fifteenth-century press adapted the screw press used for wine and oil to movable metal type, making books far cheaper in Europe. Earlier presses in Asia printed from woodblocks and sometimes movable type. The press spread literacy, standardized texts and created a typographic trade.

Press design controls inking, impression pressure and registration: type is locked into a forme, inked with balls or rollers, and pressed against sheets laid on a tympan. Relief printing transfers ink from raised glyphs; later lithographic presses use flat plates and the repulsion of oil and water. Press mechanics shaped point, pica and type-height conventions.
