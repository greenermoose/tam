PAM-4 (4-level Pulse-Amplitude Modulation)

PAM-4 is a four-level electrical signaling method that packs two bits of data (00, 01, 10, or 11) into every signal symbol.

If PAM-2 is like a light switch with only on and off, PAM-4 is like a dimmer with four distinct brightness steps. Because each step stands for a two-bit pair instead of a single bit, a wire can carry twice as much data at the same switching speed. The catch is that the four steps sit much closer together, so the receiver has to be far more careful not to confuse neighboring levels.

Part of PAM modulation, PAM-4 is used by PCIe 6.0, 400G/800G Ethernet, and GDDR6X/GDDR7 memory links. Packing 2 bits per symbol lets these buses double throughput without doubling the symbol rate, avoiding extreme high-frequency cable and trace losses. Because the vertical gap between voltage levels shrinks to one-third of binary signaling, PAM-4 links rely on Forward Error Correction (FEC) to fix occasional bit misreads.

How does PAM-4 double bandwidth without doubling the wire’s switching frequency?

Each voltage pulse represents one of four levels (encoding 00, 01, 10, or 11), carrying 2 bits per symbol instead of 1. The symbol rate stays the same, so the signal’s fundamental frequency does not double.

PAM-4 maps pairs of bits onto four equally spaced baseband levels (2 bits/UI), halving the Nyquist frequency for a given bit rate at the cost of three stacked eyes—each one-third the height of an NRZ eye, yielding a ~9.54 dB SNR penalty. To keep bit-error rates acceptable, PHYs pair Gray coding (so adjacent-level slicer errors flip only 1 bit) with multi-tap DFE/FFE equalization, tight transmitter linearity (RLM), and low-latency FEC (such as KP4 RS(544,514) or PCIe 6.0’s lightweight FEC with flit-level CRC/retry) to drive post-FEC BER below 10^-12 or 10^-15.
