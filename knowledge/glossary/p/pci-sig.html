PCI-SIG (PCI Special Interest Group)

PCI-SIG (PCI Special Interest Group) is the industry consortium that develops and maintains the PCI and PCI Express (PCIe) hardware standards.

Every time you plug a graphics card or an NVMe solid-state drive into a desktop motherboard, it fits and works because different companies followed the same rulebook. PCI-SIG is the group of hardware and software companies that writes and updates that rulebook so parts from different brands work together reliably.

PCI-SIG publishes the specifications for each PCIe generation (such as PCIe 4.0, 5.0, and 6.0), defining lane speeds, slot shapes, power budgets, and error reporting. On Linux, tools like lspci -vv display link speeds (for example, 16GT/s for PCIe 4.0 or 32GT/s for PCIe 5.0) and capability structures that directly reflect PCI-SIG’s standardized registers. Compliance testing by PCI-SIG ensures cards and motherboards interoperate across vendors.

Why does an industry consortium like PCI-SIG matter to end users buying GPUs or NVMe drives?

Without a shared, vendor-neutral specification and compliance program, motherboards and expansion cards from different manufacturers would not share compatible connectors, electrical signaling, or configuration registers.

PCI-SIG owns the PCI Express Base Specification (protocol, transaction/data-link/physical layers, PAM4/FLIT encoding in PCIe 6.0, L0/L0s/L1 ASPM states, AER, SR-IOV, CXL-adjacent electricals) and the Card Electromechanical (CEM) and M.2 form-factor specifications. Member companies participate in working groups, receive Vendor IDs from the PCI-SIG registry, and validate physical-layer margin and protocol compliance at PCI-SIG Compliance Workshops (Integrators List) before shipping silicon or add-in cards.
