Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with P / PPI (Pixels Per Inch)

Pixels per inch, a measure of physical screen density.

Imagine painting a picture with tiny tiles: the more tiles you pack into one inch, the smoother letters and curves look. PPI counts how many pixels fit across one inch of a screen, which is why high-density displays look crisp up close.

Physical density depends on both resolution and screen size: a 1920 by 1080 panel is roughly 157 PPI at 14 inches but about 82 PPI at 27 inches. Without scaling, higher PPI makes interface text physically smaller, so desktops increase a scale factor rather than the font size alone.

If two monitors both have 1920 by 1080 resolution, why does text look smaller on a 13-inch screen?

The same pixels occupy a smaller physical area, so PPI is higher and each unscaled glyph is physically smaller.

PPI is the ratio of pixel count to linear inch, while logical UI sizing uses reference pixels anchored to visual angle and compositor scaling. Fractional scaling often renders clients at a higher buffer scale and downsamples, trading bandwidth and slight softness for consistent logical sizes. Font rasterizers work from pixels per em, so effective density and scaling determine how hinting and antialiasing appear.
