Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with P / PPI (Pixels Per Inch)

Pixels per inch, a measure of physical screen density.

Imagine painting a picture with tiny tiles: the more tiles you pack into one inch, the smoother letters and curves look to your eye. PPI counts how many tiny dots (pixels) fit across one inch of a screen, which is why high-PPI displays look crisp like printed paper.

Physical pixel density depends on both resolution and screen size: a 1920×1080 panel has about 157 PPI on a 14-inch laptop, versus about 82 PPI on a 27-inch desktop monitor. Without display scaling, higher PPI makes interface text physically smaller.

If two monitors both have 1920×1080 resolution, why does unscaled text look smaller and sharper on a 13-inch laptop than on a 27-inch monitor?

The same number of pixels is packed into a much smaller physical area on the 13-inch screen, giving it a higher PPI. Each pixel—and each unscaled glyph—is physically smaller.

PPI is the physical ratio of pixel count to linear inch along the panel, whereas logical UI sizing relies on CSS reference pixels (anchored to visual angle) and compositor scaling. Under Wayland (wl_output integer scale or wp_fractional_scale_v1), fractional scaling such as 1.5× typically renders clients at 2× and downsamples, trading GPU bandwidth and slight softness for consistent logical sizes; X11 lacks native per-output scaling and relies on Xft.dpi or compositor transforms.
