Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with G / Greylisting

Greylisting asks an unknown sender to try again later before accepting a message.

The first time a stranger knocks, the receiver asks them to come back after a short wait. A real mail server will return; many bulk-mail senders give up. No message is thrown away permanently by the first refusal.

The receiver returns a temporary SMTP failure, often a 4xx reply such as 451, and records the sender address, recipient and time. When the sender retries after the delay, the combination is allowed through.

RFC 6647 documents greylisting and its costs. It delays legitimate mail, especially from servers with long retry intervals, and it is weaker against senders that retry faithfully. It works as one signal among many, not as a universal check.
