Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with G / Grapheme

A grapheme is what a reader thinks of as one character, which may be made of several stored code points.

When you see an accented letter such as é you think of one character. A computer might store it as a letter plus a separate accent mark. The single thing you see is a grapheme; distinguishing it from stored numbers prevents strange editing behavior.

Grapheme boundaries matter for cursor movement, selection and deleting text: backspace should remove the whole accented letter or emoji, not just part of it. Unicode's UAX #29 rules define grapheme clusters from code-point sequences; emoji with skin tones and family sequences are also single units for most readers.

A grapheme is a linguistic unit; a grapheme cluster is the Unicode approximation, defined by extended cluster rules that join base characters with combining marks, ZWJ sequences, variation selectors and regional indicators. HarfBuzz shapes a cluster into one or more glyphs, and one glyph can represent several clusters, as with a ligature. Editors should move by cluster rather than by code point or byte.
