Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with E / Enumeration (hardware)

The startup process where the operating system detects a newly plugged-in device, reads its IDs, and loads the matching driver.

Enumeration is the handshake that happens when you plug in a USB drive or keyboard: the computer notices something arrived, asks what model it is, and loads the right driver.

When a plugged-in device does not work, checking kernel logs (dmesg or journalctl -k) shows whether enumeration succeeded or failed due to a bad cable or missing power.

If a USB device never appears in kernel logs at all when plugged in, what does that suggest?

Hardware enumeration never even started—pointing first to a power, port, or charge-only cable problem rather than a software setting.

During USB/PCIe enumeration, the host controller assigns bus addresses, reads device/configuration descriptors (VID/PID/class), binds a matching kernel driver via modalias, and triggers udev to create /dev nodes.
