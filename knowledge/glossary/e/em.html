Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with E / em square

The em square is the notional square a font designer uses as the reference for all letter sizes and spacing.

Imagine every letter designed inside the same imaginary square. How big the capital letters or the lowercase x are inside that square is a design choice. This shared square is why the same point size does not make every font look equally large.

Designers divide the em into units per em, commonly 1000 in OpenType or 2048 in TrueType. Metrics such as advance width, ascender and stem weight are integer counts of those units, later scaled to pixels by the rasterizer. CSS uses em as a length equal to the current font size, so 1.5em means one and a half times that size.

In SFNT fonts head.unitsPerEm is a power of two for TrueType outlines and typically 1000 for CFF fonts; the same number scales hmtx, hhea and outline coordinates. TrueType glyf contours are integers in font units, converted by FreeType to fixed-point pixel coordinates using pixels per em and hinting. Optical-size axes change how much of the em a glyph occupies, so equal point sizes can render at different cap heights.
