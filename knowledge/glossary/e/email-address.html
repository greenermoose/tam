Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with E / Email address

An email address names a recipient by pairing a local part with a domain.

An address such as alex@example.com has two parts: the name before the @ sign and the domain after it. The domain tells the sender which mail system to ask about; the local part identifies the mailbox there.

The text after @ is used for DNS lookup, especially MX records. The text before @ is interpreted by the destination system, so a dot or plus sign can have local meaning. That is why case and character rules must not be guessed by the sender.

RFC 5322 defines the addr-spec syntax for the message header, while RFC 5321 defines the envelope address syntax used by SMTP. They are related but not identical. SMTPUTF8 extends both to support internationalized local parts and domains.
