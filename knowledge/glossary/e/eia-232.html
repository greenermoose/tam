Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with E / EIA-232 (RS-232)

EIA-232 (later TIA-232, and widely known as RS-232) is the formal Electronic Industries Alliance standard for single-ended asynchronous serial communication.

EIA-232 is the official standard name behind the classic “RS-232” serial port. Even though most people and cable labels still say RS-232, hardware manuals often use EIA-232 for the same plug signals used to talk to network switches, lab instruments, and headless Linux consoles.

See RS-232 for voltage levels and serial console usage. In Linux, EIA-232 ports appear as /dev/ttyS* or /dev/ttyUSB* and are accessed with tools like screen or minicom; unlike 3.3 V or 5 V microcontroller UARTs, EIA-232 uses higher bipolar voltages and requires a level-shifter chip (such as a MAX232) before connecting to GPIO pins.

Why can’t you wire an EIA-232 port directly to a 3.3 V microcontroller UART pin?

EIA-232 uses bipolar voltages up to ±15 V with inverted logic, which will damage 3.3 V logic pins and misread bits without a level-shifting transceiver.

EIA/TIA-232-F (1997) defines single-ended bipolar signaling where a mark (logic 1) is −3 V to −15 V and a space (logic 0) is +3 V to +15 V, leaving −3 V to +3 V as an undefined transition zone. Slew-rate limits (max 30 V/µs) and a ~2500 pF total capacitive load ceiling bound cable length and baud rate (typically ≤115.2 kbps over short runs), alongside DTE/DCE pinouts and hardware flow control (RTS/CTS, DTR/DSR).
