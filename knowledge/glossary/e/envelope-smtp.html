Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with E / Envelope (SMTP)

The SMTP envelope is the routing information a client gives a server before sending the message itself.

Think of the envelope as the outside of a paper letter: it says where the letter goes and who to contact if it cannot be delivered. The message inside may show a different "from" name.

The envelope sender comes from MAIL FROM and each recipient comes from RCPT TO. These commands are separate from the From: and To: header fields. The final delivery agent records the envelope sender in a Return-Path header.

RFC 5321 section 4.1 defines the envelope commands, and section 4.4 defines bounce handling. SPF checks the envelope-related domain, while DMARC connects that result to the visible author domain through alignment. Forwarding can change the envelope sender, which is why SRS exists.
