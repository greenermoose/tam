Exponent

An exponent tells you how many times to multiply a base by itself, or where a decimal or binary point belongs.

In 2^3, the 3 is the exponent. It means 2 times 2 times 2, which is 8. In scientific notation, an exponent also tells you how large or small a written number is.

The value 1.5 times 10^6 means 1.5 multiplied by a million, or 1,500,000. A negative exponent means division: 1.5 times 10^-3 is 0.0015. In floating point, the significand holds the digits and the exponent sets their scale, so a single format covers very large and very small numbers.

What does an exponent of -2 mean in decimal scientific notation?

Divide by 100, shifting the point two places left. 7.5 times 10^-2 is 0.075. Negative exponents make small values, not negative ones; the sign is a separate part of the number.

IEEE 754 stores a biased exponent: an unsigned field whose value is offset by a fixed bias so that both positive and negative mathematical exponents can use an ordinary unsigned comparison. For binary32 the bias is 127, giving usable normal exponents from -126 to 127. All-zero and all-one exponent fields are reserved for subnormal values, infinity and NaN. Exponent range, not significand width, sets the largest and smallest magnitudes.
