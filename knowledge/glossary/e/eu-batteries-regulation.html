Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with E / EU Batteries Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/1542)

The EU Batteries Regulation is a European rule for batteries placed on the EU market, covering design, labelling, collection and supply-chain duties.

One visible effect is that a portable battery inside an appliance must be easy for the end user to take out and replace. That matters both for repairing a device and for what happens to the battery when the device is discarded.

Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 entered into force on 18 August 2023 and mainly applies from 18 February 2025. From 18 February 2027, portable batteries in appliances must be readily removable and replaceable by end users, with derogations adopted on 14 July 2026. A battery passport for electric vehicle, LMT and industrial batteries above 2 kWh is also required from 18 February 2027. Due diligence obligations were postponed to 18 August 2027 by Regulation (EU) 2025/1561.

LMT means light means of transport, such as e-bikes and scooters. The 2 kWh threshold applies to industrial batteries for the passport requirement, while the removability rule is about portable batteries in appliances; they are distinct obligations with a shared start date. Derogations are exceptions to removability, not a repeal of it. The regulation complements right to repair and sits beside WEEE for end-of-life handling.
