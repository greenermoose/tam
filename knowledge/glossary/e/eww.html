EWW (Elkowar's Wacky Widgets)

EWW (Elkowar’s Wacky Widgets) is a Rust-based widget system configured in a Lisp-like language called Yuck to build custom bars, popups, and overlays on Wayland and X11.

Most desktops come with a fixed panel that you can only tweak a little. EWW lets Linux users design their own status bars, clocks, and volume popups from scratch—like building custom dashboard gauges for your screen.

Often paired with tiling Wayland compositors like Hyprland or Sway, EWW runs as a background daemon (eww daemon) that renders GTK widgets styled with CSS/SCSS. Unlike prebuilt bars such as Waybar that expose fixed modules in JSON, EWW gives you full control over the widget tree in .yuck files fed by shell scripts or JSON streams.

How does EWW keep widgets like battery level or active workspaces up to date?

EWW binds widgets to reactive variables: defpoll runs a command on a timer, while deflisten reads continuous output lines from a long-running script, automatically re-rendering only the widgets that depend on those variables.

A Rust daemon built on GTK3 (gtk-rs) and gtk-layer-shell on Wayland (anchoring windows via wlr-layer-shell-unstable-v1 with configurable layer and exclusive zones) or EWMH struts/override-redirect on X11. Yuck S-expressions compile into a reactive widget graph where defvar, defpoll, and deflisten updates mutate GTK properties in place, and SCSS is compiled via grass with hot reload. Trade-offs: short-interval defpoll spawns subprocesses frequently, whereas deflisten on an IPC socket avoids polling wakeups; relying on wlr-layer-shell also excludes Wayland compositors without that protocol, such as GNOME Mutter.
