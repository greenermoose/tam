Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with E / E2EE (End-to-End Encryption)

E2EE (End-to-End Encryption): protection that only the communicating endpoints can undo, so relays and stores cannot read content.

E2EE means only the devices at the ends of a conversation can read the words. A service that stores or forwards the message cannot read it.

People compare a safety number or code to check they share the right key. A changed code is a prompt to verify out of band, not automatic proof of an attack.

E2EE does not automatically hide metadata such as who contacted whom and when. Backups, linked devices and unlocked endpoints can also expose content. RFC 9420 and the Signal Protocol are two concrete protocol families.
