Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with E / EGL (Embedded-System Graphics Library)

EGL (Embedded-System Graphics Library) is a Khronos Group interface that connects rendering APIs such as OpenGL and OpenGL ES to native windowing systems and display buffers.

EGL acts like a translator between a 3D drawing engine and your screen manager. An app knows how to draw shapes and colors, while Wayland or Linux’s display layer knows how to place windows on your monitor—EGL sits in between so finished frames can be handed off smoothly without slow copying.

On modern Linux desktops, Wayland compositors (such as Hyprland) and toolkits like Qt/Quickshell use EGL over wl_egl_window or GBM instead of X11’s legacy GLX. You can inspect available EGL platforms and extensions with eglinfo, and GPU buffers are shared between apps and the compositor via zero-copy DMA-BUF.

Why do Wayland compositors and clients use EGL rather than GLX?

GLX is tied to the X11 protocol and X server, whereas EGL is window-system-agnostic and works directly with Wayland surfaces and kernel DRM/GBM buffers.

The Khronos EGL 1.5 specification manages EGLDisplay, EGLConfig, EGLSurface, and EGLContext objects, using eglGetPlatformDisplay() to bind Wayland clients (EGL_PLATFORM_WAYLAND_KHR), DRM/KMS compositors (EGL_PLATFORM_GBM_KHR), or headless renderers (EGL_PLATFORM_SURFACELESS_MESA). Zero-copy buffer sharing relies on EGLImageKHR and EGL_EXT_image_dma_buf_import with explicit DRM fourcc formats and modifiers, synchronized via EGL_KHR_fence_sync or EGL_ANDROID_native_fence_sync (sync_file file descriptors).
