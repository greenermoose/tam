Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with E / ENERGY STAR (US EPA product certification)

ENERGY STAR is a voluntary product certification from the US Environmental Protection Agency for products that meet an efficiency specification in a named category.

A product carries the mark only in categories the programme defines, so it is a claim about energy use, not a general environmental award. The specification for a computer is different from the one for a display or a server.

Products are certified by EPA-recognised certification bodies using EPA-recognised laboratories, against specifications for the category. Current examples include Computers v9.0 (27 October 2025), Displays v8.0, Enterprise Servers v4.0 (12 January 2024) and Data Center Storage v2.1 (19 January 2022). The specifications set power or typical energy consumption (TEC) limits and include power-management requirements.

Software alone cannot be ENERGY STAR certified. A custom software image is part of some computer certifications, but the certified product is the computer configuration, not the image sold on its own. Other labels answer different questions: EPEAT rates broader environmental criteria and EU Ecodesign is a regulatory framework rather than a voluntary US certification.
