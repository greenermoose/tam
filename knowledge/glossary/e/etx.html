ETX (End of Text, 0x03)

ASCII control character 0x03 (End of Text), sent when you press Ctrl+C in a terminal to interrupt a running program.

When a command in your terminal gets stuck or takes too long, pressing Ctrl+C sends an invisible stop signal—ETX—instead of typing a letter. It acts like an emergency brake, asking the running program to quit right away and return you to the prompt.

Part of the C0 control codes, ETX (0x03) is mapped by default to VINTR in the Linux terminal line discipline, which sends SIGINT to the foreground process group. Contrast Ctrl+C (SIGINT, requests interruption) with Ctrl+Z (SIGTSTP, pauses the job in the background) and Ctrl+\ (SIGQUIT, aborts and may dump core).

Why can a program sometimes ignore Ctrl+C or take a moment to exit after you press it?

Ctrl+C triggers SIGINT, which programs are allowed to catch or ignore with a signal handler—for example, to finish writing a file cleanly before exiting. If a program catches or masks SIGINT, it will not stop immediately.

ASCII 0x03 (^C, binary 00000011), originally paired with STX (0x02) to delimit text payloads in serial framing. In POSIX/Linux termios, when ISIG is set in c_lflag, the n_tty line discipline intercepts c_cc[VINTR] (default 0x03) and dispatches SIGINT to the foreground process group (tcgetpgrp), flushing input/output queues unless NOFLSH is set. Clearing ISIG (as in cfmakeraw) passes 0x03 straight through to read(2), allowing editors or SSH clients to treat Ctrl+C as data or forward it to a remote PTY.
