Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with E / en

An en is half the width of an em square, traditionally used for dashes and spacing.

If an em is the width of a font's design square, an en is half of that. The short dash used for ranges, like 1990–2000, is an en dash and is roughly one en wide. It is a convenient way to discuss spacing without naming fixed pixels.

En dashes mark ranges, connections and parenthetical breaks; em dashes mark stronger interruptions. In unit strings such as 10 px/en, the word means half the current font size, not a special character. Publishers use en and em units because they scale automatically with type size.

The en dash is U+2013, the em dash U+2014 and the ordinary hyphen U+002D; they are distinct characters and should not be substituted for one another in finished text. In most fonts the en dash advance is about half the em, but that is a design decision, not a mandated metric. CSS has no native en unit, so authors write 0.5em.
