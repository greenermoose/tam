Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with E / Email reputation

Email reputation is a practical judgment about how likely a sender is to send wanted mail.

A receiver remembers how a sender has behaved, rather like a shopkeeper who notices which customers return goods or cause trouble. Good history makes delivery more likely; complaints and abuse make it harder.

Reputation inputs include complaint rates, bounces, authentication results, blocklist entries, sending volume and local history. It is not one universal number: each receiver weighs signals differently.

Reputation systems are operational, not standardised by a single RFC. They need bounded data retention, appeal paths and protection against poisoning. A passing DMARC result proves authorised use of a domain but carries no promise about content, so it is one input rather than a final verdict.
