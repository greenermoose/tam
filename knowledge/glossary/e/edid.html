EDID - Extended Display Identification Data

EDID (Extended Display Identification Data) is a standardized data format used by monitors to describe their identity and capabilities to a computer.

Think of EDID as a digital business card your monitor sends through the display cable as soon as it is plugged in. It tells the computer the monitor’s brand, model, physical screen size, and which resolutions and refresh rates it can safely display—so you get a sharp picture automatically without typing settings by hand.

EDID is a 128-byte (or 256-byte+ extended) binary structure standardized by VESA that the Linux DRM/KMS subsystem reads during display hotplugging to pick the native resolution and refresh rate. You can inspect the raw data under /sys/class/drm/card*-*/edid with edid-decode, or override a faulty monitor’s EDID via drm.edid_firmware=.

What usually happens if a KVM switch or damaged cable blocks EDID from reaching Linux?

Without EDID, the kernel cannot verify the monitor’s native timing, so it falls back to conservative safe VESA modes such as 1024×768 or 640×480 (or treats the output as disconnected).

A VESA-defined binary descriptor read over the I2C-based Display Data Channel (DDC2B at address 0x50, with E-DDC segment pointer 0x30 for blocks beyond 256 bytes) or DisplayPort I2C-over-AUX. The 128-byte base block encodes manufacturer PnP ID, physical dimensions, gamma, CIE chromaticity coordinates, Established/Standard Timings, four 18-byte Detailed Timing Descriptors, and a modulo-256 checksum; CTA-861 or DisplayID extension blocks add HDMI audio, VRR, and HDR metadata, and corrupted checksums or truncated I2C reads can cause modesetting failures.
