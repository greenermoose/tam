LK201 (DEC Keyboard)

A 1983 Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC) terminal keyboard whose physical layout and key codes shaped the escape sequences still used by modern terminals.

Before modern personal computers, people used dedicated keyboards plugged into text-only screens called terminals. The LK201 was a famous keyboard made in 1983 for the DEC VT220 terminal. Even though that hardware is long gone, today’s terminal apps on Linux still mimic the special signals its keys sent, which is why some keyboard shortcuts and function keys behave the way they do.

When you press keys like F5, F6, or Page Up in a Linux terminal emulator, the terminal does not send a raw key name—it sends a VT-style escape sequence inherited from the LK201/VT220 era. Notice that function-key escape sequences skip numbers 16 and 22 (~-terminated codes jump from 15 to 17 and from 21 to 23) because the LK201’s top row had non-function keys and physical gaps between key groups.

Why do modern Linux terminal emulators still skip numbers 16 and 22 in VT function-key escape sequences?

Modern terminals maintain compatibility with DEC VT220 escape codes, which were numbered sequentially along the LK201’s top row. Positions 16 and 22 were not standard function keys on that 1983 keyboard (they sat at group breaks and special keys), so those sequence numbers were never assigned to F-keys and remain skipped today.

Introduced in 1983 with the VT220, the DEC LK201 arranged 20 function keys (F1–F20) across the top row in four groups separated by physical gaps, with F1–F5 reserved locally on the terminal and F6–F20 transmitting CSI sequences of the form ESC [ <n> ~. Because the firmware mapped key positions sequentially along the top row—where positions 16 and 22 fell on group boundaries/special keys—CSI 17 ~ became F6 and CSI 23 ~ became F11, leaving 16 and 22 unassigned. Modern xterm/terminfo definitions (such as kf6–kf12) preserve these legacy gaps, while F1–F4 on PC keyboards typically emit VT100-era SS3 sequences (ESC O P through ESC O S) instead of LK201 CSI codes.
