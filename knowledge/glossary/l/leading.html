Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with L / Leading

Leading is the spacing added between lines of text, measured from one baseline to the next.

The name comes from strips of lead that printers once placed between lines of metal type. In text today, leading is simply the vertical breathing room between lines. Too little makes lines collide; too much makes a paragraph feel scattered.

Line height is usually a little larger than the font's ascent plus descent. CSS line-height sets the line box height; values around 1.4 to 1.6 times the font size are comfortable for body text. Designers reduce leading for headings and increase it for small text or for readers with dyslexia.

Fonts recommend extra leading in hhea.lineGap and OS/2 sTypoLineGap, while usWinAscent and usWinDescent define a Windows-compatible line box. Browsers compute a normal line height from these values, which vary between fonts and can shift layout when fallback is used. Half the leading is distributed above and below the glyph box.
