Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with L / Large language model (LLM)

A large neural network trained on text to predict and generate language, often used for chat, code, and summarization.

An LLM is an AI model that has learned patterns from lots of text. It can generate responses by predicting likely next tokens.

Most modern LLMs use transformer architectures. They tokenize text, compute vector representations with attention, and are trained with next-token prediction and optimization algorithms such as backpropagation and Adam-family optimizers.

LLM behavior emerges from architecture, scale, data, training objective, and alignment/post-training. Key systems concerns include quadratic attention cost, KV-cache memory, quantization, batching, evaluation limits, and reliability/hallucination risks.
