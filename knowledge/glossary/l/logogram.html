Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with L / Logogram

A logogram is a sign that stands for a whole word or meaningful part of a word, not for a single sound.

A sign that means 'sun' by itself, whatever word your language uses for it, is a logogram. Chinese characters and Egyptian signs often work this way: the sign carries meaning directly rather than spelling a sound.

Most logographic systems also use phonetic signs, so calling a whole script logographic is a simplification. Chinese characters usually combine a meaning hint with a sound hint; cuneiform mixes word signs with syllable signs. A logogram can be read differently in different languages, as Japanese uses Chinese characters with several readings.

Unicode encodes logograms as characters with code points and, for CJK, unifies similar forms across languages using source variants and variation selectors. One logogram can have several pronunciations, so speech synthesis needs language context. Fonts need tens of thousands of glyphs for full coverage and often use TTC collections or fallback for regional forms.
