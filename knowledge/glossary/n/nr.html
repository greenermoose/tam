NR (New Radio / Non-Return)

The 5G New Radio cellular wireless standard (and also an abbreviation for Non-Return in digital signaling).

When people talk about “5G” on a phone or cellular modem, the radio technology connecting the device to the cell tower is called 5G NR (New Radio). It is the successor to 4G LTE, built to carry data faster, handle more devices in crowded places, and respond with lower delay. (In low-level electronics, “NR” can also mean Non-Return signaling, where a signal does not drop back to zero between bits.)

3GPP 5G NR operates across two main frequency ranges: FR1 (sub-6 GHz, similar to 4G bands with better wall penetration and coverage) and FR2 (millimeter wave, 24+ GHz, offering huge bandwidth over short line-of-sight distances). Unlike 4G LTE’s turbo codes, 5G NR uses LDPC (Low-Density Parity-Check) codes for user data and polar codes for control channels. On Linux laptops with WWAN cards, 5G NR modems are managed via ModemManager over MBIM or QMI.

Why does 5G NR use both FR1 and FR2 frequency bands instead of only high-speed mmWave?

FR2 (mmWave) offers very wide channels and high throughput, but high frequencies are easily blocked by walls, trees, and rain. FR1 (sub-6 GHz) travels farther and penetrates buildings better, providing reliable wide-area coverage.

Standardized in 3GPP Release 15+ (TS 38.xxx series), 5G NR replaces LTE’s fixed 15 kHz subcarrier spacing with scalable CP-OFDM numerologies (15 * 2^mu kHz, mu from 0 to 4+ depending on FR1/FR2), enabling shorter slot durations for URLLC and wider bandwidth parts (BWPs) up to 100 MHz in FR1 and 400 MHz in FR2. It supports both Non-Standalone (NSA, anchored to an LTE eNB/EPC) and Standalone (SA, connected to 5G Core) deployments, massive MIMO beamforming, and dynamic TDD slot formats. Separately, in baseband signaling, NR (Non-Return) is shorthand for NRZ-style unipolar/polar coding that holds level across the bit interval.
