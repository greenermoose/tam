Guide / Glossary A-Z / Terms beginning with N / Nyquist criterion

To capture a signal faithfully, take more than two samples per cycle of its highest frequency.

A wave that wiggles too quickly between snapshots can look like a slower wave. Taking at least two snapshots per wiggle is the minimum needed to tell a wave from a slower one. That is the idea behind the Nyquist rule.

Half the sample rate is called the Nyquist frequency. Audio sampled at 48,000 times per second has a Nyquist frequency of 24 kHz. A 20 kHz tone is safe; a 30 kHz tone is not and will alias into a lower frequency. Real converters filter the analog input before sampling, because the rule assumes the signal contains no energy above that limit.

Is exactly two samples per cycle enough?

Only in the ideal mathematical case with perfect timing and known phase. Practical systems sample faster and use a filter with a transition band, so there is room to remove unwanted frequencies.

The sampling theorem states that a band-limited signal with no content at or above B hertz can be reconstructed exactly from samples taken at a rate greater than 2B, using an appropriate interpolation filter. Equality is not sufficient in general because a component exactly at the band edge can be ambiguous or cancel. Oversampling relaxes analog filter design and can reduce quantization noise; decimation must include a digital anti-alias filter before lowering the rate.
