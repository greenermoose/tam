Guide / Glossary A-Z / Terms beginning with N / NaN (not a number)

NaN is a special floating-point marker for a result that has no ordinary numeric value.

Sometimes arithmetic has no sensible answer, such as zero divided by zero. Instead of stopping or inventing a number, floating point can carry a marker that says the result is not a number.

Operations such as 0/0, infinity minus infinity and the square root of a negative number produce NaN in typical floating-point systems. A NaN spreads through later calculations, so one appears in the final result as a warning that something went wrong earlier. It is not equal to itself: a check like x == x is false when x is NaN.

Why test for NaN instead of comparing it with a number?

No numeric comparison with NaN succeeds, including equality with itself. Languages provide isnan() or an equivalent check that inspects the special pattern rather than comparing values.

IEEE 754 reserves exponent fields of all ones and a nonzero significand for NaNs. A quiet NaN propagates through operations without raising an invalid exception; a signaling NaN raises invalid when used and may convert to a quiet NaN. Payloads let libraries carry diagnostic bits, though they are not portable across all systems. Comparisons other than ordered ones signal invalid. Sorting or minimising data containing NaNs needs an explicit policy because ordinary comparisons do not order them.
