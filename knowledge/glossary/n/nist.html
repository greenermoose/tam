Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with N / NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology)

The U.S. federal agency that develops measurement standards, technology guidance, and public standards used across computing and security.

NIST is a U.S. government organization that publishes trusted technical rules and guidance. When you see a security standard such as FIPS 197 for AES encryption or SHA-256 hashing, NIST is the agency that published it.

NIST, part of the U.S. Department of Commerce, publishes Federal Information Processing Standards (FIPS) and Special Publications (SP 800 series) that define cryptographic algorithms, security controls, and testing guidance. In Linux and internet security, NIST documents are often the authoritative source for algorithms such as AES, SHA-2, SHA-3, ML-KEM, and ML-DSA.

Is a NIST publication the same thing as a law?

No. NIST publications are technical standards and guidance. Some, such as FIPS, are mandatory for U.S. federal systems, while many others are widely adopted voluntarily because they are clear, public, and well reviewed.

NIST (formerly the National Bureau of Standards, renamed in 1988) develops standards through public processes including Federal Register notices, draft publications, and community review. Its Computer Security Resource Center (CSRC) publishes FIPS and NIST SP 800-series guidance; examples include FIPS 180-4 (SHA-2), FIPS 202 (SHA-3), FIPS 203 (ML-KEM), FIPS 204 (ML-DSA), and FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA). NIST standards are public and generally reusable, but cryptographic module validation for regulated use is handled separately through CMVP under FIPS 140.
