Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with A / ACID

ACID is a shorthand for the four safety properties people expect from a reliable database transaction.

It is a four-part promise: the whole change happens or none does, rules stay true, overlapping work does not interfere, and a finished change survives a crash.

A bank transfer is the usual example: money must not vanish or appear twice, even if two transfers run together or power fails.

Real systems implement ACID with write-ahead logs, locks or multiversion concurrency control, and configurable durability. Distributed systems may relax isolation or consistency for availability.
