Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with A / ACID (Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability)

ACID (Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability) names the four safety properties people expect from a reliable database transaction. A stands for Atomicity, C for Consistency, I for Isolation and D for Durability.

A – Atomicity: the whole change happens or none of it does. C – Consistency: the stored data still follows the rules. I – Isolation: overlapping changes do not interfere with each other. D – Durability: a finished change survives a crash or power loss.

A bank transfer shows the difference. Atomicity means both balances change together, or neither does. Consistency means money is not created or lost and rules such as "no negative balance" still hold. Isolation means two transfers running at once leave the same result as running them one after the other. Durability means a confirmed transfer is still there after the machine restarts.

Database engines implement these properties with different mechanisms: write-ahead logs and commit records for atomicity and durability, constraints and triggers for consistency, and locks or multiversion concurrency control for isolation. Each property also has levels; a system may offer snapshot isolation rather than full serializability, or delay acknowledgement until storage has been flushed. Distributed systems sometimes relax consistency or isolation for availability, so ACID names a set of guarantees rather than a single algorithm.
