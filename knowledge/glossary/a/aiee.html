AIEE (American Institute of Electrical Engineers)

American Institute of Electrical Engineers: a historical US engineering society (1884–1963) that merged with the IRE in 1963 to form the IEEE.

Before the IEEE existed as the organization behind standards like Wi-Fi and Ethernet, electrical engineers and radio engineers belonged to two separate groups. The AIEE was the older society focused on electrical power, wiring, and wired communications. In 1963, it merged with the radio-engineering society to form today’s IEEE.

You will see AIEE cited in foundational computing papers, early character-encoding history, and pre-1963 standards prefaces. Understanding AIEE’s 1963 merger with the Institute of Radio Engineers (IRE) explains why modern IEEE standards span both electrical power systems and digital or wireless communications.

Why do modern networking and CPU standards come from the IEEE rather than the AIEE?

In 1963, the AIEE (focused on power and wired communications) merged with the Institute of Radio Engineers (IRE, focused on radio and electronics) to create the IEEE, uniting both fields under a single standards organization.

Founded in 1884 (with early members including Edison, Tesla, and Bell), the American Institute of Electrical Engineers published Transactions of the AIEE and participated in early American Standards Association (now ANSI) committees. Its January 1, 1963 consolidation with the IRE (founded 1912) merged AIEE’s power/industrial groups and Computing Devices Committee with the IRE’s Professional Group on Electronic Computers, establishing the IEEE Computer Society and the standards lineage behind IEEE 802 and IEEE 754.
