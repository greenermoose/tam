Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with A / ARC (Authenticated Received Chain)

ARC carries a record of earlier authentication checks through a forwarding service.

The letters stand for Authenticated Received Chain. When a message passes through a mailing list or forwarder, the original checks may no longer apply. ARC keeps a signed history so the next receiver can see what was found earlier.

An ARC chain uses numbered sets of ARC-Authentication-Results, ARC-Message-Signature and ARC-Seal headers. Each intermediary adds the next set. A receiver may choose to trust the chain when DMARC would otherwise fail after forwarding.

RFC 8617 specifies ARC as Experimental. It preserves claims rather than proving the message is good; each participating hop signs what it observed, and the final receiver decides whether those signatures are trustworthy. ARC does not repair SPF or DKIM by itself.
