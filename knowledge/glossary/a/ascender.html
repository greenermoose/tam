Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with A / Ascender

An ascender is the stroke of a lowercase letter that extends above the normal small-letter height.

Look at the letters b, d, f, h, k, l and t: the tall parts that stick up above letters like a and e are ascenders. They give a line of text its recognizable skyline and help you read words quickly. The tall stroke is part of the letter, not a separate mark.

Ascender height is a font metric: the distance from the baseline to the top of tall lowercase letters. It may differ from cap height, the height of uppercase letters, and it is not the same as the font's full design square. When a line mixes fonts, mismatched ascenders make text look uneven, which is why fallback and line-height calculations matter.

TrueType records the recommended value in hhea.ascent, while OS/2 uses sTypoAscender and usWinAscent; these can disagree, and applications choose one to lay out lines. Actual ink may exceed the declared ascender, especially for accented capitals or mathematical symbols. In variable fonts, vertical metrics can vary with axis coordinates, and line-gap fields add extra space above the ascender.
