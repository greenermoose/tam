Assembly (Tamlinux)

On Tamlinux, an assembly is a recorded bundle of packages and exact source revisions delivered together as a single deployable unit.

Instead of installing software one piece at a time from different places, Tamlinux groups everything that belongs to one release into a single labeled bundle called an assembly. Because every version inside the bundle is written down together, your system always gets a tested, matching set of programs.

In Tamlinux workflows, an assembly records the exact package revisions and build inputs selected from tamlinux-packages so a deployment can be verified and reproduced rather than assembled ad hoc from local files. Software reaches a session only through a recorded assembly—never directly from a source checkout, copied payload, or unrecorded override.

Why does Tamlinux require software to be delivered through a recorded assembly rather than a local checkout?

A recorded assembly pins exact package revisions and inputs so deployments are reproducible, auditable, and easy to roll back, whereas local checkouts or copied files leave unrecorded state.

A Tamlinux assembly is a cryptographic bill of materials pinned from the tamlinux-packages flake, selected by tam-deploy and tied to a Home Manager generation—never a checkout or copied payload. Recording the exact package revisions and build inputs as one immutable bundle guarantees that the deployed closure can be cryptographically verified, reproduced bit-for-bit, and cleanly rolled back via its generation.
