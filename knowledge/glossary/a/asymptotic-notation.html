Asymptotic notation

Mathematical notation, such as Big-O, Omega, and Theta, for describing how a function grows as its input becomes large.

It is a way to describe growth trends without getting stuck on small details. It helps compare algorithms by how they scale.

Big-O gives an upper bound, Omega gives a lower bound, and Theta gives a tight bound when both match. These are used for time and space complexity.

Asymptotic notation characterizes limiting behavior: O(g(n)) upper-bounds, Ω(g(n)) lower-bounds, Θ(g(n)) tight-bounds, while o(g(n)) and ω(g(n)) denote strict dominance. Correct use requires stating the variable tending to infinity and the cost model.
