ACPI - Advanced Configuration and Power Interface

An industry standard that lets a computer’s firmware describe hardware components and hand power and thermal management over to the operating system.

Every laptop has hardware features like battery sensors, sleep buttons, fans, and screen brightness controls that work slightly differently from one model to another. ACPI is the common guidebook that the computer’s built-in firmware hands to the operating system at startup so the OS knows how to put the laptop to sleep, read battery charge, and wake up when you open the lid.

On Linux, the kernel’s ACPI subsystem parses firmware tables at boot to discover non-enumerable platform devices, battery status, thermal zones, and sleep states (such as S0ix modern standby vs. S3 suspend-to-RAM). You can inspect ACPI tables under /sys/firmware/acpi/tables/ and watch power-button or lid events in journalctl or acpi_listen. When a laptop fails to wake from sleep or a function key does nothing, buggy vendor ACPI tables or missing _OSI quirks are often the culprit.

Why does Linux need ACPI tables for motherboard devices when buses like PCIe and USB can already auto-detect what is plugged in?

Many platform components—such as power buttons, laptop lid switches, embedded controllers, and thermal sensors—do not sit on self-describing buses like PCIe or USB, so the firmware must declare their addresses, interrupts, and control methods through ACPI.

ACPI (maintained by the UEFI Forum) exposes static tables (RSDP → XSDT/RSDT → FADT, MADT, MCFG, SSDT) plus a dynamic namespace interpreted by an OS-hosted AML (ACPI Machine Language) virtual machine (ACPICA in Linux). Hardware transitions are mediated via Fixed Hardware registers (PM1a/PM1b) and General Purpose Events (GPEs) routed through SCI interrupts, alongside device methods like _DSM, _STA, and _OSC for OS-firmware capability negotiation. Kernel debugging typically involves inspecting acpidump/iasl disassemblies, acpi_osi= quirks, and mem_sleep_default=deep vs. s2idle power-drain regressions.
