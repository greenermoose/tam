Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with A / Atkinson Hyperlegible

A free font family designed by the Braille Institute to make letters easier to tell apart.

Some letters look almost identical: I and l, O and 0, or 5 and S. Atkinson Hyperlegible reshapes those letters so each has a distinct outline, helping people with low vision read text more confidently. It is a normal text face, not a special symbol font.

The design emphasizes character differentiation rather than decoration, with a large x-height, open counters and distinctive terminal shapes. It ships in regular, bold and italic styles with a broad Latin character set. It suits accessibility-focused themes, signs and documentation, but it is not a monospace font, so it is a poor fit for terminal grids.

Developed with the Braille Institute and released under the SIL Open Font License, the family uses OpenType outlines and custom italic drawings rather than a simple shear. Legibility also depends on size, weight, contrast and spacing, so a legible typeface does not by itself satisfy WCAG contrast requirements. Install it in a user font directory and select it by family name.
