Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with A / AI (Artificial Intelligence)

Artificial Intelligence: software and systems designed to perform tasks associated with human intelligence, such as pattern recognition, reasoning, and language generation.

Traditional software follows exact step-by-step instructions written by a programmer for every case. AI software instead learns patterns from large collections of examples—like photos, text, or audio—and uses those patterns to answer questions, translate languages, or sort information. Because it works by spotting patterns rather than truly understanding the world, it can be very helpful for drafts and searches, yet still make confident-sounding mistakes.

In modern computing, AI most often refers to machine-learning models—especially neural networks and large language models (LLMs)—that infer patterns from data rather than relying on hand-coded rules. On Linux, you may encounter both cloud APIs and local inference runtimes (running quantized models on CPU or GPU via Vulkan, ROCm, or CUDA), trading off privacy and offline availability against RAM/VRAM limits and speed.

Why can an AI language model produce an incorrect answer that sounds completely confident?

Language models generate text by predicting the most probable next token from patterns in their training data, not by checking facts against a verified database. When a topic is ambiguous or rare in training data, the model still outputs fluent, high-probability phrasing.

AI encompasses both symbolic methods (search, constraint solvers, formal planners) and statistical learning (supervised/unsupervised learning, reinforcement learning, transformer architectures). Practical deployment is governed by compute and memory-bandwidth ceilings: autoregressive LLM decoding is typically memory-bandwidth bound as the KV cache grows linearly with context length and batch size, while weight quantization (FP16/BF16 down to INT8/INT4) trades perplexity for VRAM fit. Key failure modes include distribution shift, training-data bias, non-deterministic sampling (temperature, top_p), and prompt-injection surfaces when models invoke tools.
