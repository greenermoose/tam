Meta key

The Meta key is the primary modifier key used to control the Tamlinux desktop and its windows.

On most keyboards, the Meta key is the key marked with a Windows logo or Apple Command (⌘) symbol, usually sitting next to Alt. Just as Shift capitalizes letters and Control runs shortcuts inside an app (like copying text), Meta is reserved for system-wide desktop actions—such as switching workspaces or arranging windows—so desktop shortcuts do not collide with your apps.

Introduced at the Stanford AI Lab in 1970 and adopted by MIT and Emacs, the Meta key sits at the desktop layer above application shortcuts. On Tamlinux, Meta controls the compositor, tiling, and workspaces without conflicting with application-level Control or Alt shortcuts, replacing the vendor name “Windows key” and the Linux X11 convention “Super key.”

Why does the desktop use Meta instead of Ctrl or Alt for window-management shortcuts?

Applications already rely heavily on Ctrl and Alt for their own commands (such as Ctrl+C or Alt+F). Using Meta for the compositor and window manager keeps desktop navigation separate so shortcuts do not clash.

In Linux’s input stack, physical left and right Meta keys emit evdev keycodes KEY_LEFTMETA (125) and KEY_RIGHTMETA (126), which XKB maps to keysyms Super_L / Super_R and virtual modifier Mod4 (mask 0x40). Wayland compositors intercept Meta bindings before forwarding remaining events to clients over wl_keyboard, avoiding the passive-grab conflicts common in X11.
