Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with M / Measurement

Measurement is the act of collecting evidence about how a system behaves under stated conditions. A useful result says what was observed, how it was observed and what the number does not tell you.

A single successful run only shows that something worked once. Measurement asks a repeatable question: how long did it take, how many attempts failed, or how far did a value change? The answer is evidence you can compare, not a general impression.

A measurement names its unit, its conditions and its limits. Measuring algorithm performance shows why a benchmark on one machine and data set cannot prove a theoretical growth rate. A DPI (Dots Per Inch) value says something about printed or sensor density, not directly about perceived sharpness. Without the method, a number is easy to over-read.

Measurements have uncertainty: sampling choices, instrument resolution and the observer's effect on the system all matter. Repeated trials and a stated environment make a result reproducible; an unrepeatable demo is an anecdote. In messaging research, delivery, delay and privacy leaks involve queues, retries and other people's behaviour, so a field measurement needs a defined threat model and a clear boundary between observed data and interpretation.
