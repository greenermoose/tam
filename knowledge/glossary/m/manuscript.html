Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with M / Manuscript

A manuscript is a document produced by hand rather than by a printing press.

Before printing, every copy of a book was written out by a scribe, one page at a time. Such handwritten books are manuscripts. Each copy could differ slightly from the next, which makes them valuable evidence for editors.

Manuscripts were written on papyrus, parchment or paper and could be scrolls or codices. Scribes copied exemplars, and correctors, illustrators and binders contributed to the finished book. Manuscript culture survived for ceremonial and specialized texts long after printing became common.

Palaeography studies handwriting while codicology studies the book as an artifact; script, abbreviation, ruling and quire structure help date and localize a manuscript. Textual criticism compares witnesses to reconstruct a source text, since copyists introduced errors. Digital editions keep transcription separate from physical features such as page breaks and illumination.
