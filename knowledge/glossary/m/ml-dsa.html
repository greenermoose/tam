Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with M / ML-DSA (Module-Lattice-Based Digital Signature Algorithm)

NIST's primary standardized post-quantum digital signature algorithm (FIPS 204), formerly known as CRYSTALS-Dilithium.

ML-DSA is a post-quantum digital signature method that lets software publishers and servers prove a file or message is authentic in a way that resists quantum computers.

Defined in FIPS 204 (ML-DSA-44, ML-DSA-65, ML-DSA-87), ML-DSA produces signatures of a few kilobytes—larger than Ed25519 but small and fast enough for certificates, package signing, and TLS authentication.

What problem does ML-DSA solve that ML-KEM does not?

ML-KEM establishes a shared secret key (confidentiality), whereas ML-DSA creates and verifies digital signatures (authenticity and integrity).

ML-DSA applies the Fiat-Shamir with Aborts paradigm over Module-LWE and Module-SIS hard lattice problems, achieving EUF-CMA security with deterministic or hedged signing.
