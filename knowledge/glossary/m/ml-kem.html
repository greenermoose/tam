Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with M / ML-KEM (Module-Lattice-Based Key-Encapsulation Mechanism)

NIST's standardized post-quantum key encapsulation algorithm (FIPS 203), formerly known as CRYSTALS-Kyber.

ML-KEM is a modern post-quantum key-agreement method designed to stay secure even against future quantum computers, already used alongside classic algorithms in SSH and HTTPS.

Standardized in FIPS 203 in three security tiers (ML-KEM-512, ML-KEM-768, ML-KEM-1024), ML-KEM is commonly paired with X25519 in hybrid key exchanges (such as X25519MLKEM768) to protect against harvest-now, decrypt-later threats.

Why do TLS and SSH often combine ML-KEM with X25519 in a hybrid exchange?

A hybrid exchange stays secure as long as at least one of the two algorithms remains unbroken, protecting against both future quantum attacks and any unexpected flaw in a newer scheme.

ML-KEM bases its security on the Module Learning With Errors (MLWE) problem over the polynomial ring Z_q[X]/(X^256+1) with q=3329, using Number Theoretic Transforms (NTT) for fast encapsulation and IND-CCA2 decapsulation.
