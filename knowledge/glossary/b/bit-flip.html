Bit flip

An unintended change of a single binary digit from 0 to 1 or from 1 to 0 in memory, storage, or a transmission link.

A bit flip is an accidental glitch where a single 0 turns into a 1 (or vice versa) due to electrical noise, a loose cable, or radiation.

Error-detecting codes (like parity and CRC) catch bit flips so corrupted packets or files are rejected; error-correcting codes (like ECC RAM) can fix single-bit flips automatically.

Why can a single unnoticed bit flip be serious in a computer?

Flipping one bit can turn a small number into a huge one, corrupt a file, or change a machine instruction into a crash.

Bit flips arise from electromagnetic interference, signal integrity degradation, flash charge leakage, or cosmic-ray single-event upsets (SEUs); Hamming codes and Reed-Solomon/LDPC codes provide bounded correction.
