Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with B / Blackletter

Blackletter is a family of bold, narrow letter styles with heavy vertical strokes and sharp angles.

The dramatic old-looking script on medieval manuscripts, old newspapers and some certificates is blackletter. Its letters are packed closely and often have pointed tips, which makes them look formal and ornate but harder to read in long paragraphs.

Styles include Textura, Schwabacher and Fraktur; the last was common in German printing for centuries. Gutenberg's 42-line Bible used a textura blackletter, and early movable-type presses imitated manuscript hands. Today the style is used for headlines, logos and academic crests rather than body text.

Blackletter is a script category, not a font format: it uses ordinary Latin code points, with the visual style carried by glyph outlines. OpenType fonts may offer historical alternates through hist and stylistic sets for period-correct letterforms. Dense strokes and narrow counters reduce legibility at small sizes, so designers pair it with a readable text face; a family name does not guarantee a correct regional form.
