Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with B / Bowl

A bowl is the curved, usually enclosed part of a letter that wraps around a hollow space.

The round belly of letters like o, b and p is called a bowl. In some letters it is closed all the way around; in others it attaches to a stem and is only partly enclosed. Bowls give letters their familiar rounded shapes.

Type designers describe bowls together with stems, terminals and counters: the bowl is the curved stroke, while the counter is the white space it encloses. A bowl can be round, as in o, or flattened, as in e. Its weight and tension help define a typeface's character, and consistent bowls across a family are a sign of careful design.

In outline fonts a bowl is not a separate object but a contour made of Bézier segments; a two-storey 'a' has upper and lower bowls, while 'g' may be single- or double-storey. Optical sizing opens bowls slightly at small sizes to keep counters from filling in, and hinting can shift bowl contours to preserve symmetry at low pixel densities. Curved parts of letters such as n are sometimes described as bowls even when they are not enclosed.
