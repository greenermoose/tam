BSD (Berkeley Software Distribution)

A family of open-source Unix operating systems (FreeBSD, OpenBSD, NetBSD, DragonFly BSD) and the permissive software licenses named after them.

When people talk about open-source Unix-like systems, Linux is the most famous, but BSD is an older sibling that grew out of research at UC Berkeley in the 1970s and 1980s. Unlike Linux, where different groups mix and match a kernel with separate user programs, each BSD (like FreeBSD or OpenBSD) is built and released as one complete, coordinated operating system. BSD also gave its name to a simple license that lets anyone reuse the code—even in commercial products—as long as they keep the copyright notice.

In everyday administration, notice two big differences between Linux and BSD: each BSD develops its kernel and core userland tools (like ifconfig, pf, and init) together in one repository, cleanly separated from third-party packages under /usr/local; and each BSD is known for distinct strengths—OpenBSD for security auditing and OpenSSH, FreeBSD for ZFS and jails, and NetBSD for portability. Tools widely used on Linux, such as OpenSSH and tmux, originated in or are maintained by BSD projects.

How does a BSD operating system’s structure differ from a Linux distribution?

A Linux distribution combines the Linux kernel with GNU/userland tools and packages from many upstream projects. A BSD project develops both its kernel and its base userland together as one unified system, keeping add-on packages separate in /usr/local.

Modern BSDs descend from 4.4BSD-Lite (following the USL v. BSDi settlement) rather than AT&T System V. Key architectural distinctions from Linux include a unified base/ports split (base system in /bin, /sbin, /usr/bin; third-party ports/pkg in /usr/local), kqueue/kevent (predating Linux epoll) for scalable event notification, pf/ipfw packet filtering, FreeBSD’s jail and Capsicum capability sandboxing, and OpenBSD’s pledge(2)/unveil(2) syscall and filesystem restrictions. The 2-clause (Simplified/FreeBSD) and 3-clause (Modified) BSD licenses are permissive non-copyleft licenses compatible with GPLv2/v3, unlike the deprecated 4-clause BSD license with its advertising clause.
