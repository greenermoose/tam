Bit Rate

Bit rate is the number of binary bits transferred per unit of time across a communication link or stored per second in a media stream.

Bit rate measures how fast digital information flows through a cable, Wi-Fi signal, or audio/video file, usually written in bits per second (bps). Just as a wider pipe carries more water per second, a higher bit rate lets more data move every second—for example, a gigabit internet link can carry one billion bits per second, and higher-bitrate video looks sharper because fewer details are thrown away.

When reading network or storage numbers on Linux, watch the unit capitalization: b means bits and B means bytes (8 bits), so a 100 Mbps (megabits per second) link transfers at most 12.5 MB/s (megabytes per second) before protocol headers. Bit rate also differs from baud rate (symbol rate): modern modulation schemes pack multiple bits into each signal symbol, so a link’s bit rate can be several times its baud rate. Tools like ethtool report physical link bit rate, while iperf3 or ss measure actual payload throughput after TCP/IP and framing overhead.

Why does a 1 Gbps Ethernet link never deliver a full 125 MB/s of file-copy throughput?

One gigabit per second equals 125 MB/s at the wire level, but Ethernet preambles, inter-frame gaps, MAC/IP/TCP headers, and acknowledgments consume part of that capacity, typically leaving around 115–118 MB/s of usable application payload.

Channel capacity is bounded by the Shannon–Hartley theorem, C = B · log₂(1 + SNR), where B is analog bandwidth in Hz and SNR is the linear signal-to-noise ratio. Physical-layer line coding trades raw bit rate for clock recovery, DC balance, and forward error correction: 1GbE uses 8b/10b (20% overhead on 1000BASE-X) or PAM-5 over four pairs (1000BASE-T), whereas 10GbE and PCIe 3.0+ use 64b/66b or 128b/130b (~1.5–3% overhead). In multimedia encoding, constant bit rate (CBR) simplifies buffer sizing for real-time streams, while variable bit rate (VBR) allocates bits to complex scenes to maximize perceptual quality per average bit.
