Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with B / Baseline

The baseline is the imaginary line that most letters rest on as they are drawn along a line of text.

Look at a row of lowercase letters: their bottoms line up along an invisible line. That is the baseline. Letters with tails, such as g and p, dip below it, while capitals and tall letters rise above it. The baseline gives text a steady footing.

Text layout places glyphs by their origin on the baseline, then uses the font's ascent and descent to compute line boxes. When a line mixes scripts or fonts, renderers align baselines rather than bounding boxes, which keeps text visually level even when glyph heights differ. Superscripts, subscripts and inline images can be offset from the baseline.

TrueType and OpenType define baseline metrics in the hhea and OS/2 tables; vertical writing uses a different baseline and vhea/vmtx metrics. The baseline is also the reference for underline position in post and for combining-mark anchors. Font fallback that mixes faces with different baseline-to-design-square ratios causes visible vertical jitter.
