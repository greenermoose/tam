Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with B / Brominated flame retardant (BFR)

A brominated flame retardant, or BFR, is a bromine-containing chemical added to a material to slow or stop it burning.

BFRs help plastic housings, circuit boards and cable insulation meet fire-safety ratings. Their usefulness in a fire and their persistence in recycling are both part of the reason they are closely regulated.

Some BFRs, notably PBB and PBDE, are restricted under RoHS and the Stockholm Convention, while other formulations remain in use. Not all brominated flame retardants are banned.

RoHS caps PBB and PBDE at 0.1% by weight in homogeneous materials, and the Stockholm Convention addresses certain persistent brominated compounds; those limits do not cover every bromine-based chemistry. Common rigid board material can use brominated epoxy resin, as described under FR-4, so the presence of bromine alone is not proof of a RoHS breach. Contaminated plastics are often downcycled.
