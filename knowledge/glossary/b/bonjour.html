Bonjour

Apple's name for its implementation of zero-configuration networking, used to find printers, shares and other devices on a local network.

Bonjour is what Apple calls the technology that lets a Mac find a printer without being given its address. It is an instance of Zeroconf, not a different protocol. Linux uses a compatible implementation called Avahi, so a device announcing itself with Bonjour can be discovered by Avahi on the same network.

A Bonjour-enabled service publishes a name such as My Printer._ipp._tcp.local. Clients browse a service type, see a list of named instances, and resolve the chosen one to a host, address and port. On Linux the same records are read with avahi-browse. The compatibility comes from shared standards, not from Apple software being installed.

Does finding a Bonjour printer require an Apple computer on the network?

No. The announcements use open standards on the local link. Any compatible implementation, including Avahi, can publish and read them.

Bonjour is the product name for Apple's mDNS responder and DNS-SD implementation. The wire behavior follows RFC 6762 and RFC 6763: queries and responses use UDP 5353 and link-local multicast groups, service instances are described by PTR, SRV and TXT records, and names in .local are resolved without a configured server. Brand names differ; the mechanisms and record formats are shared.
