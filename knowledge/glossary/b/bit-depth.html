Guide / Glossary A-Z / Terms beginning with B / Bit depth

The number of bits used to store one sample or one colour value.

Bit depth decides how many distinct levels are available. One bit gives two levels, like on and off. Eight bits give 256 levels, which is enough for many images but coarse for quiet sound.

With n bits there are 2^n codes. A 16-bit audio sample has 65,536 possible levels; an 8-bit image channel has 256. Higher bit depth makes the quantization step smaller, so faint details are less likely to disappear. It does not change the sample rate or the number of pixels.

Does 24-bit audio always sound better than 16-bit audio?

Not by itself. It has a lower noise floor and more headroom, but the source, converter, microphones and listening conditions often matter more. The extra bits help most during processing and when recording quiet sources.

Effective resolution can be lower than nominal bit depth because of converter noise and non-linearity. Dither can trade coarse correlated distortion for a small, less objectionable noise floor. In images, bit depth per channel combines with channel count: 8 bits per RGB channel gives 24 bits per pixel, while high dynamic range formats may use 16-bit floating point per channel. Storage and bandwidth grow in direct proportion to bit depth.
