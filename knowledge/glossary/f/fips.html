FIPS (Federal Information Processing Standards)

Public cryptographic and computing standards published by NIST, such as FIPS 197 (AES), FIPS 203 (ML-KEM), and FIPS 204 (ML-DSA).

FIPS is the series of official technical standards published by the US National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) that define widely trusted cryptographic algorithms like AES and SHA-256.

When security documentation cites FIPS 197 (AES), FIPS 180-4 (SHA-2), FIPS 202 (SHA-3), or FIPS 203–205 (post-quantum cryptography), it is pointing to the authoritative specification of that algorithm.

What does a citation like FIPS 203 tell a reader?

It names the exact NIST standard defining the algorithm (ML-KEM) so implementations across vendors interoperate.

FIPS publications specify mathematical algorithms, parameter sets, and test vectors (validated via CAVP/CMVP under FIPS 140-3 for regulated cryptographic modules).
