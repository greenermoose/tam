Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with F / Font family

A named group of matching font styles (regular, bold, italic) that share the same visual design.

A font family is a complete set of matching letter styles—regular, bold, italic and their combinations—under one name such as Inter or JetBrains Mono. Choosing a family gives you a coherent look across a document.

Applications select a face from the family as needed: bold for emphasis, italic for quotations. A family may be built from several files or from one variable font with weight and slant axes. Designers compare cap heights and x-heights across families, because equal point sizes do not guarantee an equal visual size.

What is the difference between a font family and a single font file?

A file usually holds one face, such as Bold Italic; the family groups all related faces under one design name.

Fontconfig resolves family names through alias, prefer and accept rules into ordered fallback lists of faces matched by weight, slant, width and Unicode coverage. OpenType name IDs 1, 2 and 16/17 can carry regular, typographic and full family names that disagree. CSS font matching picks the closest face by weight and stretch and may synthesize bolding if no matching face exists.
