Fourier transform

A mathematical transform that represents a signal as a combination of frequencies.

It turns a "how it changes over time" view into a "which frequencies are inside it" view. This helps with sound, images, radio, and many other signals.

For sampled data, the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) maps N samples to N frequency coefficients. Each coefficient measures how much a sine/cosine frequency contributes. The inverse DFT reconstructs the original signal.

The DFT is a linear invertible map onto complex exponentials. Practical use requires attention to sampling (Nyquist/aliasing), windowing/leakage, scaling conventions, and numeric precision. The convolution theorem makes frequency-domain filtering efficient when paired with FFT.
