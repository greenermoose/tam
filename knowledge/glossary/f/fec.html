FEC - Forward Error Correction

FEC (Forward Error Correction) is a technique that adds redundant data to a transmission so the receiver can repair errors without asking for a resend.

Imagine mailing a postcard in the rain where a few letters might smudge. Instead of asking the sender to mail a new card every time, you add a short set of clues at the bottom so the reader can reconstruct the missing letters on their own. FEC does the same thing for digital data.

FEC is used wherever resending lost packets is too slow or impossible—such as live video streams, satellite links, and high-speed fiber connections. Unlike TCP, which relies on acknowledgments and retransmissions to fix packet loss, FEC spends a little extra bandwidth up front to keep latency predictable and avoid round-trip delays.

Why do live audio/video streams and satellite links prefer FEC over simply resending lost packets?

Waiting for a retransmission takes at least one full round-trip, which causes stutters in live media or multi-second delays over long-distance links. FEC lets the receiver repair minor corruption immediately using the redundant data already received.

A channel-coding scheme parameterized as (n, k), encoding k information symbols into n transmitted symbols (code rate R = k/n) with n − k parity symbols. Common families include algebraic block codes (Hamming, Reed–Solomon over GF(2^m) for burst errors), capacity-approaching LDPC and Polar codes, and convolutional/turbo codes (Viterbi/BCJR decoding). Soft-decision decoding using channel log-likelihood ratios (LLRs) yields roughly 2–3 dB more coding gain than hard-decision bit decoding; in 25G/100G/400G Ethernet, RS-FEC (KR4/KP4) is required to hold post-FEC bit-error rates below 10^-12–10^-15 under PAM4 noise.
