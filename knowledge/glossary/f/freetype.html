Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with F / FreeType

FreeType is the library most Linux applications use to load fonts and turn their outlines into pixels.

When a program needs to draw text it often asks FreeType. It reads the font file, finds the right letter and works out which pixels to color, so the characters appear on your screen. It is a library, not a program you normally launch yourself.

FreeType supports TrueType, CFF/Type 1, OpenType, WOFF and bitmap formats, and handles hinting, antialiasing and color glyphs. It does not decide which font to use—Fontconfig does that. It also does not shape complex scripts: HarfBuzz or Pango supplies positioned glyph IDs before FreeType rasterizes them.

The library exposes faces, sizes, slots and glyphs; FT_Load_Glyph applies hinting targets such as FT_LOAD_TARGET_LCD, and FT_Render_Glyph produces a bitmap or outline. Its TrueType interpreter runs glyph bytecode for grid fitting, while the Adobe CFF hinter applies automatic stem snapping and blue-zone alignment. FreeType supplies the raster result; layout engines handle shaping and toolkits composite the glyph bitmaps.
