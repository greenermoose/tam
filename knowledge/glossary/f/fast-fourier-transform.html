Fast Fourier Transform (FFT)

An efficient algorithm for computing the Discrete Fourier Transform, typically in O(N log N) time.

The FFT gives the same frequency result as a direct Fourier transform, but much faster for large inputs. That speed makes real-time audio, image, and radio processing practical.

A direct DFT costs O(N^2) because it compares every sample with every frequency. FFT algorithms such as Cooley-Tukey divide the problem into smaller transforms and reuse intermediate results. See Time complexity and Big-O notation for how these growth rates are measured.

FFT families include radix-2/4, mixed-radix, split-radix, Bluestein, and real/complex variants. Implementation choices affect cache behavior, numerical error, bit-reversal/in-place layout, and vectorization. FFT enables fast convolution, polynomial multiplication, spectral estimation, and many signal-processing pipelines.
