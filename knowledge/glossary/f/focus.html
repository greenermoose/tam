Focus (keyboard)

The desktop state that determines which window or interface element receives the characters and shortcuts you type.

When you have several windows open—say, a web browser and a terminal—only one of them can listen to your keyboard at a time. That active window is the one with “focus.” Clicking inside a window or switching to it with a shortcut moves focus there so your keystrokes go to the right place.

On a Linux desktop, the focused window receives application keystrokes, while the compositor or window manager still intercepts global shortcuts (such as Meta key combinations) before passing remaining keys to the client. Visual cues like a raised window or mouse pointer position do not always match keyboard focus—especially in tiling compositors or with focus-follows-mouse settings—so verify the active indicator before typing sensitive commands or closing a window.

If your mouse pointer is hovering over Window A, can Window B still receive what you type?

Yes. Under click-to-focus (the default on many desktops), hovering does not move keyboard focus, so keystrokes still go to whichever window was last focused until you click or switch focus explicitly.

Keyboard focus is a compositor or display-server input-routing state independent of Z-order stacking, surface visibility, and workspace or output assignment. Under Wayland, wl_keyboard.enter and wl_keyboard.leave events notify only the focused client surface (preventing background clients from sniffing keystrokes, unlike X11 where XSelectInput or XGrabKeyboard allowed broader capture); a surface can hold keyboard focus while partially occluded or during a pointer grab. Within a toolkit (GTK, Qt, or browser DOM), focus further divides into top-level window activation (xdg_activation_v1) and intra-widget focus chains, where modal dialogs or input-method (zwp_text_input_v3) pre-edit states can intercept key events before the application widget sees them.
