Fused multiply-add (FMA)

A single instruction that computes x times y plus z with one final rounding.

Dot products keep doing the same pair of jobs: multiply two numbers, then add the result to a running total. A fused multiply-add does both jobs as one step, which is quicker and keeps more of the answer.

Written separately, the multiplication rounds its product and then the addition rounds again. The fused form keeps a wider intermediate value and rounds once. That often gives a more accurate result and uses fewer instructions. Processors expose it through SIMD instructions, and optimized BLAS kernels use it heavily.

Does FMA always change the final digits of a dot product?

No. If the product is exact or the rounding steps happen to agree, the result is unchanged. When they differ, FMA is usually more accurate but not bit-identical to a separate multiply then add.

IEEE 754-2008 defines the fusedMultiplyAdd operation with a single rounding to the destination format. Languages expose it through functions such as fma() or explicit intrinsics. Compilers may fuse a multiply and add only when the language allows contraction; flags such as FP_CONTRACT control that choice. Reproducibility across compilers or machines therefore needs a stated contraction policy, not just the same source expression.
