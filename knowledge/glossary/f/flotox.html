FLOTOX (FLOating-gate Tunnel OXide)

FLOTOX is a floating-gate memory cell design used in EEPROM devices, where charge is moved through a thin tunnel oxide to store or erase a bit.

Imagine a tiny sealed bucket that can hold an electric charge. Writing puts charge into the bucket; erasing lets it leak back out through a very thin wall. Because the bucket is sealed well enough, the charge stays there when power is off, so the memory remembers its value.

In a FLOTOX cell, a floating gate is electrically isolated by oxide. A special thin tunnel-oxide region allows electrons to pass during program and erase operations when a high electric field is applied. The presence or absence of charge on the floating gate changes the transistor threshold voltage, which the chip reads as a 0 or 1. Compared with flash, traditional EEPROM FLOTOX designs support small, byte-oriented updates, but they use more cell area and are usually found in small configuration memories.

Why can a FLOTOX-based EEPROM keep data without power?

The charge is stored on an electrically isolated floating gate. With no direct conductive path, the charge can remain for years, and the transistor reads differently depending on whether that stored charge is present.

FLOTOX cells use Fowler–Nordheim tunneling through a thin tunnel oxide window to move charge onto or off a polysilicon floating gate. Stored charge shifts the sense transistor threshold, enabling non-volatile state retention. The tunnel oxide endures high-field stress during program/erase cycles, so endurance and data retention are limited by oxide wear, charge trapping, and leakage; practical EEPROM parts specify finite write cycles and retention under defined temperature conditions. Byte-addressable EEPROM arrays trade density for write granularity, unlike NAND flash block erase architectures.
