Watt (W)

The standard unit of electrical power, equal to one joule of energy used per second (volts multiplied by amperes).

A watt measures how fast a computer is drawing electricity at this moment—just like miles per hour measures speed. Lower idle watts mean longer battery life and less heat.

On Tamlinux, tam-meter reports live power draw in watts so you can see immediately whether a background process or bright screen is draining your battery.

What is the difference between watts (W) and watt-hours (Wh)?

Watts measure instantaneous power draw right now; watt-hours measure total energy stored in a battery or consumed over time.

Instantaneous electrical power P = V × I (in watts); integrating P(t) over time yields energy in joules or watt-hours (1 Wh = 3600 J), exposed on Linux via RAPL and /sys/class/power_supply/.
