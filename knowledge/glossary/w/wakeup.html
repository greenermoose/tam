Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with W / Wakeup

An event—such as a timer firing, a network packet arriving, or a key press—that pulls a sleeping CPU core out of a low-power idle state.

A wakeup is any tiny tap on the shoulder that forces a resting processor to wake up and check something—even if there is no real work to do.

Hundreds of unnecessary timer wakeups per second from polling loops keep CPU cores out of deep sleep states and drain laptop batteries much faster.

Why do frequent wakeups increase power draw even when total CPU usage looks close to 0%?

Each wakeup interrupts deep package C-states and powers up clocks and caches briefly, preventing the processor from staying in its lowest-power idle tier.

Kernel timer wheels, hardware IRQs, and userspace poll/epoll timeouts generate wakeups observable via powertop, perf, and /proc/interrupts; event-driven blocking and coalesced timers minimize wakeup rate.
