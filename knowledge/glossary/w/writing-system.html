Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with W / Writing system

A writing system is the full set of conventions that turns a spoken language into visible marks.

A writing system is more than an alphabet: it includes the signs, spelling rules, direction, punctuation and conventions that let readers recover meaning. Latin, Chinese and Japanese each have their own systems.

Writing systems are often grouped by what their signs represent: alphabets use sound units, syllabaries use syllables, abjads mainly write consonants, and logograms stand for words or morphemes. Real systems mix these types, and a language may use more than one script. Reforms can change spelling without changing the spoken language.

Unicode encodes characters, not whole writing systems: direction is supplied by the Bidirectional Algorithm, line breaking by UAX #14 and grapheme boundaries by UAX #29. A system's orthography may define which code points and normalization form are preferred. Rendering complex systems needs fonts with the right sign repertoire plus shaping rules in GSUB/GPOS; a script is only one part of that stack.
