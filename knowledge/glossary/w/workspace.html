Workspace

A workspace is a named or numbered group of windows that you can switch between to keep different tasks separated on the same screen.

Think of a workspace like having several clean desks in one room. You can keep your web browser on Desk 1 and your editor on Desk 2, and switch between desks with a shortcut instead of minimizing windows on one crowded screen.

In Linux window managers and compositors, viewing another workspace is different from moving the focused window to it: one changes what you see, while the other relocates the window without necessarily following it. On multi-monitor setups, also notice whether workspaces are bound to each monitor individually or can be pulled across screens.

If you switch to workspace 2 and a window you wanted stays on workspace 1, which action did you perform?

You changed the active workspace view rather than moving the window; relocating the window requires a “move window to workspace” shortcut or action.

A compositor-managed grouping and visibility abstraction (or _NET_CURRENT_DESKTOP in X11 EWMH) whose output mapping and migration semantics depend on the compositor. In per-output models (such as Sway/i3), each workspace is bound to a wl_output and switching workspaces swaps the visible render tree on that output without altering client xdg-shell state; distinguish focus changes, container/window moves between workspaces, and whole-workspace moves across physical outputs.
