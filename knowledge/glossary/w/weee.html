Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with W / WEEE (Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment)

WEEE (Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment) means discarded electrical and electronic gear, and also the EU directive that makes producers responsible for collecting and treating it.

The letters expand to Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment. When an old computer is taken to a collection point instead of the rubbish bin, that route exists because producers have duties for what happens after a product is thrown away.

EU Directive 2012/19/EU sets producer duties for separate collection, treatment, financing, marking and reporting. Since 2019 the collection target has been 65% of the average weight of electrical and electronic equipment placed on the market in the previous three years, or, as an alternative for member states, 85% of the WEEE generated there. Separate category targets for recovery and for preparing for reuse and recycling also apply. These targets bind member states, not individual products.

The collection rate is a member-state result measured against either a market-placement baseline averaged over three years or the waste actually generated, so it is not a per-product threshold. Category-level recovery and reuse-and-recycling targets sit alongside that headline figure. Producers organise take-back systems and depollution to handle the collected material, while RoHS controls what goes into new equipment rather than what happens to old equipment.
