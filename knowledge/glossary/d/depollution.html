Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with D / Depollution (hazard removal before recycling)

Depollution is the recycling step that removes hazardous parts from electronic equipment before it is shredded.

It is like taking the battery out of an old device before breaking it up. The parts that can leak or release dust are taken out first, so the rest can be handled as material.

Depollution removes batteries, mercury lamps and switches, LCD backlights, CRT tubes, toner and capacitors before shredding. Doing this prevents dust and smelting risks from mercury, lead and brominated flame retardants.

Removing the listed parts before shredding keeps mercury, lead and brominated flame retardants out of shredder dust and furnace feed, which is what makes later hydrometallurgical and pyrometallurgical recovery safer. Plastics that still contain flame retardants can be difficult to recycle into high-value products and are often downcycled.
