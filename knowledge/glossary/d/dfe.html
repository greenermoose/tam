DFE (Decision Feedback Equalizer)

A receiver circuit that removes lingering interference from earlier bits when reading high-speed signals.

When data races down a fast cable at billions of bits per second, each electrical pulse leaves behind a faint “echo” that blurs into the next pulse. A Decision Feedback Equalizer (DFE) is a smart filter at the receiving end that remembers the last few bits it saw and subtracts their echoes, making the next bit crisp and easy to read.

In high-speed links such as PCIe, USB4, and DisplayPort, DFE works alongside linear equalizers like CTLE. Because linear equalizers boost high frequencies, they also amplify high-frequency noise and crosstalk; DFE avoids this by subtracting interference based on already-decided digital bits rather than amplifying the incoming analog signal.

Why does a receiver use DFE in addition to a linear equalizer like CTLE?

CTLE boosts high frequencies to counter cable loss, which also amplifies noise; DFE cancels post-cursor echoes using clean decided bits without boosting noise.

A non-linear, tapped delay-line equalizer that subtracts weighted prior symbol decisions (the sum of h_k * d_{n-k} over taps k = 1..N) from the sampled input before slicing, eliminating post-cursor inter-symbol interference (ISI) without noise enhancement. Its key failure mode is error propagation: a single mis-sliced bit feeds an incorrect subtraction into subsequent taps, and the first tap must settle within a single unit interval (UI).
