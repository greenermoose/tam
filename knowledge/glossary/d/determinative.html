Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with D / Determinative

A determinative is a sign added to a word to indicate what kind of thing it is, without adding a sound.

In some ancient scripts a scribe placed a small sign next to a word to say 'this is a god' or 'this is a place'. The sign guided the reader but was not pronounced. Such signs are called determinatives.

Egyptian hieroglyphs and cuneiform use determinatives to disambiguate words written with consonants or logograms alone. The same sign can classify many different words, and scribes did not always use it consistently. Determinatives help modern readers decide whether a sequence names a person, a city, a deity or an ordinary object.

Determinatives are semantic classifiers rather than phonetic signs: they sit outside the word's sound value and are typically placed at its end. Because they are optional and variable, transcriptions mark them separately from the transliterated word, often with small capitals or dedicated markup. In Unicode they have their own code points, so a font must include those signs even though they contribute no pronunciation.
