Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with D / DOCSIS (Data Over Cable Service Interface Specification)

The telecommunications standard that delivers high-speed internet and data over coaxial cable television networks.

For decades, thick round coaxial cables carried only television channels into homes. DOCSIS is the shared language that lets a cable modem and the cable company’s equipment send internet data over those exact same wires alongside TV signals. Because many homes on the same street share one coaxial line, DOCSIS also coordinates whose turn it is to talk so neighbors’ traffic does not collide.

Unlike point-to-point fiber or Ethernet, DOCSIS runs over a shared hybrid fiber-coax (HFC) medium where the provider’s Cable Modem Termination System (CMTS) schedules upstream transmission slots for all modems on a node. Historically, cable internet had much slower upload than download speeds because most of the cable’s frequency spectrum was reserved for downstream TV and data. DOCSIS 3.1 and 4.0 expand capacity with wider channels and modern error correction, though your Linux router behind a cable modem in bridge mode still sees only standard Ethernet.

Why does cable internet often slow down slightly during evening peak hours compared to dedicated fiber?

Coaxial cable segments are shared among multiple subscribers on the same local node, so the CMTS must divide the available upstream and downstream radio-frequency capacity across everyone active at the same time.

Maintained by CableLabs, DOCSIS defines MAC and PHY layers over HFC RF spectrum. DOCSIS 3.0 bonds multiple 6 MHz (or 8 MHz EuroDOCSIS) single-carrier QAM channels; DOCSIS 3.1 introduces 24–192 MHz OFDM downstream and OFDMA upstream channels (up to 4096-QAM, with optional 16384-QAM) paired with LDPC forward error correction to approach the Shannon limit. DOCSIS 4.0 extends spectrum to 1.8 GHz via either Full Duplex (FDX) echo cancellation or Extended Spectrum (ESD) high-split bands. At the MAC layer, Active Queue Management (PIE) and Low Latency DOCSIS (LLD) Dual-Queue Coupled AQM mitigate bufferbloat under congestion.
