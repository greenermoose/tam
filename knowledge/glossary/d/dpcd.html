Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with D / DPCD (DisplayPort Configuration Data)

A standardized register space inside a DisplayPort monitor that the GPU reads and writes over the AUX channel to configure the video link.

When you plug in a DisplayPort monitor, your graphics card and screen need to agree on how fast they can send video data over that specific cable. DPCD is like a small spec sheet and control panel inside the monitor. Before showing a picture, the graphics card reads that sheet to learn what speeds the monitor supports, then writes back settings until both sides lock onto a reliable connection.

Unlike HDMI’s DDC/I2C bus (which mainly carries static EDID display info), DisplayPort’s bidirectional AUX channel lets Linux DRM drivers (such as amdgpu or i915) actively read and write DPCD registers during link training. Drivers inspect DPCD to discover maximum lane count (1, 2, or 4), link rates (HBR, HBR2, HBR3, UHBR), and optional features like Display Stream Compression (DSC) or Panel Self Refresh (PSR). If a flaky cable causes link training to fail, the kernel logs DPCD/AUX errors and falls back to a lower resolution or refresh rate.

How does DPCD differ from EDID when a DisplayPort monitor is connected?

EDID describes what resolutions, timings, and color capabilities the monitor supports, whereas DPCD exposes the physical DisplayPort link’s capabilities, live link-training status, and control registers over the AUX channel.

Defined by the VESA DisplayPort specification, DPCD is a 20-bit byte-addressed space (0x00000–0xFFFFF) accessed via native AUX transactions. Key ranges include Receiver Capability (0x00000–0x000FF: DPCD revision, MAX_LINK_RATE, MAX_LANE_COUNT, eDP/DSC caps), Link Configuration (0x00100–0x001FF: LINK_BW_SET, LANE_COUNT_SET, TRAINING_PATTERN_SET), and Link/Sink Status (0x00200–0x002FF: Clock Recovery and Channel Equalization flags). During link training, the source iteratively adjusts voltage swing and pre-emphasis in TRAINING_LANE0_SET–LANE3_SET based on ADJUST_REQUEST feedback; short-pulse HPD (Hot Plug Detect) interrupts signal link loss or sink IRQ events.
