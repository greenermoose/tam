Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with D / Direct-to-device

Direct-to-device is a satellite service that talks to an ordinary phone, rather than requiring a separate satellite phone or terminal.

Where a phone has no terrestrial coverage, a satellite can sometimes provide a narrow link to the same handset. The service is usually limited: short messages, emergency contact or low-rate data rather than a full home network. It is an extension of reach, not a replacement for a local cell.

The radio path is different from a terrestrial RAN (Radio Access Network): the satellite is far away, the link budget is tight and the delay is larger. The phone may use a different modem mode and may still rely on a gateway and the carrier's core network for identity and routing. Coverage depends on geometry, antenna orientation and local regulation.

Non-terrestrial network work in 3GPP studies how to adapt access and core procedures to long propagation delays and moving beams. A message that succeeds over a satellite path is not automatically private or instantaneous; the same questions about transport, receipts and encryption still apply. Availability and supported features change quickly, so check the current carrier and handset documentation for a specific service.
