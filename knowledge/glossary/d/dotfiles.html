Dotfiles

Personal configuration files and folders whose names begin with a dot (.) in a user's home directory.

Dotfiles are plain-text settings files stored in your home directory—like .bashrc or folders inside ~/.config—whose names start with a period so ordinary directory listings stay uncluttered.

On Tamlinux, personal overrides live in your dotfiles under ~/.config/ rather than in system directories under /usr/, keeping your customizations safe across upgrades.

Why do dotfiles start with a period character in Unix and Linux?

By convention, filenames starting with a dot are hidden from plain ls output so configuration files do not clutter everyday working directories.

Dotfiles are ordinary user-owned files resolved by shells and XDG-compliant apps; keeping user state in ~/.config separates personal configuration from root-owned cultivar files under /usr.
