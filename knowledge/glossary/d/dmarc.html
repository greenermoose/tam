Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with D / DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance)

DMARC connects SPF and DKIM results to the domain a person sees in the From: header.

The letters stand for Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance. A domain owner publishes a rule that says what a receiver should do when a message claims to be from that domain but fails its checks.

The policy is a TXT record at _dmarc.domain beginning v=DMARC1. A pass requires SPF or DKIM to pass and the authenticated domain to align with the author domain. Common policy tags are p=none, p=quarantine and p=reject; rua requests aggregate reports.

RFC 9989 is the current DMARC specification and obsoletes RFC 7489. Alignment can be strict or relaxed with adkim and aspf. A DMARC pass proves authorised use of a domain, not that the content is safe. Third-party senders often fail alignment even when SPF and DKIM pass separately; ARC can preserve prior results across intermediaries.
