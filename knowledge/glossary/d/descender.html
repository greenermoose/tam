Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with D / Descender

A descender is the stroke of a lowercase letter that extends below the baseline.

Letters such as g, j, p, q and y have tails that hang below the line the other letters sit on. Those tails are descenders. They stop text from looking as though it is floating and help distinguish similar letters.

Descender depth is the distance from the baseline to the lowest point of those letters and is one of the metrics a renderer uses to size a line. It may be shallower or deeper than the ascender. When a font lacks coverage and fallback supplies a glyph from another face, mismatched descenders make a line look uneven.

TrueType stores the recommended value in hhea.descent (usually negative) and OS/2 has sTypoDescender and usWinDescent; these are layout values, not guarantees about actual ink. Some glyphs overshoot the descender line for optical balance, and mathematical symbols can extend much further. Line spacing is computed from ascender, descender and line gap, so inconsistent metrics cause clipping or jumps.
