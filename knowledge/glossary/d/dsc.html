DSC (Display Stream Compression)

VESA’s visually lossless, low-latency compression standard used over DisplayPort and HDMI so high-resolution, high-refresh-rate modes fit within cable bandwidth.

Think of DSC like vacuum-packing clothes into a suitcase so everything fits, then unpacking them instantly at the other end. It shrinks high-resolution video inside modern display cables in real time so a single cable can drive 4K at 144 Hz or 8K without visible blur or lag.

DisplayPort 1.4 and HDMI 2.1 links have finite raw bandwidth; DSC 1.2 compresses the pixel stream at roughly 3:1 (down to around 8–12 bits per pixel) with sub-scanline latency so high-refresh monitors work without visible artifacts. On Linux, DRM/KMS drivers (amdgpu, i915, xe) negotiate DSC automatically via EDID and DPCD capabilities when a mode exceeds the uncompressed link rate, which you can inspect in drm_info or /sys/kernel/debug/dri/.

Why can a monitor still flicker or drop to a lower refresh rate even when both the GPU and display advertise DSC?

DSC only activates if every link in the path—GPU display engine, cable or adapter, MST hub, and monitor controller—supports it and Forward Error Correction (FEC) negotiates cleanly. A passive adapter or marginal cable can force a fallback to uncompressed bandwidth limits.

VESA Display Stream Compression 1.2a partitions each frame into independent horizontal slices (typically 1–4 slices per line, 8–108 lines tall) encoded in a modified YCoCg-R color space. It combines a 3-pixel line-buffer DPCM predictor, Indexed Color History (ICH) for sharp UI/synthetic edges, and delta-size unit variable-length coding (DSU-VLC) at a constant bit rate (CBR) enforced by a rate-control buffer model. Because slices are independent, bit errors stay spatially bounded, though DisplayPort requires Reed–Solomon FEC to keep error rates below visible slice corruption. On multi-monitor DP MST chains or VR headsets, GPU pipe/DSC-engine allocation limits and slice-count constraints can still cap concurrent modes even when total link bandwidth appears sufficient.
