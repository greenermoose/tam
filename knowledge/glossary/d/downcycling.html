Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with D / Downcycling (lower-value material reuse)

Downcycling is reprocessing a material into something less valuable than what it came from.

A plastic casing that becomes a low-grade filling material has been downcycled: the material is used again, but not as the same kind of product.

This differs from closed-loop recycling, where material returns to the same or an equivalent use. Downcycling is common for e-waste plastics contaminated with brominated flame retardants, because the additives limit the uses the recovered plastic can serve.

Downcycling still keeps material out of a waste stream, but it does not preserve the value of the original part. Metals recovered through hydrometallurgy or pyrometallurgy can return to higher-value uses, whereas contaminated plastics often cannot; depollution happens before either route.
