DFS (Dynamic Frequency Selection)

A 5 GHz Wi-Fi mechanism that listens for weather or aviation radar and vacates shared channels when radar is detected.

Some 5 GHz Wi-Fi channels are shared with airport and weather radar. Dynamic Frequency Selection (DFS) is the polite rule that lets Wi-Fi routers borrow those less-crowded channels as long as they listen first and immediately switch away if a radar pulse shows up.

On Linux, regulatory rules for DFS channels (UNII-2 and UNII-2 Extended) depend on your wireless regulatory domain (iw reg get). Before a router or access point can transmit on a DFS channel, it must listen silently for a Channel Availability Check (often 60 seconds, or longer on weather-radar channels), which can delay 5 GHz availability after a reboot or cause brief disconnects if radar triggers a channel switch.

Why can a 5 GHz access point take a minute or more to appear after booting on a DFS channel?

The access point must complete a silent Channel Availability Check to verify no radar is using the frequency before transmitting.

Defined in IEEE 802.11h and regional regulatory domains (FCC/ETSI), DFS mandates an initial Channel Availability Check (CAC, typically 60 s, up to 600 s in ETSI weather-radar sub-bands 5600–5650 MHz) and continuous In-Service Monitoring. Upon radar signature detection, the AP must cease transmissions within the Channel Move Time (10 s), broadcast a Channel Switch Announcement (CSA), and place the channel into a 30-minute Non-Occupancy Period.
