Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with D / dGPU - Discrete Graphics Processing Unit

A separate graphics processor with its own dedicated memory, distinct from the main CPU.

Most laptops and desktops have basic graphics built right into the main processor, which is fine for everyday tasks. A discrete GPU (dGPU) is a separate, dedicated graphics chip—like hiring a specialist artist with their own desk and supplies—so heavy 3D games, video editing, or AI workloads run much faster without crowding the main processor’s memory.

In dual-GPU laptops (hybrid graphics), the integrated GPU drives the display efficiently on battery while the dGPU powers down (runtime D3) until an application requests it via PRIME offload (e.g., DRI_PRIME=1 or vendor offload variables). Tools like lspci and nvidia-smi or radeontop let you verify whether the dGPU is active or suspended.

Why does a laptop with both an iGPU and a dGPU usually run everyday apps on the iGPU?

The integrated GPU draws far less power, letting the dGPU stay powered off until a graphics-heavy workload needs its dedicated VRAM and compute units.

A standalone GPU device on its own die or package with high-bandwidth local VRAM (GDDR6/HBM) and dedicated VRM power delivery, attached over PCIe or another coherent fabric. On Linux DRM/KMS, hybrid setups rely on DMA-BUF PRIME buffer sharing for render offload and RTD3 power management to gate PCIe link and rail power, trading copy and synchronization overhead when presenting dGPU-rendered frames through an iGPU-wired display engine.
