Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with D / Denylist

A denylist names the items that are blocked, while everything not on the list is allowed.

Think of a denylist as a notice at a door that names the people who may not enter. Everyone else gets through unless someone adds them to the notice.

In email, a denylist may hold addresses, domains, IP ranges or servers from a DNSBL. It is the opposite of an allowlist: convenient for known abuse, weaker against new or renamed sources.

A denylist implements default-allow policy (permit(x) ⇔ x ∉ D). It needs timely updates and careful canonicalisation, and it creates false-positive risk when a shared address is listed. Mail systems often combine denylists with authentication and scoring rather than using a single list as the only verdict.
