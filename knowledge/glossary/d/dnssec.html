DNSSEC (Domain Name System Security Extensions)

Cryptographic signatures on DNS records that let resolvers verify answers have not been forged or altered.

Ordinary DNS is like asking someone for a phone number on a postcard—anyone along the way could swap the number for a fake one and send you to an imposter website. DNSSEC adds a tamper-proof digital seal to each DNS answer. Your computer’s resolver can check that seal to be sure the address really came from the domain’s rightful owner, not an attacker trying to hijack your connection.

DNSSEC protects integrity and authenticity, not privacy: queries and responses are still sent in plaintext unless paired with DoT or DoH. In practice, a validating resolver (like systemd-resolved or unbound) checks signatures from the root zone down to the target domain; if a domain’s signatures are broken or expired, the resolver returns SERVFAIL rather than risking a spoofed IP. Validated DNSSEC also unlocks trust in records like SSHFP (verifying SSH host-key fingerprints) and TLSA/DANE (pinning TLS certificates).

Does enabling DNSSEC encrypt your DNS queries so your ISP cannot see which domains you look up?

No. DNSSEC only signs DNS records to prove they are authentic and unaltered; the queries and responses remain unencrypted unless you also use a transport like DNS over TLS (DoT) or DNS over HTTPS (DoH).

Specified in RFC 4033–4035 (with NSEC3 in RFC 5155), DNSSEC builds a chain of trust from a pre-configured root trust anchor (KSK). Each parent zone publishes a DS (Delegation Signer) hash of the child zone’s DNSKEY, while the child signs its RRsets with a Zone Signing Key to produce RRSIG records. Authenticated denial of existence uses signed NSEC or hashed NSEC3 records to prove a name or type does not exist without allowing trivial zone enumeration (for NSEC3). Common failure modes include clock skew breaking RRSIG validity windows, forgotten DS updates during key rollovers causing validation SERVFAILs, and large signed responses triggering UDP fragmentation unless EDNS0 buffer sizes are tuned.
