UV (Ultraviolet / Undervoltage)

UV is an acronym whose meaning depends on context: it refers either to ultraviolet light in optics and sensors, or to undervoltage in power-supply and hardware diagnostics.

Short acronyms often get reused in different parts of computing. In camera, scanner, or biometric topics, UV means ultraviolet light—the invisible light just beyond violet that can cure coatings or affect sensors. In hardware power discussions, UV means undervoltage—when a power rail drops below the voltage a chip needs to run reliably, which can cause freezes or sudden reboots.

Always check the surrounding topic before interpreting UV. In power and firmware logs (such as battery controllers, USB Power Delivery, or embedded boards), UV or UVLO flags a brownout or drooping supply rail—often caused by a weak adapter or thin cable—rather than a software crash. In optics, display coatings, or environmental sensors, UV refers to ultraviolet wavelengths or UV-index readings. Note that in Python tooling, lowercase uv can also refer to the Python package manager, though in hardware and system glossaries UV means ultraviolet or undervoltage.

If a system log reports a UV event right before an unexpected reboot, should you look at optics or power hardware?

Power hardware—in system and board diagnostics, UV indicates an undervoltage condition where a voltage rail dipped below safe operating limits, triggering a brownout or protective reset.

In physical and optical contexts, UV spans the 10–400 nm electromagnetic band (UVA 315–400 nm, UVB 280–315 nm, UVC 100–280 nm), relevant to photolithography, optical sensor filtering, and polymer degradation. In power electronics, UV refers to undervoltage detection and Undervoltage Lockout (UVLO): comparator hysteresis circuits in PMICs and voltage regulators hold a rail or MOSFET gate disabled until V_in exceeds a rising threshold, and assert a fault or reset (PWR_OK deassertion) when V_in drops below a falling threshold to prevent undefined logic states, flash corruption during writes, or excessive R_DS(on) heating in partially enhanced power FETs.
