HPD (Hot Plug Detect)

HPD (Hot Plug Detect) is a physical signal pin on HDMI and DisplayPort cables that lets a monitor notify the graphics card when it connects, disconnects, or needs attention.

Think of HPD as a doorbell wire inside your monitor cable. Instead of your computer constantly guessing whether a screen is attached, the monitor sends a simple electrical signal down that wire whenever it is plugged in, unplugged, or wakes up so your desktop knows to turn the display on or move your windows.

On Linux desktops, HPD changes trigger kernel DRM/KMS udev events on /dev/dri/card*, prompting Wayland compositors or X11 to read the monitor’s EDID and reconfigure outputs. Noisy cables, flaky KVM switches, or aggressive monitor sleep modes can cause rapid HPD glitches—resulting in flickering screens or wl_output churn as the desktop repeatedly tears down and rebuilds the display layout.

What is the difference between a full HPD drop and a short IRQ_HPD pulse on DisplayPort?

A sustained low HPD signals a physical disconnect or power-off, prompting full link teardown. A short 0.5–1 ms low pulse (IRQ_HPD) keeps the link up and tells the GPU to poll DPCD registers over the AUX channel for events like link-training loss or MST topology changes.

HDMI uses pin 19 (pulled toward the source’s +5 V pin 18 through a 1 kΩ sink resistor once EDID is ready), while DisplayPort uses pin 18 (active high, ~2.4–3.6 V). In DisplayPort, a 0.5–1.0 ms low pulse signals IRQ_HPD (triggering AUX CH reads of DPCD 0x00200–0x00205 for lane/symbol lock loss, HDCP, or MST sideband messages), whereas a low state >2 ms indicates unplug/re-plug. Kernel drivers debounce HPD via drm_helper_hpd_irq_event; over USB-C DP Alt Mode, HPD is virtualized inside USB-PD Attention VDMs rather than a dedicated copper pin, which frequently causes dock-resume races and MST link-loss storms.
