Hertz (Hz)

The unit of frequency, meaning one cycle per second.

Hertz tells you how many times something repeats every second. If a monitor has a 60 Hz refresh rate, it redraws the picture 60 times each second. If a CPU runs at 3 GHz, its clock ticks 3 billion times per second.

Common multiples are kilohertz (kHz, 1,000 cycles/s), megahertz (MHz, 1,000,000 cycles/s), and gigahertz (GHz, 1,000,000,000 cycles/s). Hz is used across computing: CPU clock signals, display refresh rates, audio sample rates (44.1 kHz, 48 kHz), and radio frequencies. A higher Hz number does not always mean "faster" in the same way - a 3 GHz CPU is not automatically faster than a 2 GHz CPU if it does less work per cycle.

Frequency is the inverse of period: f = 1/T. In synchronous digital logic, clock frequency sets the upper bound on cycle time, but effective throughput depends on IPC, pipeline depth, memory hierarchy, and thermal/power constraints. In displays, refresh rate is a frame deadline (60 Hz = 16.7 ms); in signal processing, sample rate constrains representable bandwidth by the Nyquist criterion. Care is needed when comparing Hz across domains because the "cycle" being counted differs (clock edge, frame, sample, waveform period).
