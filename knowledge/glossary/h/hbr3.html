HBR3 (High Bit Rate 3)

HBR3 (High Bit Rate 3) is a DisplayPort 1.3 and 1.4 link speed of 8.1 gigabits per second per lane, designed for high-refresh 4K monitors, multi-monitor daisy-chaining, and 8K with compression.

HBR3 raises the DisplayPort lane speed limit again—to three times the original HBR rate. That extra capacity lets a single cable handle ultra-sharp 4K screens at fast gaming refresh rates, or power multiple high-resolution monitors through one dock.

In DisplayPort link rates, 4 lanes of HBR3 provide 25.92 Gbps of usable video bandwidth (32.4 Gbps raw). DisplayPort 1.4 pairs HBR3 with Display Stream Compression (DSC), which lets Linux desktops drive 4K 144 Hz, 5K, or 8K panels over one cable without visible quality loss—provided both the GPU driver and monitor advertise DSC support.

How does HBR3 enable 4K at 144 Hz or 8K at 60 Hz when 25.92 Gbps is not enough for those modes uncompressed?

It pairs HBR3 with Display Stream Compression (DSC), a visually lossless compression standard (typically up to 3:1) supported in DisplayPort 1.4, shrinking the required link bandwidth to fit inside 25.92 Gbps.

Introduced in DisplayPort 1.3 and extended with DSC 1.2 in DisplayPort 1.4, HBR3 operates at 8.10 Gbps/lane using 8b/10b encoding (6.48 Gbps/lane payload, 25.92 Gbps across 4 lanes) and requires Training Pattern Sequence 4 (TPS4) with DFE/CTLE receiver equalization. At 8.10 Gbps/lane, passive copper cable length and connector insertion loss become tight; marginal cables frequently fail TPS4 channel equalization and step down to HBR2. Later DisplayPort 2.0/2.1 UHBR modes (10/13.5/20 Gbps/lane) replace 8b/10b with 128b/132b encoding to cut overhead from 20% to ~3%.
