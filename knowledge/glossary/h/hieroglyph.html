Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with H / Hieroglyph

A hieroglyph is one of the picture-like signs of ancient Egypt's formal writing system.

Egyptian writing used small pictures of birds, people, plants and objects. Some stood for sounds, some for words and others acted as clues to meaning. Carved on temple walls, these signs are called hieroglyphs.

Hieroglyphs combine phonetic signs, logograms and determinatives, and can be written in either direction; the faces of animals and people point toward the start of the line. The Rosetta Stone, with the same text in hieroglyphic, Demotic and Greek, enabled Champollion's decipherment in the 1820s.

The Unicode Egyptian Hieroglyphs block spans U+13000 to U+1342F with additional blocks for extended signs and format controls. A sign can be polyvalent, acting as a one-, two- or three-consonant sign, a logogram or a determinative depending on context. Rendering needs fonts with the repertoire and correct ordering; the Gardiner sign list remains the standard catalogue.
