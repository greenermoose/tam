Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with H / Half Duplex

A two-way communication link where both sides can send and receive data, but only one side can transmit at any given moment.

Think of people talking on walkie-talkies: both people can speak and listen, but they have to take turns. If both press the talk button at the exact same second, their voices collide into static. Half duplex works the same way for devices sharing a wire or radio frequency.

Half duplex contrasts with simplex (one-way only, like a broadcast radio) and full duplex (simultaneous two-way communication on separate send and receive paths, like modern switched Ethernet). You still encounter half duplex in 2-wire RS-485 industrial buses, single-channel Wi-Fi, and misconfigured Ethernet links. If one end of an Ethernet cable is forced to full duplex while the other auto-negotiates to half duplex (a duplex mismatch), the half-duplex side detects late collisions and throughput collapses.

Why does a duplex mismatch between a switch port and a Linux server cause severe packet loss under load?

The full-duplex side transmits whenever it wants without listening for collisions, while the half-duplex side treats simultaneous traffic as a collision, drops frames, and backs off—causing heavy retransmissions as traffic increases.

Half-duplex links implement Time-Division Duplexing (TDD) or shared-medium arbitration (such as CSMA/CD on legacy IEEE 802.3 or CSMA/CA on IEEE 802.11). In wired buses like RS-485, transceivers must toggle between driver-enable and receiver-enable states; the turnaround time (t_turnaround), bias-resistor settling, and UART TX-empty interrupt latency directly cap effective bus utilization, especially for short request-response frames. On Linux, Ethernet link duplex is inspected via ethtool and the kernel’s netif PHY state machine; modern 1000BASE-T and faster PHYs operate exclusively in full duplex in practice, making half-duplex negotiation a legacy fallback.
