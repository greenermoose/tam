Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with H / HBR2 (High Bit Rate 2)

HBR2 (High Bit Rate 2) is a DisplayPort 1.2 link speed of 5.4 gigabits per second per lane, doubling the bandwidth of HBR to support 4K displays at 60 Hz.

If HBR is a classic four-lane highway, HBR2 doubles the speed limit on every lane. That extra room lets a single cable carry much sharper 4K video smoothly at 60 frames per second, or drive more than one standard monitor from a single DisplayPort output.

Among DisplayPort link rates, HBR2 delivers 17.28 Gbps of usable payload across 4 lanes (21.6 Gbps raw). On Linux laptops connected through USB-C DisplayPort Alt Mode, USB 3 data often shares the cable by cutting DisplayPort from 4 lanes down to 2 lanes—so a 2-lane HBR2 link gives only 8.64 Gbps, which can drop a 4K monitor from 60 Hz to 30 Hz.

Why can a 4K monitor be stuck at 30 Hz over a USB-C dock even when both the laptop and monitor support HBR2?

USB-C DisplayPort Alt Mode with simultaneous USB 3.x data typically allocates only 2 of the 4 high-speed lanes to video, halving HBR2 usable bandwidth from 17.28 Gbps to 8.64 Gbps—too little for uncompressed 4K at 60 Hz.

Introduced in DisplayPort 1.2, HBR2 runs at 5.40 Gbps/lane with 8b/10b encoding (4.32 Gbps/lane payload, 17.28 Gbps across 4 lanes) and adds Training Pattern Sequence 3 (TPS3) for stronger receiver equalization during link training. Uncompressed 3840×2160 at 60 Hz 8 bpc RGB (~12.54 Gbps active/blanking depending on CVT-RB timing) fits comfortably in 4-lane HBR2, leaving margin for Multi-Stream Transport (MST) daisy-chaining or 10 bpc HDR at 60 Hz (~15.68 Gbps). Signal-integrity failures or poor-quality passive cables often cause link-training fallback to HBR, which manifests in DRM logs as reduced link-rate fallback.
