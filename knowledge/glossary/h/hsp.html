Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with H / HSP (Hidden Subgroup Problem)

The Hidden Subgroup Problem (HSP) is the algebraic problem generalized by Shor’s algorithm to break RSA, Diffie–Hellman, and ECC on quantum computers.

Imagine a mathematical function that repeats in a hidden pattern, like wallpaper whose repeating tile size you do not know. Regular computers have to test huge numbers of points to spot the repeat, which takes far too long. A quantum computer uses wave interference to make the hidden repeat pop out quickly—and it turns out that breaking today’s RSA and elliptic-curve locks is just finding that hidden repeat, whereas lattice locks do not have that kind of shortcut.

Shor’s factoring and discrete-logarithm algorithms are instances of the abelian HSP, where the group operation is commutative and the Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT) extracts the hidden subgroup in polynomial time. By contrast, post-quantum lattice and code-based schemes (such as ML-KEM and HQC) do not reduce to abelian HSP, and non-abelian HSP (over symmetric or dihedral groups) has resisted efficient general quantum algorithms.

Why does Shor’s algorithm break both RSA and ECC even though they use different math?

Both integer factoring (RSA) and discrete logarithms in cyclic or elliptic-curve groups (DH/ECC) reduce to period-finding over a finite abelian group—instances of the abelian Hidden Subgroup Problem, which the Quantum Fourier Transform solves in polynomial time.

Formally, given a finitely generated group G and an oracle f: G -> S constant on cosets of an unknown subgroup H <= G and distinct across cosets (f(x) = f(y) <=> xH = yH), find generators for H. For finite abelian G, weak Fourier sampling over G yields characters orthogonal to H in O(poly(log |G|)) queries and gates. For non-abelian G, weak Fourier sampling fails to distinguish conjugate subgroups, and even entangled multi-register Fourier sampling faces exponential representation-dimension obstructions in S_n (graph isomorphism). Regev (2004) showed a quantum reduction from the dihedral HSP to the Unique Shortest Vector Problem on lattices, yet the best known algorithm (Kuperberg’s sieve) remains subexponential (2^O(sqrt(log N))).
