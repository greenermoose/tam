Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with H / Hieratic

Hieratic is a flowing, handwritten form of ancient Egyptian writing, simplified from carved hieroglyphs.

Carved hieroglyphs were formal and slow to draw. For everyday records, Egyptian scribes used a quicker cursive version called hieratic, much like the difference between printed and handwritten letters today. It was written with ink on papyrus.

Hieratic served administration, letters, mathematics and religious texts, while monumental inscriptions tended to stay hieroglyphic. Its signs are simplified and often joined, and the script was usually written right to left. Later Demotic and Coptic writing descend from it, with Coptic adding Greek letters.

Hieratic signs reduce multi-stroke hieroglyphs to single pen strokes and are conventionally transcribed back into formal signs for study. Unicode encodes hieratic within the Egyptian Hieroglyphs block rather than a separate script block, so a font claiming hieroglyph coverage does not necessarily include cursive forms. Sign variants and direction depend on the period.
