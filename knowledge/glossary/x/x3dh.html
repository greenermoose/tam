X3DH (Extended Triple Diffie-Hellman)

An asynchronous key-agreement protocol that lets two people establish a shared encryption key even when one of them is offline.

Normally, locking a shared secret box together requires both people to be online at the same time to agree on the key. X3DH solves this by letting the receiver leave a few public lockboxes on a server ahead of time—like a drop box at a post office—so a sender can start a private, encrypted conversation at any hour without waiting for the other person to come online.

In apps such as Signal, each client uploads a bundle of public keys to the messaging server: a long-term identity key, a regularly rotated signed prekey, and a batch of single-use one-time prekeys. Unlike classic TLS handshakes, where both sides must respond live, the sender downloads that bundle, computes the shared secret locally, and attaches the first encrypted message immediately. Once the receiver comes online and consumes a one-time prekey, the server deletes it so future sessions get fresh entropy and past sessions gain forward secrecy.

What happens if a receiver runs out of one-time prekeys on the server before coming online?

The handshake still succeeds using the identity key and signed prekey alone, falling back from four Diffie-Hellman exchanges to three. You lose the extra per-session forward secrecy of a single-use key until the client uploads more one-time prekeys, which is why clients replenish them periodically.

Defined by the Signal Protocol specification, X3DH computes a master secret SK = KDF(F || DH1 || DH2 || DH3 [|| DH4]) over Curve25519 (X25519) or Curve448 (X448), where F is a domain-separation byte sequence (32 0xFF bytes for X25519). DH1 and DH2 cross the sender’s identity key IK_A and ephemeral key EK_A with the receiver’s signed prekey SPK_B and identity key IK_B for mutual authentication and deniability; DH3 (EK_A, SPK_B) and optional DH4 (EK_A, OPK_B) provide forward secrecy. Associated data binds both identity keys to prevent unknown key-share attacks, and the output seeds the Double Ratchet.
