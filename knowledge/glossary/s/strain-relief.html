Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / Strain relief

A flexible sleeve or collar where a cable meets a rigid connector plug to prevent sharp bending from breaking internal wires.

Strain relief is the bendy rubber boot right behind a cable plug that spreads out bending stress so the thin copper wires inside do not snap.

Cracked or missing strain relief is one of the fastest ways a display, power, or USB cable develops intermittent dropouts when bumped.

Why do cables most often fail right behind the connector plug?

Because that is where flexible wire meets a rigid plug, concentrating mechanical bending stress unless strain relief spreads the bend over a gentler curve.

Mechanical strain relief enforces a minimum bend radius on copper conductors and optical fibers, preserving impedance continuity and preventing fatigue fractures at solder or crimp terminations.
