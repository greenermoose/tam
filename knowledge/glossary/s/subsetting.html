Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / Subsetting

Subsetting is the process of shrinking a font file by keeping only the glyphs a document or page needs.

A font can contain thousands of characters, but a single page may use only a hundred of them. Subsetting builds a smaller font with just those letters, so documents load faster and take less space.

Websites subset fonts to reduce downloads; PDF producers embed subsets containing only the characters used on a page. Tools such as fontTools and TTX can inspect and subset fonts. Subsetting must preserve the character-to-glyph mapping, or copying and searching text will break.

Subsetting requires glyph closure: starting from requested code points, follow cmap, GSUB closure for ligatures and alternates, composite components and variant tables such as gvar. The subsetter rewrites glyph IDs, offsets and name records, and may rename the family. A DSIG signature becomes invalid, and text added later can render as tofu if its glyphs were dropped.
