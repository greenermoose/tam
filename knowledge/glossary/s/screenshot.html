Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / Screenshot

An image file that captures what is currently displayed on a computer screen, a specific window, or a selected region.

A screenshot is a digital photo of your screen taken by the computer itself. It is a quick way to save an error message, share what you are looking at with someone else, or keep a record of something on screen without using a phone camera.

Most Linux desktops bind Print Screen to capture the full screen, a single window, or a dragged rectangle, saving a PNG image (and optionally copying it to the clipboard). On Wayland desktops, individual apps cannot silently grab the whole screen for security reasons; instead, a system portal asks you to confirm or select what to capture.

Why do older X11 screenshot tools often fail or capture only a black rectangle on a Wayland session?

Wayland isolates each application’s buffers so clients cannot read other windows or the global framebuffer directly; screenshots must go through the compositor or xdg-desktop-portal with user consent.

On X11, tools such as scrot or import read the root window via XGetImage or MIT-SHM. On Wayland, compositors mediate capture either through xdg-desktop-portal (org.freedesktop.portal.Screenshot / PipeWire streams) or privileged wlroots protocols like wlr-screencopy-unstable-v1 (passing wl_shm or DMA-BUF frames). Implementation subtleties include composite cursor plane inclusion, per-output fractional scaling transforms, and HDR/wide-gamut color-space conversion (e.g., linear/PQ buffers tonemapped or tagged with sRGB ICC profiles upon PNG encoding).
