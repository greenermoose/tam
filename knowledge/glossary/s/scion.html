Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / Scion

In the Tamlinux botanical analogy, the scion is the Tamlinux software that can install on a rootstock.

In grafting a fruit tree, the rootstock is the root system and lower stem, while the scion is the upper shoot grafted onto it that grows the branches, leaves, and fruit. In Tamlinux, the rootstock is the base operating system (such as Arch Linux, Void Linux, Windows, or macOS) installed on a host, and the scion is Tamlinux’s own software—its desktop, commands, configuration, and this tam guide—which you grow from the bud and which bears fruit in the form of useful applications for you and your family.

Separating the scion from the rootstock lets Tamlinux’s upper workstation environment stay consistent even when the underlying base operating system changes. Today Tamlinux runs on an Arch Linux rootstock, with plans to support Void Linux and other bases. Each major and minor release of Tamlinux (for example, 0.6 or 0.7) is called a cultivar.

Why does Tamlinux distinguish the scion from the rootstock?

Keeping Tamlinux’s own software (the scion) separate from the underlying base OS (the rootstock) makes it possible to swap or shrink the foundation—such as moving from Arch Linux to Void Linux—without redefining the upper workstation experience.

Architecturally, the scion comprises the Tamlinux-owned user-layer packages, desktop configuration, shell integration, tam-* utilities, plugins, and tam knowledge corpus deployed above the rootstock without modifying distribution-managed files under /usr. Versioning of a scion release uses a cultivar (major + minor version and tamarack variety name), while individual components retain independent package versions.
