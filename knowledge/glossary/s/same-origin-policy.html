Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / Same-origin policy

The same-origin policy is a browser rule that stops a page from reading data from a different origin unless permission is granted.

A page may read its own site's information, but not another site's private replies. The rule keeps one tab from quietly reading another service you are logged into.

An origin is the combination of scheme, host and port. A different port or a change from http to https makes a different origin. CORS is the server-side way to allow a specific cross-origin read.

If same-origin policy blocks cross-origin reads, why is CSRF still possible?

The policy limits what script can read, not what a browser can send. A form submission or simple request may still carry a session cookie to another site, so state-changing actions need their own CSRF defence.

The policy restricts reads of response bodies, not the act of sending a request. Images and forms can load cross-origin without granting script access. postMessage offers an explicit channel, and CORS headers grant narrow exceptions to the default rule.
