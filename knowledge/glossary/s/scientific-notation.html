Scientific notation

Scientific notation writes a number as a short digits part times a power of ten.

Very large and very small numbers take a lot of zeroes. Instead of writing 1,500,000 you can write 1.5 times ten to the sixth power. The small part gives the digits; the power tells you how big the number is.

1.5 times 10^6 means move the point six places right, giving 1,500,000. 1.5 times 10^-3 means move it three places left, giving 0.0015. Calculators and programming languages often print 1.5e6 or 1.5E-3. The significand is 1.5 and the exponent is 6 or -3.

Is 15 times 10^5 the same value as 1.5 times 10^6?

Yes. Both are 1,500,000. Normalized scientific notation chooses one nonzero digit before the point so that each value has one standard written form.

A normalized decimal representation writes a value as m times 10^e with 1 less than or equal to the absolute value of m and less than 10. Floating point applies the same idea with a binary base and a fixed number of significand bits; normalization gives one leading one bit in ordinary values. The exponent's range sets magnitude limits while the significand's width sets relative precision. Displaying a stored binary value in decimal scientific notation can make its approximation look tidier than it is.
