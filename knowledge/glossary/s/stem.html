Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / Stem

A stem is the principal straight stroke that gives a letter its basic structure.

The long straight line in letters like l, b and H is the stem. It is the backbone of the letter; other parts, such as bowls and serifs, attach to it. Stems are the first thing a type designer sketches.

Designers describe stems by weight and direction: vertical stems in n and u, diagonal ones in A and V. Consistent stem weight across a family gives it a coherent look, and bold weights thicken stems more than other details. In sans-serif fonts stems end plainly; in serif fonts they often end with serifs.

Stems matter to hinting: TrueType instructions and CFF stem hints explicitly align strokes to pixel boundaries so they stay even at small sizes. Stem weight relative to x-height defines a font's weight class and contrast. Variable masters must interpolate stems consistently, or intermediate weights produce uneven strokes and collapsing counters.
