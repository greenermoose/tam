Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / Syllabary

A syllabary is a set of signs where each sign stands for a syllable rather than a single sound.

Instead of spelling ka as two letters, a syllabary has one sign that means 'ka'. Japanese kana work this way: each character stands for a sound combination such as 'shi' or 'to'.

Syllabaries range from small sets of simple signs, such as Japanese hiragana and katakana, to larger sets distinguishing length and voicing. They differ from alphabets, where letters represent individual consonants and vowels, and from abugidas, whose consonant signs carry an inherent vowel. Japanese mixes kana with logographic kanji.

A syllabary's inventory reflects the phonotactics of its language: Japanese kana encode morae, including a standalone n and length marks, while Cherokee has roughly 85 characters. Unicode encodes kana in dedicated blocks with combining dakuten for voicing. Shapers handle kana compressions and alternate forms in vertical text.
