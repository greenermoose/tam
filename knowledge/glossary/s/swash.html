Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / Swash

A swash is a decorative, flourished form of a letter, usually longer or more elaborate than the standard shape.

Some fancy letters have sweeping tails and curls that spill into the space around them. Those flourishes are swashes. They add elegance but can collide with neighboring letters if overused.

Swashes are available in many calligraphic and display fonts through OpenType features such as swsh or stylistic sets. They work best in headings and initials rather than body text. A swash may have a much larger advance width or side bearing than the ordinary letter.

Swash glyphs are alternate outlines referenced by GSUB single-substitution lookups; their advances and bearings can be enlarged or negative, so clipping and line breaking must account for ink outside the nominal box. HarfBuzz applies swsh only on request, often with a variant selector. Some fonts use stylistic sets ss01 to ss20 instead.
