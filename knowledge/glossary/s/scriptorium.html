Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / Scriptorium

A scriptorium is the dedicated writing room where medieval monks copied and decorated manuscripts.

In a monastery, one room was set aside for copying books. It was quiet, lit by windows and used by scribes who spent hours writing and illuminating pages. That room is a scriptorium.

Work followed the daily routine of the community; copying was both craft and discipline. One book might pass through several hands: a scribe wrote the text, a rubricator added headings and an illuminator painted initials. Libraries grew from the books produced there.

Scriptoria developed standardized scripts and house styles that palaeographers use to localize manuscripts. Evidence comes from surviving books, library catalogues and monastic buildings; not every monastery had a distinct scriptorium, and copying also happened elsewhere. Colophons occasionally name the scribes and record the time taken.
