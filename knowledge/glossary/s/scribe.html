Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / Scribe

A scribe is someone who writes or copies documents by hand as a craft.

Before printing, if you wanted a book, someone had to copy it letter by letter. That person was a scribe. Scribes wrote letters, laws, religious texts and records, sometimes spending months on one book.

Scribes worked in courts, temples, monasteries and universities, often from an exemplar and sometimes from dictation. They used abbreviations and standardized scripts to work faster, and a corrector might check the result. Their habits explain spelling variants in surviving texts.

Palaeography identifies scribal hands by letterforms, ligatures, abbreviations and spacing, helping date and localize a manuscript. A scriptorium could divide work among scribes, rubricators and illuminators. Copying introduced errors, omissions and glosses, so editors compare witnesses to reconstruct a text.
