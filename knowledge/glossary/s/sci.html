SCI (Software Carbon Intensity)

Software Carbon Intensity (SCI) is a standard metric defined by ISO/IEC 21031 that measures the carbon emissions produced per functional unit of software.

SCI is like a fuel-economy rating for software. Just as a car is rated in grams of CO2 per kilometer, SCI estimates how many grams of carbon emissions a program causes for each unit of work it does—such as one web request or one processed video—so teams can see whether their software is becoming cleaner over time.

Defined in ISO/IEC 21031, SCI is a rate (emissions per unit of work) rather than a total footprint, which makes it useful for comparing software efficiency as traffic grows. To lower a system’s SCI score, teams can reduce energy use (faster code), run jobs when the electrical grid uses cleaner power (carbon-aware scheduling), or keep servers in service longer to spread out manufacturing emissions.

Why does SCI measure carbon per functional unit (R) instead of total carbon emissions?

Total emissions naturally rise when more users adopt an application, even if the code becomes more efficient. Dividing by a functional unit like API calls or active users isolates software efficiency from growth.

ISO/IEC 21031 specifies the rate SCI = ((E × I) + M) / R, where E is energy consumed (kWh) over a defined software boundary, I is location-based marginal or average grid carbon intensity (gCO2eq/kWh), M is amortized embodied emissions of the underlying hardware, and R is the chosen functional unit (e.g., per request, ML inference, or user-hour). Notably, ISO/IEC 21031 prohibits subtracting purchased carbon offsets or market-based RECs from the score, ensuring that reductions come strictly from energy efficiency, hardware efficiency, or temporal/spatial load shifting.
