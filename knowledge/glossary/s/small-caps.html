Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / Small caps

Small caps are capital letters drawn at roughly x-height so they sit comfortably within lowercase text.

Small caps look like capital letters but are about as tall as small letters. Writers use them for names, labels or emphasis without making a word shout. They are common in books and academic writing.

A real small-caps font includes separate glyphs; faking them by scaling full capitals produces thinner strokes and uneven spacing. OpenType exposes small caps through smcp and all-capital variants through c2sc; CSS uses font-variant-caps: small-caps. Some fonts also provide oldstyle digits to match.

Small-cap glyphs are separate outlines with cap-like shapes at x-height and adjusted stem weights, reached through GSUB features rather than case mapping. smcp substitutes lowercase to small caps while c2sc substitutes uppercase; if both are enabled the last wins. A small positive letter-spacing often helps because small caps are wider than the lowercase letters they replace.
