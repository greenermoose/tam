Guide / Glossary A-Z / Terms beginning with S / Significand

The significand is the part of a number that carries its digits.

In 1.5 times 10^6, the 1.5 is the significand. It says which digits the number has, while the exponent says where to put the point.

The same number can be written as 15 times 10^5 or 1.5 times 10^6, so its significand depends on normalization. In floating point, the significand has a fixed number of bits and the exponent shifts their scale. More significand bits mean more precision, but the exponent controls the overall range.

Is the significand the same as the mantissa of a logarithm?

No. Mantissa is an older word sometimes used for the significand, but it also has a separate meaning in logarithms. Significand is the precise term for the digit field of floating point.

IEEE 754 binary formats store the fractional field of a normalized significand and imply a leading one bit, which is not stored. binary32 has a 23-bit field and effectively 24 bits of significand; binary64 has a 52-bit field and effectively 53 bits. Subnormal values drop the implicit leading one so tiny numbers can be represented. Decimal formats may store coefficient digits directly, with different rules for what counts as a significand.
