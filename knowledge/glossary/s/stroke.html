Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / Stroke

A stroke is a single continuous line of a letter, drawn with a pen, brush or outline path.

Letters are built from strokes: the downstroke of a T, the curve of an O. Calligraphers control pressure and angle to make strokes vary in width, which gives writing its personality and rhythm.

In type design strokes can be monolinear, with nearly constant width, or contrast-heavy, as in Didone fonts where vertical strokes are far thicker than hairlines. Stroke endings, or terminals, can be blunt, pointed or ornamented. Stroke treatment is one of the clearest clues to a font's style and period.

In outline fonts a stroke is not stored as a centered path: it is represented by the contours bounding the filled shape, or by a single closed contour for an enclosed stroke. Variable fonts interpolate those outlines. Rasterizers derive coverage from filled contours, so apparent stroke weight also depends on antialiasing and gamma correction.
