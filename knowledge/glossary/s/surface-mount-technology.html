Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / Surface-mount technology (SMT)

Surface-mount technology, or SMT, is the method of soldering components directly onto pads on the surface of a printed circuit board.

Instead of pushing component legs through holes, small parts are placed on top of the board and soldered in place. That is why modern boards can hold so many tiny parts.

The usual sequence is stencilled solder paste, pick-and-place of the components, and reflow soldering to form the joints. Acceptability of the finished joints and assembly is described in IPC-A-610.

This SMT is not the SMT entry for simultaneous multithreading in processors: the acronym has two unrelated meanings. Surface mounting is a board assembly process on an FR-4-type substrate, while simultaneous multithreading is a CPU execution technique.
