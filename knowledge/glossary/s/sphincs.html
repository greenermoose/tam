Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / SPHINCS+ (SLH-DSA)

A post-quantum digital signature algorithm built solely from cryptographic hash functions, standardized by NIST as FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA).

Most digital signatures today rely on math puzzles that a future quantum computer could break. SPHINCS+ avoids those puzzles entirely and builds signatures out of simple, well-understood “digital fingerprints” (hashes)—and unlike older hash-based schemes, it doesn’t need to keep a counter of every signature ever made, so you can’t accidentally break it by reusing an old backup.

NIST standardized SPHINCS+ as SLH-DSA (Stateless Hash-Based Digital Signature Algorithm) in FIPS 205 alongside lattice-based ML-DSA (FIPS 204). Because SLH-DSA relies only on hash-function security rather than newer lattice assumptions, it serves as a conservative fallback for long-lived keys like root CAs or firmware signing—at the cost of much larger signatures (roughly 8–50 KB depending on parameter set) and slower signing than ML-DSA.

What does “stateless” mean in SPHINCS+ / SLH-DSA, and why is it a big advantage over older hash-based signatures like XMSS?

Stateful schemes like XMSS track a strict index of used one-time keys; reusing an index (for example, after restoring a VM snapshot) destroys security. SPHINCS+ uses a randomized hypertree structure so signing requires no mutable state.

SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) combines FORS (Forest of Random Subsets, a few-time signature) at the leaves with a multi-layer hypertree of XMSS trees using WOTS+ (Winternitz One-Time Signature Plus) and tweakable hash functions (SHA-2 or SHAKE256, in -s small/slow vs -f fast parameter sets across NIST security categories 1, 3, and 5). Signature sizes range from ~7.8 KB (SLH-DSA-SHA2-128s) to ~49.8 KB (SLH-DSA-SHAKE-256f), with signing requiring thousands of hash calls; security reduces strictly to standard hash properties (second-preimage and PRF resistance) without algebraic structure.
