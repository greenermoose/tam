Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / Sans-serif

Sans-serif fonts have plain letter endings without the small feet and hooks called serifs.

Look at the letters on a sign or a website: many have clean, straight endings with no little decorative feet. Those are sans-serif fonts, usually easy to read at small sizes and on screens. Their plain shapes feel modern and direct.

Families such as Liberation Sans, DejaVu Sans, Inter and Roboto are common defaults for interfaces and web pages. They tend to look neutral, though their simplicity can make long paragraphs less restful than a well-set serif face. Fontconfig's generic sans-serif family maps to an installed face through alias rules.

Sans-serif is a classification, not a font format: the glyphs are ordinary outlines in TTF/OTF files and the style shows in unflared stroke endings. Subcategories include grotesque, neo-grotesque, geometric and humanist. In CSS the generic sans-serif keyword delegates to the system's chosen family; the classification is not a machine-readable SFNT field.
