Guide / Glossary A–Z / Terms beginning with S / Stress

Stress is the direction of thickening in a letter's strokes, traditionally caused by the angle of a broad pen.

If you write with a flat-tipped pen held at an angle, some strokes come out thick and others thin. That direction of thickness is stress. It gives handwriting and serif fonts a recognizable slant to their contrast.

In Roman capitals stress is usually vertical or slightly tilted; in italic designs it follows the slant. Designers speak of diagonal stress when thick strokes run from upper right to lower left. Sans-serif fonts often have little stress, keeping strokes more even.

Stress is a design property visible in contour geometry, not an OpenType field. It differs across systems: Latin broad-pen stress, Chinese brush-pressure stress and copperplate pointed-pen stress follow different mechanics. Optical-size axes can reduce stress at small sizes by distributing weight more evenly; avar remapping or custom masters can alter it in variable designs.
