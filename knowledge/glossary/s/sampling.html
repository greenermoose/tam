Guide / Glossary A-Z / Terms beginning with S / Sampling

Sampling records a changing value at separate moments instead of continuously.

A movie is a series of still pictures. Played quickly, they look like smooth motion. Sampling a sound or sensor reading works the same way: each snapshot becomes one stored number.

The sample rate says how many snapshots are taken each second, measured in hertz. Audio at 48 kHz takes 48,000 samples per second. Sampling decides when to look; quantization decides how precisely to record what was seen. They are separate steps performed by an ADC.

If you double the sample rate, do you double the highest frequency you can capture?

Yes, approximately: the highest recoverable frequency is just under half the sample rate, the Nyquist frequency. Real filters need a transition band, so usable bandwidth is somewhat lower.

Ideal sampling multiplies a continuous signal by an impulse train. Perfect reconstruction from those samples requires the signal to be band-limited below half the sample rate and uses sinc interpolation in theory. Real systems use sample-and-hold circuits, anti-alias filters and practical reconstruction filters. Clock jitter, aperture uncertainty and timing skew between channels introduce error even when amplitude quantization is fine.
