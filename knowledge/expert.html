The expert's path

These chapters go into the design decisions and internals behind the workstation. They read best after the intermediate path, and several of them are reference material rather than a narrative.

  1. C libraries — the glibc and musl question, and what the C runtime really is.
  2. Lua scripting — embedding a small language for configuration and control.
  3. Base distributions — the philosophies behind Arch, Void, and antiX.
  4. Init systems — PID 1, service supervision, and process trees.
  5. Compositors and windowing — the Wayland protocol and the compositor's role.
  6. Virtualization — namespaces, cgroups, and virtual machines.
  7. Packaging — build systems, reproducibility, and distribution formats.

Some foundation pages also carry an expert depth of their own: shell pipelines, processes and jobs, file permissions, and resources. Read one at that depth with tam show shell/pipelines?level=expert, or list every topic that offers it with tam explain --level expert.
