SMS (Short Message Service) is the classic text service. Its central idea is simple: a message centre accepts a short message, keeps it, finds the receiver's current network and tries again until the message is delivered or expires.

A message centre

When you send a short message, your phone gives it to a SMSC (Short Message Service Center). The centre is a store that holds the message. It asks the network where the receiver is, sends the message onward and keeps trying if the phone is unavailable.

Why it matters

The sender's phone can be switched off as soon as the centre accepts the message. The receiver's phone can be off too. The centre bridges the gap between them.

How to think about it

Think of a parcel depot. You drop off a parcel and the depot keeps it until a delivery route is open. If the recipient is away, the depot tries again rather than asking you to hand over the parcel again.

New words

Short message
A small text message carried by the SMS service.
Message centre
A system that stores and forwards short messages.
Retry
Trying delivery again after a temporary failure.
Validity period
How long the centre should keep trying.

If it goes wrong

You treated a delivery receipt as proof that a person read the text.
A receipt normally reports delivery to a network or device, not human attention.
You expected a long message to be one unlimited message.
SMS has a small payload. Longer text is split into segments that are reassembled by the receiving device.

Check yourself

Decide your answer before you reveal it.

What does centre acceptance prove?

It proves that the SMSC took the message and will try to deliver it. It does not prove that the receiver was found, that the phone was on, or that anyone read the text.

Why can parts of a long text arrive out of order?

The segments are separate short messages. Networks may retry or route them independently; the receiving phone uses concatenation information to reassemble them.

How it works

3GPP TS 23.040 defines the short message service, including the transfer protocol, segmentation and status reports. 3GPP TS 24.011 covers the radio-interface side of point-to-point short messages. The default alphabet allows about 160 seven-bit characters in one payload; using a wider character set reduces that count. Longer messages carry user-data headers that identify segments for reassembly. Application-to-person traffic often reaches the centre through SMPP version 3.4, while carrier systems use MAP procedures to find a serving system and forward the message.

Edge cases

  • A validity period can expire while the receiver is unreachable. The message is then discarded or reported as expired; delivery is not guaranteed.
  • Duplicate delivery can occur after a retry if an acknowledgement is lost. Idempotency keys or application-level deduplication matter for one-time codes.
  • Cell broadcast uses a different path from point-to-point SMS: one message is sent to all phones in an area, not addressed to a subscriber.

Check yourself

Why is SMS unsuitable as the only factor for a high-value action?

It is convenient but not proof of possession by the intended person. SIM swap, interception by network insiders, replay and message forwarding all break the assumption that receipt by a number means control by the account holder.
