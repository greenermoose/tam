Guide / Systems and administration / Mobile communications / Texting and instant messaging / MMS and multimedia messages

MMS (Multimedia Messaging Service) carries more than short text. A photo, sound clip or longer note is stored by a service, and the receiver's phone fetches the content after receiving a small notice.

A message with something attached

A short message is made of words. A multimedia message can include a picture, a sound or a longer piece of writing. Because that content is bigger, the phone usually does not carry it all in the same small notification that announces the message.

Why it matters

People want to share a photo or a voice note without opening a separate chat app. MMS gives the carrier messaging service a way to carry that content, even when the two phones use different models or networks.

How to think about it

Think of a postcard that says "a photo is waiting for you at the studio." The postcard is small. The photo itself is kept somewhere else until you go and collect it. Multimedia messaging works in a similar two-step way.

New words

Multimedia message
A message that can carry more than plain short text.
Media content
The picture, sound or longer text inside the message.
MMSC (Multimedia Messaging Service Center)
The service that stores multimedia messages and handles them.
Retrieval
Fetching the content after the phone has been told it is waiting.

If it goes wrong

You expected the picture to arrive with the first notice.
The notice is small. The media is usually stored and fetched in a second step.
You assumed a message that is "sent" has been viewed.
A delivery report can mean the MMSC handed it off; a read report comes from the receiving device and is not always available.

Check yourself

Decide your answer before you reveal it.

Why can a multimedia message fail when ordinary short texts still arrive?

The short notice can travel over signalling, but the media usually needs a data connection and a working carrier data profile. If that path fails, the notice can arrive while the content cannot be fetched.

What can change between the sender's phone and the receiver's phone?

The centre can adapt or resize content to suit the receiver and network policy. The picture the receiver sees need not be byte-for-byte the one the sender stored.

How it works

3GPP TS 23.140 defines the multimedia messaging service. The sender uploads content to its MMSC; the centre stores it and sends a notification to the receiver. The receiver uses a data bearer to retrieve the content from a temporary address. The message body is structured with multipart content types, so text, images and audio can be carried together. Network operators set their own maximum message size and retention policy, so a limit that works on one carrier may fail on another.

Edge cases

  • A retrieval link can expire. After expiry, the notice may still be present in the conversation but the media can no longer be fetched.
  • Roaming can break retrieval when the visited network does not supply the right data profile, even though the notification arrived.
  • Group multimedia messaging may create one stored object per recipient, which complicates privacy expectations and read reports.
  • An untrusted sender can use a multimedia notice to lure a reader into fetching content. Treat link-based delivery as part of the abuse surface, not only a transport detail.

Check yourself

A user says "the text came through but the picture did not." Which layer do you inspect first?

Inspect retrieval: did the phone receive a notice, was there a usable data connection and APN, and is the stored content still within its retention window? Only then inspect the sender's submission or the MMSC's forwarding path.
