Environment-Minded Systems and Desktops

This page introduces projects that aim to make computers last longer or use less energy.

Tamlinux is not the only project that treats software as a way to keep computers in use and spend less energy. The projects on this page say so in their own mission statements. They are listed here for comparison and ideas. None of them has a Tamlinux role, and being listed is not a recommendation.

The entries describe each project's lasting aim and design. Check a project's own site for its current release and status.

Desktop distributions for reused computers

wattOS

  • Design: a small desktop distribution, first built on Ubuntu and later on Debian, with a lightweight window manager and a short list of preinstalled applications.
  • Environmental aim: low energy use, and putting old equipment back to work rather than throwing it away. It has pursued this aim since 2008, which makes it one of the oldest projects on this page.
  • Idea worth noting: a whole distribution organized around energy use rather than around a desktop or a release model.

Emmabuntüs

  • Design: a Debian-based distribution maintained by a volunteer collective in France, with Xfce and the lighter LXQt desktops, many preinstalled applications, educational software and screen-reader support.
  • Environmental aim: refurbishing computers donated to humanitarian organizations, especially the Emmaüs communities it is named after, and reducing electronic waste. Reuse groups in France and Africa install it on donated machines.
  • Idea worth noting: reuse depends on people as much as software. Emmabuntüs is built for the volunteer who refurbishes a machine and the beginner who receives it, including people who are blind or have low vision.

GALPon MiniNo

  • Design: a minimal Debian-based distribution from GALPon, the Pontevedra Linux user group in Spain, with a lightweight desktop and a children's edition called PicarOS.
  • Environmental aim: it began as an operating system for computers recycled by the University of Vigo, aiming for the most usable system on the least hardware.
  • Status: no stable release since 2018. Read it as a record of a university and user group working together on reuse.

Desktop software and measurement

KDE Eco

  • What it is: the KDE community's environmental initiative, supported by KDE e.V. KDE makes the Plasma desktop and many applications.
  • Measurement: KDE Eco measures how much energy software uses while it runs a scripted, repeatable scenario, and it provides KEcoLab, a remote measurement lab that developers can run from their continuous integration pipelines.
  • Eco-label: the German Environment Agency's Blue Angel (Blauer Engel) label has criteria for desktop software covering energy and resource efficiency, how long existing hardware stays usable, and user autonomy. KDE's document viewer Okular became the first software product to carry the label, in 2022. KDE Eco publishes a handbook on applying the criteria to free software.
  • Outreach: its Opt Green project, which ran from 2024 to 2026, took free software to environmental events, install workshops and repair cafés under the motto that the greenest device is the one you already have.
  • Idea worth noting: energy claims that anyone can repeat and compare, because they come from a published method rather than from the developer's own description.

Green Software Foundation

  • What it is: a Linux Foundation project that writes standards for software emissions. It makes no operating system.
  • Software Carbon Intensity: its SCI specification gives a method for calculating a software system's rate of carbon emissions per unit of useful work. It became the international standard ISO/IEC 21031:2024.
  • Idea worth noting: a shared vocabulary, so one project's figures can be set beside another's.

Phones and other devices

Nura (formerly postmarketOS)

  • Design: a Linux distribution for phones and other mobile devices, built on Alpine Linux and aiming to run mainline kernels rather than the vendor kernels a phone ships with. It was renamed from postmarketOS to Nura in 2026.
  • Environmental aim: extending the life of consumer electronics, so that a phone stays usable long after its manufacturer stops updating it.
  • Idea worth noting: a device's software support, not its hardware, usually ends its useful life. The same applies to laptops and desktops.

/e/OS

  • Design: a mobile operating system derived from LineageOS, an Android distribution, with Google services removed. It is developed by the e Foundation, a French non-profit, and sold preinstalled on phones by its commercial partner Murena.
  • Environmental aim: its first aim is privacy. It also supports older phones and frames long-term, independent maintenance as part of its mission.

Radical reuse

Collapse OS and Dusk OS

  • Design: two small operating systems written in Forth by Virgil Dupras. Collapse OS targets 8-bit processors such as the Z80, which are common in old computers, calculators and other equipment. Dusk OS targets ordinary 32-bit PCs.
  • Environmental aim: keeping computing possible on scavenged hardware, with tools and documentation that do not depend on a network or on new parts.
  • Idea worth noting: a whole system small enough for one person to understand, build from source and repair.

Permacomputing

  • What it is: a community and wiki, not a single project, collecting low-energy, long-lasting computing practice. Its principles include caring for the chips that already exist and keeping systems small.
  • Idea worth noting: its project lists are a directory of small systems with similar aims, including Collapse OS.

Campaigns

End of 10 is a campaign by free software communities, KDE among them, and repair café groups. It helps people keep using computers that can no longer be upgraded past Windows 10 by installing Linux instead of buying new hardware. Its site helps people find local repair cafés, Linux user groups and install events.

Lightweight is not the same as environmental

Many distributions and desktops run well on old hardware without stating an environmental aim, such as antiX, described in The Debian family, and the Xfce and LXQt desktops. A project with "eco" or "green" in its name may have no environmental programme at all. Judge a project by what it does and measures, not by its name.

Where Tamlinux stands

Tamlinux shares the aims of reuse and low resource use. Read its approach in these pages:

  • Sustainability and hardware longevity — profiles chosen by what a machine can do, not by its age.
  • Zero-polling and event-driven architecture — keeping the processor idle between events.
  • The progressive power ladder — from blanking monitors to zero-watt hibernation.

Tamlinux holds no eco-label, and this guide does not yet publish measured energy figures for it.
