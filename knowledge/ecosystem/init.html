Starting and looking after system programs

This page explains how a computer starts the background programs it needs and how you inspect them safely.

Before you start: read-only commands only.

Do not restart services while learning. Looking is enough.

Mental model

Process ID 1 (init) starts the system and supervises services. On the current Arch rootstock, that manager is systemd. A planned Void rootstock uses runit. Seat access for graphics is a related but separate boundary (logind today; seatd on runit targets).

Worked example

$ command -v systemctl
/usr/bin/systemctl

Captured on Arch Linux, 2026-11-02. If the command is missing, you may be on a different init; do not invent systemd steps.

Try it

Run systemctl is-system-running if available and record the word it prints.

If it goes wrong

command not found
You may not be on a systemd rootstock; stop and identify the init in use.

On Tamlinux: systemd is current; runit is planned with a future Void rootstock. Keep shell tools free of hard systemd assumptions where practical.

Check yourself

Does finding systemctl prove every service is healthy?

No. It only shows the tool is installed. Inspect specific units for health.

Units, seats and failure boundaries

systemd units, sockets and targets group work; the journal stores structured logs. A Wayland compositor needs seat permission for DRM and input. On systemd systems systemd-logind grants seats; without systemd, seatd and libseat can. Restarting a login or display-related unit can end sessions—capture evidence first.

Why runit is attractive on the target rootstock: simple run scripts, direct supervision, small footprint. That is design rationale, not a claim that your current host runs runit.

Check yourself

A greeter or desktop fails only after a service restart you triggered. What should your note include?

The unit name, time, prior status evidence, and that the restart may have terminated sessions. Prefer read-only diagnosis before further restarts.
