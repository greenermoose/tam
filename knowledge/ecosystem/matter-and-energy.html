Matter and energy

This page explains the tiny building blocks of electricity and light that make computer chips work.

Meet electrons and light packets

Goal: you will be able to say what an electron and a photon are, and why chips need stepped energy rules.

Why it matters: every wire, transistor and display on your workstation moves electrons or emits photons. Naming those pieces makes later circuit and cable lessons stop feeling like magic words.

Before you start: no commands are required. Read the glossary links if a word is new. This page only explains; it does not change your system.

You do not need a laboratory. Everyday words are enough: electron = tiny charged bit of matter that can move in metal and chips; photon = tiny packet of light.

Worked example

Hold these three sentences and check them against the glossary pages:

  1. An electron carries negative charge; flowing electrons are electric current.
  2. A photon is a packet of light; your screen emits many photons toward your eyes.
  3. Quantum means some amounts in nature come in fixed steps, not any smooth value.

Open electron, photon and quantum at novice level and confirm each sentence matches.

What just happened

  1. You connected everyday computer parts (wires, screens) to two physical objects.
  2. You learned that energy in atoms often changes in steps, which is why semiconductors can switch.

If it goes wrong

The words feel abstract
Stay with the novice glossary pages and the three sentences above. Do not jump to formulas yet.

Check yourself

What does a photon carry that an electron does not?

A photon is a packet of light (electromagnetic) energy and has no rest mass. An electron is a particle of matter with negative charge that can sit in atoms or flow in conductors.

Why does "quantum" matter for a computer chip?

Electrons in solids occupy stepped energy bands. The gap between bands is what lets a semiconductor act like a switch.

Bandgap and switching

Goal: explain how a bandgap lets a semiconductor act as a controllable conductor.

Mental model

valence band (electrons "at home")
        |  bandgap (forbidden energies)
        v
conduction band (electrons free to flow)

In a metal, electrons already move easily. In an insulator, the bandgap is so large that almost none reach the conduction band. In a semiconductor the gap is moderate: heat, light, or doping can add carriers, and a gate voltage in a transistor can open or close a channel.

Worked example

Silicon's bandgap is about 1.12 eV. A red photon may lack enough energy to promote an electron across that gap; a more energetic photon may succeed. That is why some materials look opaque or transparent to different colors, and why photodiodes and cameras need matching wavelengths.

Match photon energy to the gap: if the photon energy is below the gap, absorption into the conduction band does not occur that way. Device designers choose materials and wavelengths together.

Check yourself

What sits between the valence band and the conduction band?

The bandgap: a range of energies electrons cannot stably occupy in the ideal crystal picture.

Name one practical way to add carriers in a semiconductor without melting it.

Doping (adding controlled impurities), applying a gate field in a FET, or absorbing suitable photons.

Why this model is enough for troubleshooting

Expert workstation diagnosis rarely needs full quantum-field formalism. It needs a correct causal stack: carriers and bandgaps → devices → circuits → signaling → OS → session. Mis-stating the physics (for example treating electrons as continuous fluid without resistance, or treating light as only a wave when photodetection matters) leads to wrong fault classes.

When a display is dark, ask whether photons are being produced, whether the cable carries the electrical protocol, whether the GPU and DRM stack program the output, and whether the compositor is alive. The photon step is real even when the fix is tam-desktop or a cable reseat.

Check yourself

A blank screen could be "no photons" or "photons present but wrong signal". How would you separate those classes without opening the panel?

Check whether the panel has power/backlight evidence, whether another output or VT shows text, whether the compositor and DRM report a connected sink, and whether a known-good cable or port changes the result. Optical absence and protocol absence are different layers.
