The intermediate path

These pages assume you can find your way around the shell and now want to understand how the workstation is put together. They build on the beginner's path, and go deeper than the desktop does on its own.

  1. Workspaces and multi-display layout — arranging windows across workspaces and screens.
  2. Compositors and windowing — Sway, Wayland, and the layer that draws your desktop.
  3. Init systems — runit, systemd, and what starts your machine.
  4. Desktop application suites — the KDE and GNOME toolkits that ship alongside Tamlinux.
  5. Desktop toolkits — how interfaces are built, and what QML adds.
  6. AI tooling and human agency — the local AI tools, and how to use them deliberately.
  7. Packaging — how software is built, installed, and updated.
  8. Carried patches — how fixes to third-party packages are carried and retired.
  9. Virtualization — containers and virtual machines for isolation.
  10. Base distributions — Arch, Void, and antiX, and why Tamlinux sits on Arch.

When you want the design decisions and internals behind all this, continue to the expert's path.
