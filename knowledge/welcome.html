Welcome to Tamlinux

Hello, and welcome! You are reading the Tamlinux guide. It lives on your own computer, so it works on a plane, in a cabin, or anywhere else the Wi-Fi has given up.

What is Tamlinux?

Tamlinux is a workstation environment for Linux. Fred Horch develops it so he can use the computers he owns for both personal and professional work, for many years to come. The aim is a well-appointed, ergonomic and super-efficient system that runs well on laptop and desktop computers up to twenty years old.

Efficiency is not only a kindness to old machines. Leaving out bloat, resident daemons and polling loops lets a brand-new laptop run faster, cooler and on less power, too.

Why “Tam”?

The tamarack is a larch: a hardy conifer that turns gold in autumn and drops its needles every year to grow fresh ones in spring. Tamlinux works the same way. It sheds old packages to make room for new ones, so it can keep improving. “Tam” can also mean the absolute max, or teaching and making. Pick your favorite.

Where things stand

Tamlinux is a continuing project, developed in public and used every day on Fred's own workstation. Today its desktop runs on Arch Linux with Hyprland; a move to Sway on a smaller base system is planned. There is no installable image yet, and this guide marks anything still being built as planned.

Three keys to remember

  • SUPER + SPACE opens the Tamlinux menu.
  • SUPER + RETURN opens a terminal.
  • SUPER + K lists every keybinding, for the day your fingers forget.

SUPER is usually the key with the Windows logo on it. The desktop guide covers the rest.

Getting around this guide

The guide is driven by the keyboard. Tab moves to the next link, Enter follows it and Left Arrow takes you back. A backtick (`) jumps to the Guide's front page, / searches the page, ? shows help and q quits. If you wander off, type tam welcome (or simply tam hi) in any terminal to come back here.
