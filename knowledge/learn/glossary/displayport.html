DisplayPort

DisplayPort is an open, royalty-free digital interface standard used to transmit high-resolution video and audio from computers to displays.

Beginner definition

A rectangular digital video cable connector with one angled corner, commonly found on computer graphics cards, laptops, and modern monitors.

Intermediate definition

A high-bandwidth digital display standard created by the Video Electronics Standards Association (VESA). Unlike older standards that transmit continuous pixel streams, DisplayPort breaks data into packets similar to Ethernet and PCIe, allowing multiple monitors to be connected along a single cable.

Expert definition

A micro-packet digital video protocol utilizing 1 to 4 Main Link differential serial lanes with 8b/10b or 128b/132b encoding, paired with a dedicated bidirectional half-duplex 1 Mbps to 720 Mbps AUX channel for link training, DPCD management, and EDID polling. It supports Multi-Stream Transport (MST), Display Stream Compression (DSC), and USB-C DisplayPort Alternate Mode.
