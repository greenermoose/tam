Bandgap

A bandgap is the energy step an electron must climb before it can move freely and carry current in a semiconductor.

Novice definition

In a semiconductor, electrons normally sit in a lower energy band. The bandgap is the empty energy range they must cross to reach the higher band where they can flow as current. A larger bandgap usually means a better insulator at room temperature.

Learner definition

Between the valence band and the conduction band lies a forbidden energy range called the bandgap. Silicon's bandgap is about 1.12 eV. Photons or heat with enough energy can excite electrons across it; doping shifts how many carriers are available without that excitation.

Expert definition

The energy difference between the top of the valence band and the bottom of the conduction band in a crystalline solid; it sets intrinsic carrier density and optical absorption edges.
