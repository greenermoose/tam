Semiconductor

A semiconductor is a material whose electrical conductivity can be controlled and switched.

Novice definition

A solid substance, most commonly silicon, that conducts electricity better than an insulator like rubber but not as easily as a metal like copper. By applying a control voltage or adding tiny chemical impurities, a semiconductor can be made to act like a wire or like an insulator, making it the building block of all computer chips.

Learner definition

A crystalline solid characterized by an learner energy bandgap between its valence band and conduction band. Introducing donor impurities (N-type doping) or acceptor impurities (P-type doping) alters the carrier concentration, allowing precise electrical modulation.

Expert definition

A solid-state material with a moderate energy bandgap (such as silicon at 1.12 eV) where thermal or optical excitation elevates valence electrons across the forbidden gap into the conduction band. Modulating carrier densities via external electric fields forms the basis of diodes, field-effect transistors, and optoelectronic devices.
