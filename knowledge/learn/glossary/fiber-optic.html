Fiber Optic

Fiber optic is a transmission medium that guides pulses of light through transparent glass or plastic fibers.

Novice definition

A thin flexible strand of glass or plastic that carries computer data using flashes of light instead of electricity. Because it uses light, fiber optic cables can carry enormous amounts of data over tens of miles and are completely immune to electrical interference and lightning.

Learner definition

An optical dielectric waveguide operating on the principle of total internal reflection. Light travels through a higher-refractive-index glass core bounded by lower-refractive-index cladding. Divided into single-mode fiber (narrow core, laser driven, long haul) and multi-mode fiber (wider core, LED/VCSEL driven, local datacenters).

Expert definition

Low-attenuation optical transmission medium bounded by Snell's law critical angle (critical angle = arcsin(n2 / n1)). Optical transceivers interface copper electronic circuits to fiber via modulated laser diodes (electrical to optical) and PIN/Avalanche photodiodes with transimpedance amplifiers (optical to electrical).
