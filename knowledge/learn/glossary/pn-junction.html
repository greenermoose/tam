P-N Junction

A P-N junction is the boundary inside a semiconductor between positive-hole and negative-electron regions.

Beginner definition

The microscopic meeting point created when a region of silicon with extra electrons (N-type) joins a region with missing electrons (P-type). This boundary forms a one-way gate: electricity can easily cross in one direction (forward bias), but is stopped from flowing in the other direction (reverse bias).

Intermediate definition

The interface between P-type and N-type semiconductor layers. Mobile carriers diffuse across the boundary until a depletion region forms with a built-in electric field. An external forward voltage collapses this barrier to allow conduction, while a reverse voltage widens it to block current.

Expert definition

An anisotropic semiconductor interface described by the Shockley ideal diode equation. Recombination of electron-hole pairs across a forward-biased direct-bandgap P-N junction emits photons (LEDs, laser diodes), while photon absorption across a reverse-biased junction generates photocurrent (photodiodes).
