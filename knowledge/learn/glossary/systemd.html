systemd

systemd is the program that starts and supervises most services after the Linux kernel finishes early boot on current Tamlinux systems.

Beginner definition

After the kernel mounts the installed system, systemd (or another init) starts services such as networking and login. On Arch-based Tamlinux, systemd is the init system today. You inspect it with tools like systemctl and journalctl. A future Void rootstock may use runit instead.

Intermediate definition

systemd organizes work into units (services, sockets, targets). Targets group units similarly to traditional runlevels. The journal stores structured logs. Diagnosis separates unit failure from kernel or compositor failure.

Expert definition

The init and service manager used on the current Arch-based Tamlinux rootstock; units and the journal are primary evidence sources.
