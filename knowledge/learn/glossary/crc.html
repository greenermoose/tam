CRC - Cyclic Redundancy Check

A Cyclic Redundancy Check (CRC) is an error-detecting checksum computed via polynomial division over a block of data.

Novice definition

A mathematical calculation performed on a block of data to produce a short fingerprint (checksum). The sender attaches the CRC to the message; the receiver re-runs the calculation. If even a single bit changed during transit, the numbers will not match, alerting the receiver that the data was corrupted.

Learner definition

A non-cryptographic hash function based on binary polynomial division modulo 2. The data bits represent polynomial coefficients divided by a predetermined generator polynomial; the division remainder forms the CRC code (e.g. CRC-16 or CRC-32), providing strong protection against burst errors.

Expert definition

A cyclic linear error-detecting code over the Galois field GF(2). A generator polynomial of degree r guarantees detection of all single-bit errors, all two-bit errors, all odd numbers of bit errors, and all burst errors of length less than or equal to r.
