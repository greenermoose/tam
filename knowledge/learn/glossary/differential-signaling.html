Differential Signaling

Differential signaling is transmitting a signal over a pair of wires using equal and opposite voltages.

Novice definition

Sending an electrical signal across two twisted wires: one wire carries a positive voltage and the other carries an equal negative voltage. The receiver reads the difference between the two wires, which cancels out any electrical noise or static picked up along the way.

Learner definition

A physical signaling method that rejects common-mode noise. Because external electromagnetic interference affects both closely twisted wires identically, the receiver's differential subtraction (V+ - V-) completely eliminates the noise, allowing reliable operation over long distances and low voltage swings.

Expert definition

Balanced line transmission governed by Common-Mode Rejection Ratio (CMRR). Eliminates dependence on shared ground references, prevents ground loop noise, reduces electromagnetic emissions, and underpins modern high-speed interfaces like LVDS, USB, Ethernet, and PCIe.
