Diode

A diode is a two-terminal electronic component that permits current flow in one direction only.

Beginner definition

A one-way valve for electric current. It allows electricity to pass through easily in the forward direction, but blocks it from flowing backward. Diodes convert alternating AC power into direct DC power and protect computer ports from static electricity shocks.

Intermediate definition

A semiconductor device constructed around a P-N junction. In forward bias, current flows once the applied voltage exceeds the forward threshold drop (typically 0.6 to 0.7 volts for silicon). Specialized diodes include Schottky diodes (fast switching, low forward drop), Zener diodes (voltage regulation), and TVS diodes (transient voltage suppression).

Expert definition

A non-linear two-terminal semiconductor element exhibiting asymmetric conductance. In computer hardware engineering, diodes serve in bridge rectifiers for power supplies, clamping diodes for electrostatic discharge (ESD) protection on I/O lines, and optoelectronic signal emitters (LEDs, laser diodes).
