Transistor

A transistor is a three-terminal semiconductor switch that controls electrical current using a voltage.

Beginner definition

A microscopic electronic switch with no moving parts. By applying a tiny control voltage to one terminal (the gate), a transistor turns the electrical connection between its other two terminals on or off, switching billions of times per second inside microprocessors.

Intermediate definition

A solid-state active device, typically a Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor (MOSFET). Applying a voltage to the insulated gate terminal creates an electric field that modulates the conductivity of a channel between source and drain, forming logic gates and line drivers.

Expert definition

A three-terminal semiconductor component operating across cutoff, triode/linear, and saturation regimes. Modern computing relies on Complementary MOS (CMOS) technology, pairing N-channel and P-channel MOSFETs so that logic gates consume dynamic power almost exclusively during state transitions.
