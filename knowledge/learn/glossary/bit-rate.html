Bit Rate

Bit rate is the number of binary bits transferred per unit of time across a communication link.

Beginner definition

The amount of digital information — measured in bits per second (bps) — traveling through a cable or wireless connection. For example, a gigabit network connection transfers one billion bits of data every second.

Intermediate definition

The net information throughput across a physical or logical channel, calculated as the baud rate (symbols per second) multiplied by the number of bits encoded per symbol, minus protocol framing and coding overhead.

Expert definition

The gross or net transmission rate of binary data, fundamentally bounded in noisy channels by the Shannon-Hartley theorem (C = B * log2(1 + SNR)). Line coding protocols like 8b/10b or 128b/130b introduce deterministic reduction from raw bit rate to payload throughput.
