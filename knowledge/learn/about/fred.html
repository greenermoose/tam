About Fred, the Developer of Tamlinux

I am developing Tamlinux to make my family's computers work better, and sharing my code to give back to the free software and open source communities. I hope Tamlinux helps you do good things with your computers, even the poor ancient ones you've been neglecting.

Give those old motherboards another chance to do some good in this crazy world!

Just Another Rando Developer...

Unleashing the raw power of modern hardware is a passion project taking me back to my youthful dreams of glory. I was an old-school UNIX sysadmin and then a corporate attorney, in the last century before the Y2K bug destroyed civilization.

I might have a slight leg up on the average hacker because I have a degree in computer science from Swarthmore College, where I was a student sysadmin for the department's Sun workstation lab from 1989 to 1991. Then I turned pro in the San Francisco Bay Area as a sysadmin for Sybex. After that, I earned a Juris Doctor from UC Berkeley Law School and was general counsel for TriNet Services, an Internet consulting firm in the Research Triangle (Raleigh–Durham area) of North Carolina.

My first computer was a Timex Sinclair 1000, plugged into a black and white TV in the family room. My parents splurged for the 16 KB cartridge RAM after I got my first program, a slalom game, working (sort of).

My ambition for Tamlinux is to squeeze it into 16,000 bytes. I'm still trying to figure out how Microsoft Windows can consume 8 billion bytes and still be hungry...
