About Fred Horch, the Developer of Tamlinux

Fred is developing Tamlinux to make his family's computers work better, and sharing his code freely to give back to the free software and open source communities. He hopes Tamlinux helps you do good things with your computers, even the poor ancient ones you've been neglecting.

Give those old motherboards another chance to do some good in this crazy world!

What Makes Fred Not Just Another Rando Developer

He was an old-school sysadmin and then a corporate attorney, in the last century before the Y2K bug destroyed civilization.

Fred earned a degree in computer science from Swarthmore College, where he was a student sysadmin for the department's Sun workstation lab from 1989 to 1991. Then he turned pro in the San Francisco Bay Area as a sysadmin for Sybex. After that, he earned a Juris Doctor from UC Berkeley Law School and was general counsel for TriNet Services, an Internet consulting firm in the Research Triangle (Raleigh–Durham area) of North Carolina.

His first computer was a Timex Sinclair 1000, plugged into a black and white TV in the family room. His parents splurged for the 16 KB cartridge RAM after he got his first program, a slalom game, working (sort of).

Fred's ambition for Tamlinux is to pare down its boot loader so it will fit in 16,000 bytes. He is still trying to figure out how Microsoft Windows can consume 8 billion bytes of RAM and still be hungry...
