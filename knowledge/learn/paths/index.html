Choose what you want to learn

This page helps you choose a set of readings based on what you want to do.

A path is a suggested order for reading. You can choose a reading level with the number keys; some links also select a level for you.

What do you want to do?

  • First steps: open a terminal, work with temporary files and return to the Guide.
  • Keep it healthy: identify update owners, read focused logs and prepare a recovery plan.
  • Command-line fluency: build a private executable report and manage its task, from level 1 to 3.
  • Rescue: choose a recovery procedure, check its dependencies and preserve later writes, from level 2 to 3.
  • Run lean: Compare resource evidence and prepare a small, reversible efficiency investigation.
  • Understand the design: Explain the boundaries between the rootstock, Tamlinux packages and a user session.
  • Build and contribute: Build and test a scratch program and prepare a focused source contribution.
  • Work with AI locally: Assess a local model task, run a supported query and check its answer.
  • Settle in from macOS or Windows: Find familiar workstation and documentation tasks in Tamlinux.
  • Make it yours: Practice a reversible customization and identify its owner and recovery.
  • Everyday desktop tasks: screenshots, clipboard, sound, Bluetooth, displays, and idle lock.
  • Desktop basics: tiling windows, workspaces, hotkeys, top bar, and voice commands.
  • Files and applications: where files live, permissions, text search, and launching or installing apps.
  • People and security: user accounts, passwords, keys, privileges, and trusted updates.
  • Cryptography and quantum safety: from zero prior knowledge to ciphers, SHA hashes, public-key math, and post-quantum algorithms.
  • Security protocols and access control: from everyday trust boundaries to Linux credentials, TLS 1.3, SSH internals, and formal verification.
  • Mastering computer cables, hardware communication, and protocols: understand voltage loops, semiconductors, serial and parallel channels, fiber optics, wireless RF, protocols, and bench repair.
  • From atoms to bits: connect electrons and bandgaps to circuits and digital logic.
  • From gates to machine code: follow architecture, the instruction cycle and assembly.
  • Display and GPU: pixels, cables and blank-screen checks.
  • How software runs: kernel, processes, filesystems and init.
  • Session and desktop: console login, compositor and input.
  • Terminal stack: terminfo, ncurses rendering, fonts, themes, and Lua configuration.
  • Systems programming in C11: ISO C11, object files, sanitizers, and language trade-offs.
  • Diagnose from first principles: choose a failing layer with evidence.

Where are you starting?

Browse all dynamic topic paths, or pick a curated starting path below:

  • New to the terminal: First steps or Everyday desktop tasks.
  • Already comfortable running commands: Command-line fluency or Keep it healthy.
  • Ready to reason about internals: Diagnose from first principles or Understand the design.

Press 0 to compare every level on a page, or 1, 2, 3 to choose Novice, Learner or Expert. Your choice holds across navigation. Quizzes have folded answers: decide first, then move to the [+] line and press Right Arrow to show the answer, or Left Arrow to hide it again.

First steps and Keep it healthy are the original pilot paths. Paths do not track progress or grade you.
